    One Page Summary¶

    Boise Idaho YSA Stake — Gathering Plan — One Page Summary¶

    Reaching Young Single Adults and Fostering Belonging and Meaningful Connections Through Activities, Athletics, and Enrichment

    Purpose: To reach young single adults and help them find belonging by connecting them to each other and to Jesus Christ through activities, sports, and learning.

    Stake Priorities¶

    Priority Statement
    1 See and Love as God Does
    2 Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential
    3 Focus on Jesus Christ and Access the Power of His Atonement
    4 Invite All to Come unto Christ and to Make and Keep Sacred Covenants

    How We'll Get There¶

    • Reach: personal monthly check-ins with every YSA, plus 7-day outreach and a 14-day invitation for every new move-in.
    • Belong: activities built so no one stands alone — greeters, hosts, mixed-ward teams, and name tags at every gathering.
    • Teach: monthly enrichment nights and institute encouragement centered on divine identity, purpose, and potential.
    • Invite: a clear next-step pathway — attend → connect → institute → temple → calling → covenants.

    Three Ways We Gather¶

    • Stake Activities (Social): one stake-wide activity monthly, ward-hosted on a rotation, plus quarterly signature events (Fall Kickoff, Christmas Devotional, YSA Conference/Retreat, Summer Celebration, Year-End Testimony Night).
    • Stake Athletics (Physical): weekly leagues and open-play nights in volleyball, basketball, softball, pickleball, and futsal, each opening with prayer and a spiritual thought.
    • Learning & Enrichment (Spiritual): monthly enrichment nights, institute support, workshops, and mentorship built around four threads — Identity, Purpose, Potential, and Christ & Covenants.

    Leadership & Accountability¶

    • Stake Presidency designs the plan; the Stake YSA Council (led by a high councilor and the Stake RS President) carries it out.
    • Ward reps, Gathering Specialists, and Connection Hosts invite, greet, and follow up within 48 hours.
    • Monthly reviews track attendance, outreach contacts, and covenant progress.

    Timeline & Measures of Success¶

    • Months 1–2: assess rosters, form council/committees, survey belonging.
    • Months 2–6: launch the calendar, sports seasons, first learning series and temple night.
    • Months 6–12: review, refine, and plan Year Two.
    • Success: outreach contacts, attendance, temple recommends, reactivations, callings, biannual belonging survey.

    Part 1 — The Overall Plan¶

    1. What We're Trying to Do¶

    • Our vision: We want every young single adult in our stake to feel seen, known, and loved — and to feel drawn toward Jesus Christ and sacred covenants.
    • What we mean by "gathering": reaching and creating a sense of connection and belonging among YSAs with Jesus Christ and each other.
    • What we mean by "reaching": not just getting active members to show up, but also reconnecting with those who've drifted away, welcoming people who've just moved in, and including friends who aren't members.
    • What we mean by "belonging": real relationships of trust that come before conversion — where someone's divine identity is affirmed, and covenants feel like an invitation rather than a demand.
    • The idea behind all of this: every event, league, and class should be a step toward Christ and making and keeping sacred covenants, not just something to do for its own sake.

    2. How Our Four Priorities Guide Everything We Do¶

    Here's how each stake priority shows up in what we plan:

    1. See and Love as God Does → shows up as outreach, checking in on people, and making sure everyone's included.
    2. Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential → shows up as classes and learning nights.
    3. Focus on Jesus Christ and the Atonement → shows up as spiritual depth and healing.
    4. Invite All to Come unto Christ and Make/Keep Sacred Covenants → shows up as helping people take steps toward covenants.

    Simple rule of thumb: before we plan anything, we ask how it connects to one or more of these four priorities, and how it will help a young adult move closer to Christ.

    3. Where We're Starting From¶

    • Getting the numbers straight: comparing ward rosters to who's actually showing up; keeping track of less-active and non-attending YSAs, new move-ins, institute enrollment, and temple recommend holders.
    • Checking in with people: asking YSAs how connected they feel, what makes them feel isolated, what's keeping them away, and when and how they'd like to gather.
    • Taking stock of what we have: callings already filled, the institute building, sports equipment, budget, building availability, and chances to team up with other stakes.
    • Finding where people fall through the cracks: figuring out where someone attends an activity but never takes the next step toward a covenant.

    4. Priority 1 — See and Love as God Does (Reaching Out and Caring for People)¶

    • Getting to know everyone by name: like ministering — making sure every YSA on the roster, active or not, gets a personal check-in every month.
    • Reconnecting with less-active YSAs: our Gathering Specialists team up with ward priesthood and Relief Society leaders to prayerfully figure out whom to reach out to.
    • Welcoming new move-ins: reach out within 7 days, and personally invite them to a ward or stake activity within 14 days.
    • Making sure no one's left standing alone: activities are set up so someone who shows up by themselves can instantly connect — greeters, hosts, mixed-ward teams.
    • Using sports to reach out: open, easy-to-join leagues and pickup games where non-members and less-active YSAs are genuinely welcome.

    5. Priority 2 — Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential (Learning and Growing Together)¶

    • What we teach: who we are as children of God, why we're here, what we can become, getting ready for marriage and family, and being self-reliant.
    • Leaning on the institute: helping it be a primary gathering place where people learn together, encouraging enrollment, and providing rides to and from class.
    • Monthly classes with rotating topics: "Who God Says You Are," "Discovering Your Divine Potential," "Being Self-Reliant," "Healthy Relationships and Getting Ready for Marriage."
    • Practical workshops: career help, budgeting, handling stress, and ways to study scripture — all tied back to the idea that we're meant for something more.
    • Pairing people up: connecting YSAs with more experienced stake members for advice on life and career.

    6. Priority 3 — Focus on Jesus Christ and the Power of His Atonement (Going Deeper Spiritually)¶

    • Keeping Christ at the center: every activity opens and closes with a short reminder of why we're really there, starting with prayer and a brief thought.
    • Making room for healing: a lot of YSAs are carrying anxiety, loneliness, past mistakes, or grief. We want firesides, small groups, and temple nights to be places where the Atonement's healing power can reach them.
    • Regular testimony meetings, "Come, unto Christ" firesides, and sacred music events.
    • Getting to the temple often: frequent stake temple nights, family history and service opportunities, and helping YSAs get and keep a temple recommend.
    • Music that points to the Savior: concerts, hymn sings, and devotional music nights.

    7. Priority 4 — Invite Everyone to Come unto Christ and Keep Covenants (Helping People Take the Next Step)¶

    • Mapping out the next step: attend → connect → institute → temple → a calling → mission or marriage, helping each YSA determine what's next.
    • Helping friendships lead somewhere: building relationships that naturally lead people toward baptism or toward active participation.
    • A steady push on temple recommends: interviews, help getting ready, and group temple trips.
    • Giving people something meaningful to do: callings and responsibilities that help people feel like they belong and stay committed.
    • Preparation: classes and mentoring that support YSAs in all aspects of life preparation, education, career, discipleship, and progressing along the covenant path.

    8. Our Three Main Ways We Gather¶

    A. Stake Activities (Social)

    • How often: one stake-wide activity a month, plus a few bigger events each quarter.
    • Kinds of activities: socials, service projects, dances, firesides, temple nights, outdoor and adventure events.
    • Different wards take turns hosting, so everyone shares ownership and builds friendships across wards.
    • What we're aiming for: well-planned, starts on time, spiritually grounded, and welcoming to newcomers.

    B. Stake Athletics (Physical)

    • Leagues: volleyball, basketball, softball, pickleball, and futsal, with seasons structured to keep people coming back and building real connections.
    • Open-play nights: an easy, low-pressure way for newcomers and less-active YSAs to jump in.
    • It's about more than the game: a short sportsmanship devotional, a prayer, and a spiritual thought at every league gathering.
    • Tournaments and multi-stake events help people meet even more people and grow their circle.

    C. Learning and Enrichment (Spiritual, Intellectual)

    • The Institute is the hub / Gathering Place for this; see Priority 2.
    • Monthly stake enrichment nights, rotating through the four priority themes.
    • Workshops and seminars on practical, relational, and spiritual topics.
    • Book and article discussion groups, centered on Christ, identity, and purpose.
    • Career and life-skills fairs, drawing on the skills already in our stake.

    9. Who's Doing What¶

    • Stake Presidency: designs the plan.
    • Stake YSA Council: puts the plan into action. Led by two Executive Co-Chairs — the YSA High Councilor and the Stake YSA Relief Society President — its 13-seat Executive Council also includes two Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads; two Co-Chairs each for Activities, Athletics, and Education & Enrichment; a Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead; a Communications & Calendar Lead; and a Council Secretary / Metrics Recorder. Twelve Ward Representatives (an EQ and an RS presidency counselor per ward) complete the 25-member Full Council.
    • Stake YSA Committees: Activities, Athletics, and Education & Enrichment, each led by two Co-Chairs who sit on the Executive Council, plus the Married Adults Committee.
    • Stake Gathering Specialists: assisting with identifying, reaching out, and inviting, coordinated through the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads.
    • Clear roles for every event: who's planning it, who's paying for it, who's inviting people, and who's following up.
    • A monthly check-in: looking at attendance, outreach contacts made, and progress toward covenants (temple recommends, institute enrollment, people becoming active again).

    10. How We'll Know It's Working¶

    • Early signs we're on track: outreach contacts made, visits with less-active members, welcomes to new move-ins, activity and sacrament meeting attendance, institute enrollment, and league participation.
    • Signs of longer-term success: temple recommend holders, baptisms and reactivations, callings accepted, priesthood ordinations, temple/family history participation, marriages in the stake.
    • A quick survey twice a year asking how connected people feel.
    • A check-in every few months against the four priorities, adjusting the calendar and our efforts as needed.

    11. Getting the Word Out¶

    • One shared calendar everyone can see, kept up digitally across wards.
    • A steady rhythm: a personal invite, then a text reminder, then a group announcement.
    • A mix of channels: ward announcements, group texts, simple graphics, and personal contact.
    • Leaders sharing their own experiences and testimonies to keep the momentum going.

    12. Budget and Resources¶

    • Putting money first toward the efforts that most clearly help people move closer to Christ.
    • Sharing resources across wards, and teaming up with other stakes for sports and big events.
    • Keeping costs reasonable so every YSA can take part, regardless of what they can afford.

    13. A Suggested Timeline¶

    • Months 1–2: get a clear picture of where we stand, form the committee, clean up the rosters, and survey how people are feeling.
    • Months 2–3: build the calendar, launch "Know Them by Name," and kick off the sports seasons.
    • Months 3–6: run our first learning series, hold our first stake temple night, and host a big quarterly event.
    • Months 6–12: look at how things are going, tweak what needs tweaking, grow what's working, and start planning year two.

    14. Keeping This Spiritually Grounded¶

    • Leaders fasting and praying for the YSAs of the stake.
    • Regular council meetings, seeking guidance for specific individuals.
    • Leaders sharing their own testimonies to set the tone.
    • Following Christ's example: everything we do seeks to mirror how the Savior ministered; one person at a time, with love, inviting people toward deeper covenants.

    Part 2 — Stake Activities: How We'll Run Them¶

    1. Why We Do Activities¶

    Stake activities are the most visible way we try to reach young single adults and help them feel a sense of belonging. Every activity is planned with purpose, not just for entertainment's sake. A few things guide how we plan:

    • Purposeful: we don't schedule anything without a clear answer to "which of the four priorities does this serve, and how does it help someone move a step closer to Christ?"
    • Belonging matters more than headcount: we measure success by whether real connections happened, not by how many people showed up.
    • Inclusive by default: every activity is planned imagining someone showing up alone, new to the area, less active, or there for the first time, and making sure that person is seen, welcomed, and connected.
    • A step, not the destination: every activity should point toward the next step, whether that is sacrament meeting, institute, temple, ministering, or moving closer to covenants.

    2. Types of Activities¶

    A good mix of activities throughout the year keeps things fresh, reaches different kinds of people, and helps everyone feel like they belong no matter the season. We build around four types:

    A. Social Activities (Building Friendships)

    • What it's for: building friendships and cross-ward relationships in a relaxed setting.
    • Examples: dances, game nights, ice cream socials, outdoor barbecues, talent shows, holiday parties, cultural nights.
    • How often: a stake social every month, plus smaller ward-level gatherings.
    • What we make sure happens: a way for first-timers to jump in, greeters, name tags, mingling activities, and a host whose job is making sure no one's standing alone.

    B. Service Activities (Serving Others)

    • What it's for: living out Priorities 1 and 4 by ministering to our community and stake.
    • Examples: community service projects, food bank service, neighborhood cleanups, temple and family history service, helping elderly members move or maintain their homes, JustServe projects.
    • How often: a stake-wide service event each quarter, plus ongoing ward service opportunities.
    • What we make sure happens: service feels like worship, beginning with prayer and a brief reflection on serving as the Savior did, and ending with a moment to reflect.

    C. Adventure and Outdoor Activities (Something for Everyone)

    • What it's for: reaching YSAs who might not be drawn to dances or firesides, and building friendships through shared experiences.
    • Examples: hikes, river trips, lake days, ski and snowboard outings, camping weekends, cycling rides, escape rooms and strategy nights, group outings to local events.
    • How often: monthly, depending on the season.
    • What we make sure happens: groups stay small enough for real connection, with a short prayer and thought included, plus a clear carpool and inclusion plan.

    D. Signature Stake-Wide Events (The Year's Big Moments)

    • What it's for: giving the year a shared identity, something to look forward to, and a sense of belonging.
    • Fall Kickoff Social — welcoming new students and move-ins to the stake.
    • Christmas Devotional and Concert — centered on the Savior.
    • YSA Conference / Multi-day Retreat — the year's flagship event.
    • Easter-season "Come unto Christ" fireside.
    • Spring Service Day.
    • Summer Stake Celebration / Outdoor Festival.
    • Year-End Testimony Night.
    • What we make sure happens: these are done well, have a clear spiritual purpose, draw people from every ward, and make a real effort to include the less-active and the new.

    3. Keeping Christ at the Center of Every Activity¶

    • Opening and closing: every activity begins with prayer and a short word about why we're there, tied to one of the four priorities, and closes with gratitude.
    • A "why we gather" moment: the activity leader, a YSA, shares a brief thought connecting the activity back to the Savior and to belonging.
    • Different people take turns giving that thought a chance, allowing YSAs to build their own confidence to teach and testify.
    • Every activity points to what's next: attendees are invited to the next covenant-step event.
    • We're thoughtful about the Sabbath and treat our buildings with the respect they deserve.

    4. Making Sure No One Gets Left Out¶

    What really sets this program apart is its intentional outreach to, inclusion of, and retention of YSAs who aren't currently showing up.

    Before the Activity

    • Personal invitations, not just announcements: ward YSA coordinators identify specific people and invite them personally.
    • "Bring a friend": we encourage and help members bring along someone who otherwise wouldn't come.
    • A few reminders: announcement → group text → personal text → a day-of nudge.
    • Rides for everyone: carpools are arranged so no one misses out for lack of a ride.

    During the Activity

    • Connection Hosts: called greeters whose whole job is finding whoever's standing alone, learning their name, and bringing them in.
    • Games and small groups that get people talking — we don't leave connection to chance.
    • Mixing up wards so friendships form beyond your own ward.
    • Name tags at every stake activity, so no one has to guess.

    After the Activity

    • A follow-up within 48 hours: connection hosts and ward coordinators reach out to newcomers and anyone who didn't come.
    • An invite to what's next: every follow-up includes a personal invite to the next activity, institute, or a temple night.
    • Keeping track: attendance is noted, and a pattern of missing activities leads to a personal check-in (Priority 1).

    5. Who's Running This¶

    • Stake YSA Council: co-led by a high councilor and the Stake Relief Society President, with stake representatives and leads for each category (outreach, activities, athletics, education & enrichment, gathering place/institute, and communications).
    • Ward representatives: those who actually invite people, track who came, and follow up for their ward (counselor in the ward Elders Quorum presidency and counselor in the ward Relief Society presidency).
    • Connection Hosts: called and trained greeters for every activity.
    • Activity Leads: Each activity is led by one person who is responsible for planning, maintaining the spiritual tone, ensuring inclusivity, handling logistics, and follow-up.
    • Clear roles, set ahead of time: for every activity, everyone knows who's planning, who's paying, who's inviting, who's greeting, and who's following up.

    6. How We Plan Well¶

    • At least 6 weeks of lead time for stake activities, longer for the bigger signature events.
    • A simple checklist: state the purpose → identify the priority it serves → plan for inclusion → set the spiritual focus → sort out logistics → plan the invitations → plan the follow-up → check how it went.
    • What we're aiming for: activities that start on time, are well-organized, spiritually grounded, welcoming to newcomers, and within budget.
    • A quick debrief afterward: what worked, what didn't, who came, who was new, who was less active, and what follow-up is needed.

    7. Budget and Resources¶

    • Money goes first to the activities with the clearest impact on belonging and covenants.
    • Kept affordable: activities are priced so everyone can take part, with help available so no YSA is left out.
    • Shared resources: supplies, facilities, and know-how shared across wards.
    • Teaming up with neighboring stakes for joint events, growing the social network while sharing costs.

    8. How We'll Know It's Working¶

    • Early signs: attendance trends; how many newcomers, less-active, and non-member attendees show up; follow-up contacts made within 48 hours.
    • Connection signs: cross-ward friendships, how connected people say they feel, and whether newcomers come back.
    • Covenant progress: movement from attending an activity → sacrament meeting → institute → temple night → temple recommend → a calling → baptism → reactivation → temple covenants.
    • A quick survey twice a year.
    • A check-in every few months against the four priorities, adjusting the calendar and approach as needed.

    Part 3 — Athletics: How We'll Run It¶

    1. Why We Do Sports¶

    Sports in the Boise Idaho YSA Stake aren't just recreation for its own sake. They're an easy, low-pressure way for relationships to form across ward lines, where less-active members and non-members feel welcome, and where every gathering is anchored in the Savior. A few things guide how we run it:

    • Connection matters more than winning.
    • Regular games build the steady contact that real friendship and shepherding need, and weekly leagues create that.
    • Every gathering opens with prayer and a short spiritual thought, and closes with gratitude.

    2. Levels of Play¶

    A. Open Play Nights (The Easiest Way In)

    • What it's for: the easiest possible first step for a newcomer, a less-active member, or someone just checking things out.
    • Format: drop-in, no roster, no commitment, no skill required. Teams are picked on the spot each week.
    • How often: weekly or every other week, rotating sports (basketball, volleyball, pickleball, futsal).
    • The hook: "bring a friend" nights, snacks, music. It's more about the vibe than the score.
    • Shepherding: each open night has a connection host who greets newcomers, learns their names, and follows up with a personal invite to what's next.

    B. Stake Leagues (Building Real Friendships)

    • What it's for: structured seasons that bring YSAs back week after week and build deep friendships.
    • Fall: volleyball and flag football.
    • Winter: basketball (our flagship league) and futsal.
    • Spring: softball and pickleball.
    • Summer: coed softball, ultimate frisbee, and tournament season.
    • How teams work: teams are deliberately mixed across wards to break up cliques. Each team has a captain responsible not only for the game but also for ensuring every teammate is known, contacted, and cared for.
    • Season length: 6–8 weeks, wrapping up with a stake tournament and social.

    C. Inter-Stake and Regional Events (Widening the Circle)

    • What it's for: widening the social circle beyond our own stake, helping YSAs meet a broader group of faithful peers.
    • Examples: an inter-stake basketball tournament, a regional softball invitational, combined outdoor adventures, YSA sports festivals.
    • Worth noting: these are also great moments for friendships and lasting relationships to form.

    3. Keeping Christ in Every Game¶

    • Pre-game huddle: a short prayer and a 60–90 second spiritual thought before each game, rotating among players and leaders, drawn from the four stake priorities.
    • A simple sportsmanship standard: respect, inclusion, and Christlike conduct, no ejections, conflicts handled with grace.
    • A "More Than a Game" devotional once a season; a short fireside during league play centered on Jesus Christ, identity, and purpose.
    • A service project each season, tying sports back to discipleship.

    4. Who's Running It¶

    • Stake Athletics Committee Co-Chairs: a high councilor and a called specialist who oversee the entire program, calendars, facilities, and spiritual tone.
    • Sport Commissioners: one per sport, handling scheduling, rules, equipment, and officiating.
    • Team Captains: called and trained to look after teammates, track attendance, reach out to absentees, and extend personal invitations.
    • Connection Hosts: greeters for open-play and tournament nights.
    • Ward Athletics Coordinators: bring the Athletics Committee's work to that ward: inviting people, tracking attendance at open play nights and league events, and following up with newcomers and those who missed out within 48 hours.
    • A facility and equipment steward: manages building access, gear, and safety.

    5. Using Sports to Reconnect With People¶

    • Targeted invitations: ward YSA coordinators identify less-active YSAs and personally invite them to open-play nights, the easiest possible entry point.
    • "Adopt a player": active YSAs are paired with less-active or new members on the same team, building a natural friendship.
    • Non-members are genuinely welcome: friends, coworkers, and investigators are often introduced to the Church and its people through a sports night.
    • Keeping track: attendance is recorded each week, and absentees are contacted within 48 hours — sports become an active way to shepherd people, not just an item on the calendar.

    6. Logistics and Safety¶

    • Facilities: coordinated use of stake and ward buildings, respecting priesthood-occupied spaces and the Sabbath when scheduling.
    • Safety: first aid kits, an injury plan, concussion awareness, and liability forms where needed.
    • Cost: kept minimal, with help available so no YSA is left out for financial reasons.
    • Equipment: managed centrally, with commissioners keeping track of inventory.

    7. How We'll Know It's Working¶

    • Weekly participation and whether it's trending up.
    • How many less-active YSAs are showing up.
    • How many non-members are showing up.
    • Whether people move from open-play → league → institute → temple night.
    • Friendships forming across wards (from surveys).

    Part 4 — Learning and Enrichment: What We'll Teach¶

    1. Why We Teach This¶

    Our learning and enrichment efforts aim to teach divine identity, purpose, and potential (Priority 2), deepen faith in Jesus Christ and His Atonement (Priority 3), and help YSAs move toward sacred covenants (Priority 4). We strive to speak to the whole person: spiritual, intellectual, relational, temporal, and emotional, because divine potential touches every part of life. Everything is built around four topics that mirror our stake priorities.

    2. Four Big Topics We'll Keep Coming Back To¶

    Thread 1 — Divine Identity: "Who God Says I Am"

    • What it's about: anchoring YSAs in their identity as children of heavenly parents, foreordained with purpose.
    • Why it matters: it counters the identity confusion, comparison, and anxiety so common among young adults.
    • Child of Heavenly Parents: What That Really Means
    • Foreordination and Your Divine Potential
    • Overcoming Comparison Through Divine Identity
    • Worth vs. Worthiness: Understanding Grace
    • Your Divine Nature and the Body
    • Identity in Christ When the World Says Otherwise

    Thread 2 — Divine Purpose: "Why I Am Here"

    • What it's about: mortal purpose, the plan of salvation lived out day to day, and each person's unique contribution.
    • The Plan of Happiness and Your Daily Life
    • Finding Purpose in Your Season of Life
    • Calling, Career, and Consecration
    • Service as the Path to Joy
    • What God Is Teaching You Right Now
    • Living on the Covenant Path: The Map of Mortality

    Thread 3 — Divine Potential: "Who I Am Becoming"

    • What it's about: growth, eternal progression, and preparation for future roles.
    • Becoming Like the Savior: The Process of Change
    • Marriage and Eternal Family Preparation
    • Healthy Relationships, Boundaries, and Discernment
    • Emotional Resilience and the Atonement
    • Mental Health, Faith, and Reaching for Help
    • Temporal Self-Reliance: Work, Money, and Providence
    • Developing Spiritual Gifts and Talents

    Thread 4 — Christ and Covenants: "How I Come unto Him"

    • What it's about: deepening our relationship with Jesus Christ and progressing in sacred covenants.
    • Jesus Christ and the Power of His Atonement (a recurring anchor lesson)
    • Repentance as a Joyful Return, Not a Burden
    • The Sacrament: Weekly Covenant Renewal
    • Temple Covenants and Daily Discipleship
    • Receiving Personal Revelation
    • Enduring Through Christ When Life Is Hard
    • Sharing the Gospel Naturally: Inviting Others to Christ

    3. Types of Learning Activities¶

    A. Spiritual and Worship Activities (Christ-Focused)

    • What it's for: deepening faith in Jesus Christ (Priority 3) and inviting covenant progression (Priority 4).
    • Examples: stake testimony meetings, "Come, unto Christ" firesides, devotional concerts and sacred music events, hymn sings, temple nights, scripture studies, Fast Sunday YSA gatherings.
    • How often: a fireside or devotional each month, plus a stake temple night each quarter.
    • What we make sure happens: every spiritual activity centers explicitly on the Savior, makes room for personal testimony, and closes with an invitation to take a next step.

    B. Cultural and Enrichment Activities (Identity and Potential-Focused)

    • What it's for: reinforcing divine identity, purpose, and potential (Priority 2) through shared cultural and learning experiences.
    • Examples: cultural celebrations, arts nights, film and discussion evenings, outdoor concerts, museum and outing trips, talent showcases, language and heritage events.
    • How often: quarterly.
    • What we make sure happens: each cultural event ties back to one of our learning topics, with a brief teaching moment on identity, purpose, or potential.

    4. How We'll Teach It¶

    A. Monthly Stake Enrichment Night

    • When: one evening a month, rotating through the four topics.
    • Format: a brief worship moment (hymn, prayer) → a 35–40-minute lesson → small-group discussion → a personal commitment.
    • Small groups: cross-ward, 6–10 people, with a trained facilitator; this is where real belonging and connection actually happen.
    • A commitment to close on: each night ends with a simple, specific invitation, such as reading scripture, visiting the temple, performing an act of service, or making a ministering contact.

    B. Institute Is at the Heart of This

    • Institute is the backbone of enrichment and a Gathering Place, the most effective regular gathering for real doctrinal depth.
    • Our approach: actively encourage enrollment, remove barriers like rides and scheduling, and line up institute topics with our stake's learning topics.
    • The goal: every active YSA enrolled in at least one institute course, and every less-active YSA personally invited.

    C. Workshops and Seminars

    • Career development and job-search skills.
    • Financial literacy and budgeting.
    • Mental and emotional wellness.
    • Scripture study and prayer methods.
    • Marriage preparation and healthy dating.
    • Parenting foundations.
    • Taught by our own stake members — a way to connect YSAs to mature, faithful mentors.

    D. Discussion Groups and Book/Article Studies

    • Small, recurring groups (4–8 YSAs) studying a gospel-centered book, general conference talks, or articles.
    • Examples: studying "Come, Follow Me," recent conference addresses on identity and the Savior, or other approved Church resources.
    • What it offers: the kind of depth and closeness a big event can't.

    E. Mentorship Pairing

    • YSAs paired with seasoned stake members (couples and individuals) for guidance on life, career, and faith.
    • Meets monthly, structured around the four topics.
    • Especially valuable for YSAs without nearby family.

    5. How the Year Flows¶

    • September–November (Thread 2 — Purpose): aligning with the back-to-school season and stake conference to help YSAs set their spiritual direction for the year.
    • December (Thread 4 — Christ): a Christmas-season focus on the Savior and getting ready for the temple and ward life.
    • January–February (Thread 1 — Identity): a new-year focus on who we are in God's eyes, to help push back against winter discouragement.
    • March–April (Thread 4 — Covenants): leading into Easter and general conference, with a focus on Christ, the Atonement, and the temple.
    • May–July (Thread 3 — Potential): getting YSAs ready for summer growth, relationships, careers, service, and the temple.
    • August: a capstone retreat or testimony night that brings all four topics together.

    6. How We Teach¶

    • Doctrine first: all teaching is grounded in scripture and the words of living prophets; no speculation presented as doctrine.
    • Christ-centered: every lesson connects back to Jesus Christ and His Atonement.
    • Relevant: teaching answers real questions YSAs ask and never dismisses doubt, anxiety, or struggle.
    • More conversation, less lecture: discussion, questions, and real-life application.
    • A safe space: leaders are trained to listen with love (Priority 1), especially on sensitive topics like mental health, worthiness struggles, and relationships.
    • Trained facilitators: small-group leaders are briefed on tone, confidentiality, and where to point someone with a serious need.

    7. How This Ties Back to Our Priorities¶

    • Priority 1 (See and Love): enrichment nights are set up so no one sits alone, with facilitators trained to notice and bring in the quiet or the new.
    • Priority 2 (Divine Identity, Purpose, Potential): the actual content of Threads 1–3.
    • Priority 3 (Christ and the Atonement): Thread 4, and the spiritual tone of every session.
    • Priority 4 (Invite to Christ and Covenants): every enrichment night ends with an invitation to take a next step, institute, temple, ministering, or baptism/reactivation.

    8. How We'll Know It's Working¶

    • Monthly attendance and whether it's trending up.
    • Institute enrollment numbers.
    • How many small-group facilitators we've trained.
    • A survey on how YSAs feel about their own understanding of divine identity, purpose, and potential.
    • Signs of covenant progress: temple recommends, how often people say they're studying scripture, reactivations, and callings accepted.

    How We Built This Calendar¶

    • Reinforcing each other: each month's activity, sports season, learning topic, and institute focus are intentionally lined up to support one another.
    • No dead months: steady, balanced engagement across all four channels, all year round.
    • Mindful of the seasons: the calendar takes school terms, holidays, and seasonal energy into account.
    • A path through covenants: the topics move from Identity → Purpose → Christ/Covenants → Potential, guiding YSAs along the covenant path across the year.
    • Signature events anchor the year: Fall Kickoff, Christmas Devotional, Conference/Retreat, and Year-End Testimony Night give the year its structure and momentum.
