    Stake Education & Enrichment Committee — Roles¶

    Boise Idaho YSA Stake — Education & Enrichment Committee

    Education & Enrichment Committee Co-Chair (Sister)¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Co-Chair

    Section 4.1 of the Charter

    • Reports To: YSA Council Co-Chairs (jointly with the Priesthood Co-Chair)
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Stake YSA Council Co-Chairs with stake presidency input; called, interviewed, and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered with the Priesthood Co-Chair.

    Purpose

    The Sister Co-Chair co-chairs the Education & Enrichment Committee and shares full accountability for delivering Priority 2 ("Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential") through the Monthly Stake Enrichment Night, workshops, discussion groups, and mentorship pairings (Gathering Plan, Part 4). This role carries particular responsibility for sister-facing recruiting and for ensuring that every small group is a place where someone can belong.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-leads the Education & Enrichment Committee; co-chairs every Committee Core meeting with the Priesthood Executive Co-Chair.
    • Runs the Monthly Stake Enrichment Night and rotates through the four curriculum threads over the year, together with the Priesthood Co-Chair (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4A).
    • Champions sister-specific facilitator and mentor recruiting, and makes sure small groups are set up so no one sits alone.
    • Represents the Committee at the monthly YSA Council meeting alongside the Priesthood Co-Chair.
    • Makes sure every enrichment night ends with a specific invitation to take a covenant step.
    • Uses the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's needs summary when setting each enrichment night's emphasis and designing small groups.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep a running roster of trained sister facilitators and mentors, noting who is assigned each curriculum cycle.
    • Build a shared cross-ward seating chart with the Priesthood Co-Chair so newcomers are never grouped alone.
    • Write a short "greet and connect" script for hosts to use in the first five minutes of every enrichment night.
    • Track which sisters have been personally invited to serve each thread rotation, not just who volunteered.
    • Debrief after every enrichment night on whether the closing invitation was given and how it landed.

    Education & Enrichment Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood)¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Co-Chair

    Section 4.2 of the Charter

    • Reports To: YSA Council Co-Chairs (jointly with the Sister Co-Chair)
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Stake YSA Council Co-Chairs with stake presidency input; called, interviewed, and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered with the Sister Co-Chair.

    Purpose

    The Priesthood Co-Chair co-chairs the Committee and maintains its formal reporting relationship with the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs. This role keeps the Workshop & Seminar Series aligned with the Institute and upholds the doctrine-first, Christ-centered teaching standard across every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 6).

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-leads the Education & Enrichment Committee; carries the Committee's reporting relationship with the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs.
    • Coordinates the Workshop/Seminar series and keeps it aligned with the Institute, drawing on stake members' expertise.
    • Reviews Committee coordinator changes and recommends them to the YSA Council Co-Chairs.
    • Makes sure Christ-centered framing and doctrine-first teaching standards are upheld across every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group.
    • Delivers the monthly enrichment stewardship report to the YSA Council Co-Chairs, prepared by the Secretary.
    • Serves as the assigned high councilor for the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator; reviews member-survey content and needs-based topics for Christ-centered framing and carries approvals to the Executive Co-Chairs.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Use a short "doctrine-first checklist" with every presenter or facilitator before they teach for the first time.
    • Sit in on one enrichment night, workshop, or discussion group each quarter to sample teaching quality firsthand.
    • Keep a one-page coordinator roster with term dates, flagged 60 days ahead of any turnover.
    • Use the Section 6.2 measurement framework as a fixed template so the monthly stewardship report is quick to assemble.
    • Compare Institute course topics against the current curriculum thread each term to catch alignment gaps early.

    Monthly Enrichment Night Producer¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.3 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    The Producer owns the delivery of the Monthly Stake Enrichment Night — the flagship format for Priority 2, rotating through the four curriculum threads throughout the year (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4A). Success means a well-run night every month, with a clear next step offered at the end.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Books the teaching slot and speaker for each month's enrichment night, matching the current curriculum thread.
    • Runs the standard format: brief worship (hymn, prayer), 35–40 minutes of teaching, small-group discussion, and a personal commitment invitation.
    • Coordinates room setup and logistics with the building scheduler and the Communications & Calendar Lead.
    • Makes sure the night is on the master calendar at least 60 days ahead of time.
    • Runs a quick post-event debrief with the Small-Group Facilitator Coordinator and reports attendance to the Secretary.
    • Draws on the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's topic recommendations and resource list when booking speakers, and shares commitment-invitation patterns that point to new needs.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Build a 60-day production calendar: speaker booked → room reserved → topic confirmed → promotion sent → facilitators assigned.
    • Keep a rotating "speaker bench" of 8–10 approved teachers per curriculum thread so slots are never scrambled last minute.
    • Use a repeatable run-of-show template (opening hymn/prayer, teaching, small groups, commitment invite, closing) for every night.
    • Log which commitment invitation was given each month and roughly how many responded, for the debrief.
    • Confirm next month's thread and speaker before leaving each debrief meeting.

    Small-Group Facilitator Coordinator¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.4 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    This Coordinator builds and trains the Facilitator & Mentor Network members who lead cross-ward small-group discussions at every enrichment night — the setting where real belonging happens (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4A). Training covers the six teaching standards: doctrine first, Christ-centered, interactive, safe space, confidentiality, and referral pathways.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Recruits and trains 6–10-person cross-ward small-group facilitators for every enrichment night.
    • Briefs facilitators on the teaching standards in Section 6 of Part 4 of the Gathering Plan: doctrine first, Christ-centered, interactive, safe space, confidentiality, and referral pathways.
    • Assigns facilitators to groups each cycle, and makes sure no facilitator stays with the same group long enough to form a clique.
    • Runs a short debrief after each enrichment night and passes facilitator counts and notes to the Secretary.
    • Recruits potential facilitators from the stake resource list, and passes recurring debrief themes (without names) to the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Maintain a facilitator roster with training-completion dates and current assignment.
    • Hand every new facilitator a one-page card covering the six teaching standards at training.
    • Rotate facilitator-to-group assignments each cycle with a simple numbered draw so no one defaults to the same group.
    • Build a "when to refer" card pointing facilitators to the bishop for worthiness, safety, or mental-health concerns.
    • Send a two-question post-night survey to facilitators (what went well / who needs follow-up) and log it for the Secretary.

    Workshop & Seminar Series Coordinator¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.5 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    This Coordinator develops the quarterly workshop calendar covering career, financial literacy, wellness, scripture study, marriage prep, and parenting — practical topics aligned with divine identity, purpose, and potential (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4C). The intended audience for every session is young single adults ages 18–35.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Builds the quarterly workshop calendar: career development, financial literacy, mental and emotional wellness, scripture study and prayer methods, marriage prep and healthy dating, and parenting basics.
    • Recruits presenters from among stake members with relevant expertise, starting with the stake resource list, including married-couple advisors where appropriate.
    • Coordinates logistics and makes sure every workshop is framed around divine identity, purpose, or potential rather than feeling like a stand-alone class.
    • Reports attendance and presenter-recruiting status to the Secretary each month.
    • Builds the quarterly calendar from the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's needs summary, meets monthly in the Needs & Resources Huddle, and takes on pilot topics that prove useful.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep a presenter pipeline sheet: topic, presenter, confirmed date, and a backup presenter.
    • Ask every presenter to open with a one-minute tie-back to divine identity, purpose, or potential before the content begins.
    • Publish the quarterly calendar one quarter ahead so presenters get at least 8 weeks of lead time.
    • Invite married-couple advisors specifically for marriage-prep, parenting, and financial sessions, consistent with their advisory role.
    • Log a short presenter-quality note after each workshop so strong presenters get repeat invitations.

    Discussion Group Coordinator¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.6 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    This Coordinator runs small, recurring discussion groups (4–8 people) centered on Come, Follow Me, general conference talks, and other approved Church resources — a format that offers depth a large event can't (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4D).

    Key Responsibilities

    • Forms small, recurring 4–8-person discussion groups studying Come, Follow Me, recent general conference talks, or approved Church resources.
    • Recruits and briefs group hosts on confidentiality and the fundamentals of leading a discussion.
    • Tracks group health, attendance, retention, and whether groups are building real depth and connection, not just showing up.
    • Reports the number of active groups and the participation trend to the Secretary.
    • Uses member-interest survey results from the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator to choose study topics and invite new participants and hosts.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep a directory of active groups with host name, meeting night, and location.
    • Give every new host a one-page "hosting basics" card: open with prayer, ask open questions, watch for the quiet person, close with a takeaway.
    • Check in with each host quarterly on group health, not just attendance numbers.
    • Refresh a group that has been static with the same 4–5 people for more than two cycles, to keep belonging fresh.
    • Keep a standing list of approved discussion resources so hosts never scramble for material.

    Mentorship Pairing Coordinator¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.7 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    This Coordinator pairs young single adults with mature stake members for life, career, and spiritual guidance — especially valuable for YSAs without nearby family (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4E). Because mentoring conversations are personal, confidentiality and clear referral pathways matter more here than in any other seat.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Recruits mature stake members, including couples and individuals, such as married couple advisors, to serve as mentors for life, career, and spiritual guidance.
    • Pairs YSAs with mentors, paying particular attention to single YSAs who don't have family nearby.
    • Sets up a monthly mentor-meeting rhythm built around the four curriculum threads.
    • Tracks how the pairings are going and reports active-pairing counts and meeting completion to the Secretary.
    • Draws on professional skills and life experience recorded on the stake resource list to build the mentor pool, and receives career and life-transition needs flagged by the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Use a simple mentor-application intake (background, availability, areas of experience) before pairing anyone.
    • Keep a pairing tracker: mentor, mentee, pairing date, last meeting date, and next scheduled meeting.
    • Flag any pairing with no contact in 45+ days for a personal check-in.
    • Give every mentor a one-page confidentiality reminder: keep mentoring conversations private, and refer safety, worthiness, or mental-health concerns to the Coordinator or a bishop.
    • Prioritize new pairings for YSAs without nearby family within their first 30 days in the pairing pool.

    Institute & Covenant Progression Liaison¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.8 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (works with the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead)
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    This Liaison makes Institute the true hub of the Committee's work, boosting enrollment and clearing barriers like rides and scheduling for young single adults ages 18–35 (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 4B). Institute course topics are lined up with the stake's four curriculum threads wherever possible.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Partners with institute faculty and the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead to boost enrollment and clear away barriers like rides and scheduling.
    • Lines up institute course topics with the stake's four curriculum threads wherever possible.
    • Sends institute enrollment numbers relevant to Education & Enrichment programming to the Secretary each month.
    • Shares institute course offerings with the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator and uses needs patterns to recommend courses that complement, rather than duplicate, stake workshops and firesides.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Post a simple ride-share sign-up sheet each Institute term for anyone without transportation.
    • Meet with Institute faculty each term to compare the course catalog against the current curriculum thread.
    • Track enrollment by ward so under-enrolled wards get a targeted, personal-invite push.
    • Personally invite every newly identified less-active or new-move-in YSA to at least one Institute class within their first 30 days.
    • Share a short monthly enrollment snapshot (numbers plus trend) with the Secretary before the Committee Core meeting.

    Reference

    • General Handbook 14, "Single Members" — https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/14-single-members?lang=eng

    Learning & Enrichment Coordinator¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.9 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (a stake calling under the direction of the stake presidency and the assigned high councilor, the Priesthood Co-Chair)
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    This Coordinator is the Committee's needs-and-resources hub. The role is not just event planning — it is a way of ministering: gathering members' interests, talents, and needs and connecting them with people and resources that can bless them, so every offering helps members feel seen, strengthens faith, and supports covenant keeping.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Identifies member interests, talents, skills, and unmet needs through a member survey, interviews, and input from bishoprics, Relief Society, Elders Quorum, and ward YSA leaders.
    • Keeps the stake-wide resource list of willing teachers, presenters, and subject-matter experts, and shares it with every Coordinator.
    • Recommends 3 to 5 topics each planning cycle that fit stake priorities and the current curriculum thread, each paired with a Christ-centered framing.
    • Plans stake firesides and needs-driven pilot classes, and hands those that prove useful to the Workshop & Seminar Series Coordinator so the two seats never compete.
    • Reports needs patterns, attendance, feedback, and recommended future topics to the Secretary and the Co-Chairs.

    Coordination with the Committee

    • Co-Chairs: set scope, survey approval, and reporting expectations at the start of the term; deliver a needs-and-topics summary each planning cycle.
    • Workshop & Seminar Series Coordinator: meet monthly in the Needs & Resources Huddle to align calendars, avoid duplicate topics, and hand off proven pilots and presenters.
    • Enrichment Night Producer and Discussion Group Coordinator: supply needs-based topics, speakers, and study interests from the survey and resource list.
    • Small-Group Facilitator and Mentorship Pairing Coordinators: share members with teaching, facilitation, or professional skills; receive themes and needs back without names.
    • Institute Liaison, Secretary, and Advisor Couples: share needs patterns for course alignment, provide monthly metrics for the dashboard, and invite advisor skills for the resource list.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Ask every ward and stake leader, "What are the most common needs or gaps you see among our YSA members?" and combine the answers with survey results.
    • Build the resource list as a simple spreadsheet with columns for topic, audience, suggested presenter, cost, location, and spiritual purpose — reviewed each planning cycle.
    • Plan one pilot first, such as a "Faith and Future" workshop with a short devotional, a practical class, and a testimony-bearing close; promote it well and gather feedback.
    • Track a few metrics each cycle: members surveyed, top needs, resources added, events held, and attendance.

    Committee Secretary / Metrics Recorder¶

    Committee Core — Tier 1 · Coordinator

    Section 4.10 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs
    • Selection & Term: Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the YSA Co-Chairs; extended as a calling or formal assignment, interviewed and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered.

    Purpose

    The Secretary sits at the hub of the reporting chain, consolidating every Coordinator's numbers into a single dashboard and a monthly stewardship report for the Co-Chairs (Gathering Plan, Part 4, Section 8; charter Section 6.1–6.2). Meeting notes, rosters, and attendance data are Church records and are handled accordingly.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Takes minutes and tracks action items for every Committee Core meeting.
    • Pulls together the monthly attendance, facilitator, workshop, discussion-group, mentorship, and needs-gathering dashboard from data each Coordinator provides.
    • Drafts the monthly enrichment stewardship report for the Co-Chairs to deliver to the Executive Co-Chairs.
    • Keeps track of the Committee roster, term dates, and any upcoming turnover or succession needs.
    • Records the Learning & Enrichment topic pipeline and related action items in the minutes, and adds needs-gathering metrics to the dashboard.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Build one shared dashboard (spreadsheet or shared document) with a tab per Coordinator so data rolls up automatically.
    • Send each Coordinator a same-day-of-month reminder to submit their numbers before the report is due.
    • Store minutes and the roster/term-date tracker in an access-limited shared folder, so records stay available only to those with a need to know.
    • Flag any Coordinator whose term ends within 90 days at the top of the roster so succession planning starts early.
    • Use the Attendance & Participation / Facilitator & Mentor Health / Covenant Progression / Member Needs & Resources / Look-Ahead / Risks & Needs framework as a fixed checklist every month so nothing gets skipped.

    Married Couple Advisors¶

    Standing Advisor Couples — non-voting · 2–3 couples

    Section 4.11 of the Charter

    • Reports To: Advisory relationship to the Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (not a supervisory reporting line)
    • Selection & Term: Called by the stake presidency, following the orientation in A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake; interviewed and set apart. Concurrent with assignment; no fixed rotation.

    Purpose

    Married couples serve this Committee as advisors, not leaders. They sit with the Co-Chairs to plan, join the Mentorship Pairing pool, and may be invited to speak on marriage, parenting, or career and financial topics — while young single adults plan, run, and teach every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Sit with the Committee Core in planning meetings as counsel, not as chairs; share experience when asked or when the Spirit prompts, never as a directive to follow.
    • Serve in the Mentorship Pairing pool as mentors, and may be invited to speak in the Workshop & Seminar Series on marriage, parenting, or career and financial topics; share their skills for the stake resource list and suggest topics when the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator asks.
    • Attend enrichment nights, workshops, and discussion groups as guests and friends, there to be present, not to run the program; young adult facilitators greet, lead, and teach.
    • Coach and encourage Coordinators privately; if something needs correcting, do it specifically, kindly, and away from the group, per the Guide for Married Couples.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Complete the First 30 Days orientation from the Guide for Married Couples and meet the supporting high councilor before your first Committee meeting.
    • Sit toward the back or side at events rather than the front, so facilitators are clearly seen as leading.
    • Keep a private, running note of encouragement or coaching points for each Coordinator and share it one-on-one, never in the group setting.
    • Offer your specific expertise (career, marriage, finances, parenting) to the Workshop Coordinator ahead of each quarterly calendar build.
    • Volunteer for the Mentorship Pairing pool early each cycle so the Coordinator can pair you before the roster fills.

    Facilitator and Mentor Network (Tier 2)¶

    Facilitators · Presenters · Discussion Hosts · Mentors — 15–25, seasonal

    Section 4.12 of the Charter

    • Reports To: The relevant Coordinator (Small-Group Facilitator, Workshop & Seminar, Discussion Group, or Mentorship Pairing)
    • Selection & Term: Recruited by the relevant Coordinator each curriculum cycle or season; an informal assignment, not a calling, unless the ward extends one separately. One curriculum cycle or season, renewable.

    Purpose

    Network members carry the four curriculum threads into every small group, workshop, and mentoring relationship (Gathering Plan, Part 4). They are not standing Committee members — they train, attend assigned sessions, and report back to their Coordinator each time.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Complete training on teaching standards and confidentiality within two weeks of being assigned.
    • Attend every session they're assigned to and immediately report attendance and participation notes to the Coordinator afterward.
    • Bring any individual needs, concerns, or opportunities to the Coordinator rather than handling sensitive situations on one's own.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Schedule your training within your first week of being asked to serve, rather than waiting for a reminder.
    • Keep a short note (notebook or app) after every session: who was new, who seemed to need follow-up, who to watch for next time.
    • Learn the referral pathway before your first session: personal concerns go to your Coordinator; anything involving safety, worthiness, or a mental-health crisis goes straight to a bishop.
    • Arrive 10 minutes early to greet people as they come in, especially anyone arriving alone.
    • Ask your Coordinator for the current curriculum thread ahead of each session so your teaching or discussion ties back to it.
