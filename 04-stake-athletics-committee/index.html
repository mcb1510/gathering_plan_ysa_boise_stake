    Stake Athletics Committee¶

    Charter Summary, Organization Chart, Roles, and Charter

    Charter and Meeting Plan Summary¶

    Charter and Meeting Plan — One Page Summary. Prepared for the Boise Idaho YSA Stake YSA Council — October 2026.

    Purpose¶

    The Gathering Plan identifies Stake Athletics as one of three ways the stake reaches young single adults, alongside Stake Activities and Education & Enrichment. This charter turns the Stake YSA Council's athletics assignment into a working playbook: who plans and runs every open-play night, league season, tournament, and inter-stake event, and how the Committee keeps the stake presidency in the loop via the Stake YSA Council.

    Structure & Key Roles¶

    The Committee is organized in three parts to keep accountability clear: two Co-Chairs who own results, a group that runs each sport and season, and a ward-level network that delivers invitations and follow-up. Across 6 wards and 4–6 sports running at once, the roster totals roughly 16–20 standing and rotating volunteers. Married Couple Advisors mentor from the sidelines — never planning, running, or refereeing in place of a YSA leader.

    Role (Count) Reports To Core Focus
    Athletics Co-Chairs — Priesthood (High Councilor) & Sister (2) YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs Jointly own the season calendar, facility scheduling, safety standards, and inclusion; represent the Committee at Council.
    Sport Commissioner (4–6, per season) Athletics Co-Chairs Scheduling, rules, equipment, and officiating for one sport; recruits Team Captains.
    Team Captain (8–12, per season) Sport Commissioner Tracks attendance; welcomes and follows up with newcomers and absentees within 48 hours.
    Connection Host (2/night, rotating) Co-Chairs / on-site Commissioner Greets newcomers every night and hands off names for 48-hour follow-up.
    Facility & Equipment Steward (1) Athletics Co-Chairs Building access, equipment inventory, first aid and safety procedures.
    Ward Athletics Representatives — Rep A & Rep B (2 per ward) Co-Chairs; bishopric for pastoral matters Rep A (Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency) and Rep B (Counselor, Ward Relief Society Presidency); no separate calling. Invite, track, and follow up within 48 hours.

    Levels of Play & Season Calendar¶

    Open Play (drop-in, no roster) leads to Stake Leagues (6–8 week, cross-ward seasons ending in a tournament) and then Inter Stake & Regional events — the same connection-before-competition on-ramp at every level.

    Season Sports & Notes
    Fall Volleyball and flag football leagues start; open play resumes with the Fall Kickoff Social.
    Winter Basketball (flagship league) and futsal; monthly "More Than a Game" devotional.
    Spring Pickleball and softball leagues start; basketball tournaments wrap up.
    Summer Coed softball, ultimate frisbee, and tournament season; inter-stake tournament.

    Meeting & Reporting¶

    Meeting Frequency Purpose
    Athletics Committee Meeting Weekly in-season Review weekly attendance and follow-up; resolve facility and equipment issues.
    Stake YSA Council Meeting / Stewardship Report Monthly Athletics section rolled into the Council's combined report to the stake presidency.
    Quarterly Priority Review Quarterly Deeper look at attendance and outreach against the four stake priorities.

    Decision Authority¶

    Committee decides on its own:

    • Season format, sport selection, and the monthly/quarterly calendar; team formation, roster rules, and day-to-day officiating.

    Escalate to the Council or stake presidency:

    • Calling changes, spending beyond budget, adding a sport/tournament not on the calendar, facility conflicts, safety incidents, or sensitive personal situations.

    Summary¶

    A YSA High Councilor and a Sister share full responsibility for athletics, staffed by Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, Connection Hosts, and a Facility & Equipment Steward, and reaching every ward through the existing Ward Representative network without adding new callings. A regular meeting rhythm and clear reporting keep the Council and the stake presidency informed, while married-couple advisors mentor from beside the court rather than from authority. The result: connection before competition, and every night and tournament remains an on-ramp toward covenants, not a destination.

    Role Descriptions¶

    Athletics Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood)¶

    Committee Leadership — paired calling

    • Reports To: YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs; also sits on the Stake YSA Council.
    • Positions Needed: One (YSA High Councilor)

    Purpose

    Stake Athletics is one of the three ways the Gathering Plan reaches young single adults, alongside Stake Activities and Learning & Enrichment. The Priesthood Co-Chair jointly owns that entire piece with the Sister Co-Chair — the calendar, the facilities, the safety standards, and ensuring that connection comes before competition at every open-play night, league, and tournament.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-chairs the committee with the Sister Co-Chair, and oversees the Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, and Connection Hosts.
    • Owns facility scheduling, equipment inventory, and safety standards for all athletics.
    • Builds and runs the season calendar (open play, leagues, tournaments, inter-stake and outdoor events) and gets it to the Communications & Calendar Lead at least 60 days ahead.
    • Makes sure every league and open-play night includes the "More Than a Game" spiritual message and the written sportsmanship standard.
    • Recruits, briefs, and checks in with Sport Commissioners each season, and represents the Committee at the Stake YSA Council meeting.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Build a rolling season-calendar template and lock it in 60+ days out, every season, before sending it to the Communications & Calendar Lead.
    • Open every Committee meeting, league night, and open-play with the same short prayer plus a 60–90 second spiritual thought, rotating who gives it, so "More Than a Game" never gets skipped when things get busy.
    • Keep a shared facility/equipment tracker with the Steward and flag Sabbath-scheduling conflicts before they reach the bishops.
    • When recruiting a Sport Commissioner, walk through purpose, time commitment, training, and who they report to before asking them to accept — the same pattern the General Handbook uses for extending a calling.
    • Hold a short weekly huddle with the Sister Co-Chair during season to compare notes before the full Committee meets.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §4.1, §7, §10; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §1–3; General Handbook 30.5, Callings in the Church (churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/30-callings-in-the-church).

    Athletics Committee Co-Chair (Sister)¶

    Committee Leadership — paired calling

    • Reports To: YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs; also sits on the Stake YSA Council.
    • Positions Needed: One

    Purpose

    The Sister Co-Chair shares full ownership of the athletics calendar and program quality, with a particular focus on ensuring that every league, open-play night, and tournament is designed so everyone — sisters, newcomers, less-active YSAs, and non-members — feels genuinely included and welcome, in line with the Gathering Plan's "belonging matters more than headcount" standard.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-chairs the committee with the Priesthood Co-Chair, sharing full ownership of the calendar and program quality.
    • Makes sure every league, open-play night, and tournament is designed so everyone feels included, welcome, and belongs.
    • Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
    • Coordinates recruiting and training Connection Hosts with the Ward Athletics Representatives.
    • Tracks weekly attendance, absentee follow-up, and less-active/non-member turnout alongside the Priesthood Co-Chair.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Build a simple "everyone belongs" pre-night checklist — greeters staffed, teams mixed across wards, a newcomer follow-up owner assigned — and run it before every league or open-play night.
    • Keep one shared sheet tracking weekly attendance and 48-hour follow-up completion side by side with the Priesthood Co-Chair so nothing slips.
    • Meet monthly with the Gathering Specialists / Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to get specific names — not just numbers — of less-active or new-move-in YSAs to personally invite.
    • Train every new Connection Host with the same short checklist: greet, learn names, invite to what's next, hand off newcomer/absentee names within 48 hours.
    • Give a quick shout-out in Committee meetings when a newcomer returns a second time, reinforcing that belonging, not headcount, is the measure of success.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §4.2, §7.2; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §1, §5; Part 2 §1 ("belonging matters more than headcount"); Boise YSA Stake Gathering Specialist role description ("Know Them by Name").

    Sport Commissioner¶

    One per active sport, per season

    • Reports To: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs.
    • Positions Needed: Four to six (rotating by season)

    Purpose

    The Sport Commissioner oversees one sport — volleyball, basketball, futsal, softball, pickleball, flag football, or ultimate frisbee — for its season, translating the Gathering Plan's athletics vision into a real schedule, real teams, and a season that opens and closes on time.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Owns scheduling, rules, equipment, and officiating for one sport during its season.
    • Recruits and briefs Team Captains for that sport, and makes sure teams are mixed across wards so people aren't just playing with the same ward crowd.
    • Works with the Facility & Equipment Steward on reservations and makes sure scheduling respects the Sabbath.
    • Runs the season opener and closer, including the stake tournament at the end.
    • Reports weekly attendance, any incidents, and roster changes to the Committee Secretary.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Put together a one-page season packet for each sport — schedule, roster rules, officiating plan, safety basics — and hand it to every Team Captain at recruitment.
    • Shadow the outgoing Commissioner for a full season before taking over solo, whenever the calendar allows.
    • Keep a running handoff document (facility contacts, equipment inventory, open items, lessons learned) updated continuously, not just at the end of the season.
    • Mix rosters across wards at sign-up, before the season starts, so cross-ward friendships form from week one.
    • Report weekly attendance and incidents to the Committee Secretary on the same simple form every week so nothing depends on memory.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §4.3, §5, §11; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §2B.

    Team Captain¶

    One per team, per season

    • Reports To: Sport Commissioner (functionally).
    • Positions Needed: Eight to twelve (rotating by season)

    Purpose

    A Team Captain looks out for teammates, not just plays with them — the on-the-court version of shepherding that keeps every player known, contacted, and welcomed, favoring YSAs who haven't led before so the calling helps them grow.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Looks out for teammates, not just plays with them: knows everyone's name, tracks attendance, and reaches out to anyone who misses within 48 hours.
    • Welcomes non-members, investigators, and less-active YSAs onto the team, no exceptions.
    • Pairs up active YSAs with less-active or new teammates ("Adopt a Player").
    • Leads a short prayer and a spiritual thought when a Commissioner or Connection Host isn't present.
    • Reports attendance and follow-up needs to the Sport Commissioner after each game.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep a simple roster sheet with name, ward, and a "contacted this week" checkbox — a lightweight version of the ward's monthly contact tracker.
    • Set a personal 48-hour rule: anyone who misses a game gets a text or call before the next one, mirroring the stake-wide absentee standard.
    • Run "Adopt a Player" proactively — pair a new or less-active teammate with an active one before the first game, not after someone starts slipping away.
    • Prepare a short prayer or spiritual thought ahead of time for weeks the Commissioner or Connection Host can't attend, and rotate who gives it.
    • Report attendance and follow-up needs to the Sport Commissioner right after each game, while it's still fresh.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §4.4, §5; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §2B, §5 ("Adopt a Player"); General Handbook 30, Callings in the Church (new members grow through service).

    Connection Host (Athletics)¶

    Two per night, rotating

    • Reports To: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs (functionally); on-site Sport Commissioner.
    • Positions Needed: Two per night (rotating)

    Purpose

    A Connection Host is a called greeter whose entire job is to find whoever's standing alone at an open-play, league, or tournament night, learn their name, and make sure they're followed up with — often the easiest calling for someone building confidence on a first assignment.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Greets people at every open-play, league, and tournament night, finds whoever's standing alone, and learns their name.
    • Encourages people to bring a friend, and welcomes newcomers, investigators, and less-active YSAs.
    • Leads the pre-game prayer and spiritual thought when no Commissioner is around.
    • Passes the names of newcomers and absentees to the appropriate Team Captain or Sport Commissioner for follow-up within 48 hours.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Arrive early enough to greet people at the door rather than starting once play begins, so no one arrives without a welcome.
    • Keep a small notecard or phone note during the night to jot newcomer and absentee names on the spot for hand-off within 48 hours.
    • Personally invite one newcomer or guest to "bring a friend" to the next event, every single night.
    • Rotate leading the pre-game prayer and spiritual thought when no Commissioner is present, building comfort with a first calling.
    • Debrief for two minutes with the Sport Commissioner after each night: who was new, who seemed to be standing alone, who needs a follow-up call.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §4.5, §5; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §2A ("bring a friend"); General Handbook 4.2, Leadership and Councils in the Church of Jesus Christ.

    Facility & Equipment Steward¶

    Stays in place year-round

    • Reports To: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs.
    • Positions Needed: One (12–18 month term)

    Purpose

    The Facility & Equipment Steward keeps the physical side of athletics running — handling building access, reservations, gear, and safety — so Sabbath conflicts, missing equipment, and unresolved safety issues never derail a season.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Handles building access, court and field reservations, and keeps scheduling Sabbath-respectful across wards.
    • Keeps the shared equipment inventory up to date, and hands out and collects gear each season.
    • Owns first aid kits, injury procedures, concussion awareness, and any liability/release forms.
    • Brings unresolved facility conflicts to the Priesthood Co-Chair to sort out with bishops.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Maintain one master booking calendar covering every gym and field reservation across wards, checked weekly against the season calendar for Sabbath conflicts.
    • Keep a simple equipment checklist — in/out, condition, season assigned — updated every time gear changes hands.
    • Stock and check first aid kits before each season begins, and post a written injury/incident procedure where Commissioners can find it fast.
    • Route liability or release forms through the office in advance of any tournament or off-site inter-stake event, per General Handbook activity-approval guidelines.
    • Escalate any facility conflict to the Priesthood Co-Chair the same week it appears, rather than letting it sit until game day.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §4.6, §9; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §6; General Handbook 20.7, Safety Policies and Guidelines for Activities (churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/20-activities).

    Ward Athletics Representatives (Rep A & Rep B)¶

    Ward-Level — two per ward (Rep A: Elders Quorum Counselor · Rep B: Relief Society Counselor)

    • Reports To: Athletics Co-Chairs (day-to-day); ward bishopric liaison (for pastoral matters).
    • Positions Needed: Two per ward — Rep A (Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency) and Rep B (Counselor, Ward Relief Society Presidency)

    Purpose

    The Ward Athletics Representatives bring the Athletics Committee's work home to a specific ward, organized by organization so both brothers and sisters receive personal attention. Rep A works from the Elders Quorum and Rep B from the Relief Society, each personally inviting people in their respective organizations, tracking their organizations' attendance, and following up with newcomers and no-shows within 48 hours — the personal, one-by-one outreach that keeps activities from becoming impersonal calendar postings.

    Key Responsibilities

    • These are the ward's existing Ward Representatives (§4.14 of the Stake YSA Council Charter) — the Counselor in the Ward Elders Quorum Presidency (Representative A) and the Counselor in the Ward Relief Society Presidency (Representative B) — no separate calling needed.
    • Each promotes athletics within their respective organization and personally invites people to open play, leagues, and tournaments.
    • Tracks the ward's athletics attendance alongside activities and enrichment for their organization, and follows up with that organization's newcomers and absentees within 48 hours.
    • Attends the monthly Stake YSA Council meeting and flags any facility, safety, or participation concerns to the Athletics Co-Chairs.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Pull your organization's YSA roster monthly (Elders Quorum for Representative A, Relief Society for Representative B) and check it against athletics attendance, flagging anyone who hasn't shown up in 30-plus days.
    • Send a personal invite — not just an announcement — to each YSA in your organization before every open-play night and league season kicks off.
    • Coordinate with your counterpart Representative before each season so invitations and follow-up cover the whole ward without duplicating effort or leaving gaps.
    • Follow up with newcomers and absentees from your organization's athletics participation within 48 hours, using the same rhythm as your other Ward Representative assignments.
    • Bring specific names and situations, not just numbers, to the monthly Stake YSA Council meeting, and carry stake-level athletics assignments back to your ward council.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §3.3, §4.7; Stake YSA Council Charter §4.14 (Ward Representatives); Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §4; Boise YSA Stake Gathering Specialist role description ("Know Them by Name," roster reconciliation).

    Married Couple Advisors¶

    Mentors, not managers — assigned by the stake presidency

    • Reports To: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs (as mentors, not managers).
    • Positions Needed: Assigned by the stake presidency, as needed

    Purpose

    Following the Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake, married couples serve as mentors and role models rather than managers — showing up, encouraging, and sharing their experience without planning, running, or refereeing in place of a YSA leader.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Show up to open-play nights, league games, and tournaments as guests and friends, not running things per the stake's married-couple guidance.
    • Offer experience and encouragement to Sport Commissioners and Team Captains when asked or prompted. Never plan, run, or referee in place of a YSA leader.
    • Model "connection before competition" and the adult-to-adult boundaries laid out in A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise Idaho YSA Stake.
    • Coordinate who attends when with the Priesthood Co-Chair.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Show up consistently to the same sport or season so YSAs get to know the couple by name.
    • Offer feedback or encouragement only when a Commissioner or Captain asks — resist the instinct to referee, coach, or run things.
    • Coordinate attendance plans with the Priesthood Co-Chair each season so coverage is spread across nights rather than concentrated on a few.
    • Share brief personal experiences or encouragement with Commissioners and Captains one-on-one after games, modeling "connection before competition."
    • Review A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake before each new season as a refresher.

    Reference

    • Athletics Committee Charter and Meeting Plan §3.4, §4.8, §10, §11; Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 3 §1.

    Detailed Charter and Meeting Plan¶

    Companion document to the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan and the Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan. Prepared for the Boise Idaho YSA Stake YSA Council — October 2026.

    1. Purpose of This Document¶

    The Gathering Plan identifies Stake Athletics as one of three ways the stake reaches young single adults, alongside Stake Activities and Education & Enrichment. The Stake YSA Council Charter then hands ownership of the athletics piece to a pair of Athletics Committee Co-Chairs on the Stake YSA Council.

    This charter turns that assignment into something you can run with day to day. It outlines how the Athletics Committee is structured and staffed, and who's responsible for planning and running every open-play night, league season, tournament, and inter-stake event described in Part 3 of the Gathering Plan. The Gathering Plan sets out what athletics needs to accomplish; this document specifies who does it, how many people it takes, how the work is split across sports and seasons, and how the Committee keeps the stake presidency in the loop through the Stake YSA Council.

    Athletics Co-Chairs should use this as their working playbook and onboarding guide, following the same format as the Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan.

    2. Who's Responsible for What¶

    The Gathering Plan and the Stake YSA Council Charter draw a clear line between deciding and doing. This charter keeps that same line for athletics:

    Group What They Do
    Stake Presidency Sets the overall vision for YSA athletics through the Gathering Plan, and signs off on this charter and any major changes. Calls and releases the Athletics Co-Chairs, based on a recommendation from the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs. Approves the yearly athletics budget, facility priorities, and any spending outside the plan. Steps in on building-use conflicts, safety issues, and sensitive personal situations that get referred up through the Council.
    Stake YSA Council Holds the Athletics Committee accountable, the same way it does for Activities and Education & Enrichment. Gets a monthly report from the Athletics Co-Chairs and rolls it into the Council's overall report to the stake presidency. Settles calendar conflicts between Athletics, Activities, and Education & Enrichment.
    Stake Athletics Committee Fully owns the running of the athletics side of the Gathering Plan, including open play, stake leagues, tournaments, and inter-stake events. Builds and runs the season calendar, staffs every sport and event, tracks weekly turnout and outreach, and adjusts on the fly. Manages the approved athletics budget and oversees Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, Connection Hosts, and the Facility & Equipment Steward. Reports progress, risks, and needs to the Stake YSA Council on a regular schedule (Section 7).

    What the Committee can decide on its own:

    • Season format, which sports to run, and the monthly/quarterly calendar, as long as it fits the four stake priorities.
    • How teams are formed, roster rules, and mixing players across wards.
    • Day-to-day officiating, schedule tweaks, and mid-season roster changes.

    Check in with the Stake YSA Council or stake presidency before:

    • Calling, releasing, or changing anyone on the Athletics Committee.
    • Spending beyond the approved budget, or adding a sport or tournament that isn't already on the calendar.
    • Facility conflicts with priesthood meetings or other stake/ward activities.
    • For safety incidents, injuries requiring medical attention, or sensitive personal situations, send these directly to the bishops or the stake presidency rather than handling them within the Committee.

    3. How the Committee Is Organized¶

    The Athletics Committee is organized in three parts to maintain clear accountability: two Co-Chairs who own the results, a group that runs each sport and season, and a ward-level network that delivers invitations and follow-up to every ward.

    3.1 Committee Leadership — Co-Chairs¶

    Two seats, filled by young single adults, who jointly own the whole athletics side of the Gathering Plan, matching Section 4.7–4.8 of the Stake YSA Council Charter:

    Seat # Seats What They Do
    Athletics Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood, YSA High Councilor) 1 Co-leads athletics with Sister Co-Chair; season calendar, facility scheduling, safety standards, and the "More Than a Game" spiritual message
    Athletics Committee Co-Chair (Sister) 1 Co-leads athletics with the Priesthood Co-Chair; makes sure sisters feel included in athletics; coordinates outreach with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads

    Two people total, and both also sit on the Stake YSA Council.

    3.2 Running Each Sport¶

    As described in Part 3 of the Gathering Plan, each season's sports are staffed by Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, and Connection Hosts, plus one Facility & Equipment Steward who stays in place year-round:

    Seat Name # Seats What They Do
    Sport Commissioner (one per active sport) TBD (rotating by season) 4–6 Handles scheduling, rules, equipment, and officiating for one sport during its season
    Team Captain (one per team) TBD (rotating by season) 8–12 Looks after one team, attendance, following up with people who miss, and taking turns with the spiritual thought
    Connection Host (Athletics) TBD (rotating) 2 per night Greets and welcomes newcomers at open-play and tournament nights
    Facility & Equipment Steward TBD 1 Building access, equipment inventory, and first aid/safety

    3.3 Ward-Level Support — Rep A & Rep B¶

    Athletics doesn't add a new ward calling. As outlined in the Stake YSA Council Charter (§4.14, Ward Representatives), each ward's two Ward Representatives: the Counselor in the Ward Elders Quorum Presidency (Representative A) and the Counselor in the Ward Relief Society Presidency (Representative B) serve as the Ward Athletics Representatives for their respective organizations. They bring the Athletics Committee's work to that ward by inviting people, tracking attendance at athletic events, and following up with newcomers and those who missed out within 48 hours. Ward Athletics Representatives are part of the YSA Council and attend the monthly Stake YSA Council meeting, bringing stake-level assignments back to their ward.

    3.4 Married Couple Advisors¶

    Following the Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake, married couples serve as mentors and role models rather than as managers. They show up, offer encouragement, and share what they've learned. They don't plan, run, or referee in place of a YSA leader.

    3.5 How Big the Committee Gets¶

    Add it up: 2 Co-Chairs + 4–6 Sport Commissioners + 8–12 Team Captains (in-season) + a rotating group of Connection Hosts + 1 Facility & Equipment Steward.

    Wards in Stake Sports at Once Team Captains Co-Chairs Total Roster
    6 4–6 8–12 2 Roughly 16–20, standing plus rotating

    Keep the Co-Chairs seats at two — that's the same clear-accountability model the Stake YSA Council Charter uses for every part of the Gathering Plan.

    4. What Each Role Does¶

    Each role below is spelled out so you can hand it straight to someone when you call or brief them.

    4.1 Athletics Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood)¶

    Reports to: YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs

    • Co-chairs the committee with the Sister Co-Chair, and oversees the Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, and Connection Hosts.
    • Owns facility scheduling, equipment inventory, and safety standards for all athletics.
    • Builds and runs the season calendar (open play, leagues, tournaments, inter-stake and outdoor events) and gets it to the Communications & Calendar Lead at least 60 days ahead.
    • Makes sure every league and open-play night includes the "More Than a Game" spiritual message and the written sportsmanship standard.
    • Recruits, briefs, and checks in with Sport Commissioners each season, and represents the Committee at the Stake YSA Council meeting.

    What it looks like when it's working: attendance trending up week over week; absentees contacted within 48 hours; seasons starting on time.

    4.2 Athletics Committee Co-Chair (Sister)¶

    Reports to: YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs

    • Co-chairs the committee with the Priesthood Co-Chair, sharing full ownership of the calendar and program quality.
    • Makes sure every league, open-play night, and tournament is designed so everyone feels included, welcome, and belongs.
    • Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
    • Coordinates recruiting and training Connection Hosts with the Ward Athletics Representatives.
    • Tracks weekly attendance, absentee follow-up, and less-active/non-member turnout alongside the Priesthood Co-Chair.

    What success looks like: How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

    4.3 Sport Commissioner (one per sport, per season)¶

    Reports to: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs

    • Owns scheduling, rules, equipment, and officiating for one sport during its season: volleyball, basketball, futsal, softball, pickleball, flag football, or ultimate frisbee.
    • Recruits and briefs Team Captains for that sport, and makes sure teams are mixed across wards so people aren't just playing with the same ward crowd.
    • Works with the Facility & Equipment Steward on reservations and makes sure scheduling respects the Sabbath.
    • Runs the season opener and closer, including the stake tournament at the end.
    • Reports weekly attendance, any incidents, and roster changes to the Committee Secretary.

    What it looks like when it's working: the season starts and ends on time; facility and officiating problems get fixed the same week.

    4.4 Team Captain¶

    Reports to: Sport Commissioner (functionally)

    • Looks out for teammates, not just plays with them: knows everyone's name, tracks attendance, and reaches out to anyone who misses within 48 hours.
    • Welcomes non-members, investigators, and less-active YSAs onto the team, no exceptions.
    • Pairs up active YSAs with less-active or new teammates ("Adopt a Player").
    • Leads a short prayer and a spiritual thought when a Commissioner or Connection Host isn't present.
    • Reports attendance and follow-up needs to the Sport Commissioner after each game.

    What it looks like when it's working: team attendance trending up; most absentees contacted within 48 hours.

    4.5 Connection Host (Athletics)¶

    Reports to: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs (functionally); on-site Sport Commissioner

    • Greets people at every open-play, league, and tournament night, finds whoever's standing alone, and learns their name.
    • Encourages people to bring a friend, and welcomes newcomers, investigators, and less-active YSAs.
    • Leads the pre-game prayer and spiritual thought when no Commissioner is around.
    • Passes the names of newcomers and absentees to the appropriate Team Captain or Sport Commissioner for follow-up within 48 hours.

    What it looks like when it's working: newcomers greeted and remembered by name each night; follow-up actually happens.

    4.6 Facility & Equipment Steward¶

    Reports to: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs

    • Handles building access, court and field reservations, and keeps scheduling Sabbath-respectful across wards.
    • Keeps the shared equipment inventory up to date, and hands out and collects gear each season.
    • Owns first aid kits, injury procedures, concussion awareness, and any liability/release forms.
    • Brings unresolved facility conflicts to the Priesthood Co-Chair to sort out with bishops.

    What it looks like when it's working: no facility conflicts left unresolved; equipment inventory is accurate; every safety incident gets logged and closed.

    4.7 Ward Athletics Representatives (Rep A & Rep B)¶

    Reports to: Athletics Co-Chairs (day-to-day); ward bishopric liaison (for pastoral matters)

    • These are the ward's existing Ward Representatives (§4.14 of the Stake YSA Council Charter) — the Counselor in the Ward Elders Quorum Presidency (Representative A) and the Counselor in the Ward Relief Society Presidency (Representative B) — no separate calling needed.
    • Each promotes athletics within their respective organization and personally invites people to open play, leagues, and tournaments.
    • Tracks the ward's athletics attendance alongside activities and enrichment for their organization, and follows up with that organization's newcomers and absentees within 48 hours.
    • Attends the monthly Stake YSA Council meeting and flags any facility, safety, or participation concerns to the Athletics Co-Chairs.

    What it looks like when it's working: ward-level attendance trending up; follow-up actually gets done.

    4.8 Married Couple Advisors¶

    Reports to: Athletics Committee Co-Chairs (as mentors, not managers)

    • Show up to open-play nights, league games, and tournaments as guests and friends are present, not running things per the stake's married-couple guidance.
    • Offer experience and encouragement to Sport Commissioners and Team Captains when asked or prompted. Never plan, run, or referee in place of a YSA leader.
    • Model "connection before competition" and the adult-to-adult boundaries laid out in A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise Idaho YSA Stake.
    • Coordinate who attends when with the Priesthood Co-Chair.

    What it looks like when it's working: consistent attendance at league nights; Co-Chairs feel coached, not managed.

    5. How People Are Chosen, and For How Long¶

    Seat How They're Chosen Term
    Athletics Co-Chairs The Co-Chair (priesthood) is a YSA High Councilor. The Co-Chair (sister) is nominated by the YSA Council. The sister Co-Chair, along with the stake presidency, is extended as a calling ("Stake YSA Athletics Committee Co-Chair"), interviewed, and set apart. 12–18 months, staggered
    Sport Commissioners Nominated by the Co-Chairs each season; assigned or called depending on how long the season runs. One season (6–8 weeks) up to a full year for the flagship winter sport
    Team Captains Picked by the Sport Commissioner from the team roster, favoring YSAs who haven't led before. One season
    Connection Hosts Recruited by the Co-Chairs or Commissioners from anyone willing, including people building confidence with a first calling. Rotating, night by night or season by season
    Facility & Equipment Steward Nominated by the Priesthood Co-Chair; extended as a calling or formal assignment. 12–18 months
    Ward Athletics Representatives Already serving as Ward Representatives — Elders Quorum Presidency Counselor (Rep A) and Relief Society Presidency Counselor (Rep B); no separate calling. Same as their existing calling
    Married Couple Advisors Assigned by the stake presidency. Ongoing, reviewed annually

    Stagger terms so that no more than half the Sport Commissioners turn over at once between seasons. When the calendar allows, build in a 2–3-week overlap between an outgoing and incoming Commissioner or Captain — YSA turnover is naturally high, so this overlap helps.

    6. Season Calendar and Levels of Play¶

    6.1 The Three Levels of Play¶

    Athletics runs three levels of the same on-ramp described in Part 3 of the Gathering Plan: connection before competition, regular touchpoints, and a Christ-centered message at every level.

    Level What It Looks Like
    Open Play Nights (Easiest to Join) Drop in anytime — no roster, no commitment, no skill needed; teams get formed on the spot each week. Happens weekly or every other week, rotating through basketball, volleyball, pickleball, and futsal. A Connection Host greets newcomers, learns their names, and personally invites them to the next event or institute.
    Stake Leagues (Building Real Connection) Structured 6–8 week seasons that end in a stake tournament and social. Teams are deliberately mixed across wards so people aren't stuck with the same ward group; each team has a Team Captain.
    Inter-Stake and Regional Events (Growing the Network) Inter-stake basketball tournament, regional softball invitational, combined outdoor adventure events, and YSA sports festivals. Great chances to build friendships and, even eternal relationships and marriages.

    6.2 The Yearly Schedule¶

    The Athletics Co-Chairs send this calendar to the Communications & Calendar Lead at least 60 days before each season starts, matching the Stake YSA Council's master calendar standard.

    Season Sports Notes
    Fall Volleyball and flag football leagues start; open play resumes Kicks off with the Fall Kickoff Social and the new-move-in welcome
    Winter Basketball (the flagship league) and futsal; weekly open-play Monthly "More Than a Game" devotional; push for winter institute enrollment
    Spring Pickleball and softball leagues start; basketball tournaments wrap up Timed with the Easter-season fireside and temple recommend campaign
    Summer Coed softball, ultimate frisbee, and tournament season Outdoor adventure crossover with Stake Activities; inter-stake tournament

    7. How We Meet and Report Back¶

    Meeting Frequency Who's There What It's For

    7.1 How Information Flows Up¶

    Information and accountability move in one clear line, so nothing gets lost between the game floor and the stake presidency:

    1. Team Captains and Connection Hosts track and report attendance, contacts, and follow-up at every game and open-play night.
    2. Sport Commissioners pull that data together by sport and bring it to the Athletics Committee Meeting.
    3. The Athletics Co-Chairs combine all of it into the athletics section of the monthly report.
    4. The Committee Secretary folds the athletics report into the Stake YSA Council's overall monthly report to the stake presidency.

    7.2 What Goes in the Monthly Report¶

    Each month, the Athletics Co-Chairs write this section for the Committee Secretary to add to the Stake YSA Council's combined report. It follows the same format as the Gathering Plan's measurement framework (Part 3, Section 7):

    • Participation — weekly attendance by sport, compared to last month and to the roster.
    • Outreach — less-active and non-member attendance; absentee contacts made within 48 hours.
    • Covenant Progression — people moving from open play to league to institute to temple night.
    • Look-Ahead — what's coming in the next 60 days and what support is needed.
    • Risks, Needs & Decisions Requested — facility, equipment, safety, or staffing items that need a decision from the Stake YSA Council or stake presidency.

    9. Safety and Equipment Basics¶

    • Facilities — coordinated access to stake and ward buildings; respecting priesthood-occupied spaces; scheduling around the Sabbath.
    • Safety — first aid kits at every game and open-play night; injury procedures and concussion awareness; liability/release forms where needed.
    • Sportsmanship — a written standard that emphasizes respect, inclusion, and Christlike conduct; no ejections, conflicts resolved with grace.
    • Cost — kept low, with a scholarship or subsidy available so no one is left out for financial reasons.
    • Equipment — a shared stake supply kept up by the Facility & Equipment Steward, with Sport Commissioners keeping their own sport's inventory.

    10. How We Operate¶

    • Every meeting, practice, and game begins and ends with prayer and a short spiritual thought tied to one of the four stake priorities — the "More Than a Game" standard.
    • Connection comes before competition; winning matters less than belonging, from open play all the way to the stake tournament.
    • Conversations about specific people (absentee outreach, sensitive needs) stay within the Committee and are only shared with bishops when they truly need to know.
    • Decisions are made by consensus between the Co-Chairs and the relevant Commissioners; if something can't be resolved, it goes to the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs within a week rather than sitting unsettled.
    • Sport Commissioners are expected at the Athletics Committee Meeting. If a Commissioner can't make it, a Team Captain fills in.
    • Married Couple Advisors come as guests and friends, not staff, per the stake's married-couple guide; they let YSA leaders greet, run, and close things out.
    • Every Committee member adheres to the same idea the Gathering Plan uses everywhere: this is an on-ramp, not a destination.

    11. Bringing New People Up to Speed¶

    • New Athletics Committee members get an onboarding packet: this charter, the relevant part of the Gathering Plan, the current participation numbers, and a handoff document from whoever had the role before them.
    • New Sport Commissioners shadow the outgoing Commissioner for a full season before taking over fully, whenever the timing allows.
    • Each Commissioner maintains a running handoff document, facility contacts, equipment inventory, open items, and lessons learned, updated continuously, not just at the end.
    • Team Captains and Connection Hosts get briefed by their Sport Commissioner within a week of starting, covering how invitations work, follow-up expectations, and the sportsmanship standard.
    • Married Couple Advisors review A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake before their first season.

    12. First 90 Days¶

    Timeframe What Happens
    Weeks 1–2 Co-Chairs confirm this charter with the Stake YSA Council, check the athletics roster, and identify Sport Commissioner candidates for the next season.
    Weeks 2–4 Sport Commissioners and the Facility & Equipment Steward are called or assigned; ward bishoprics are reminded that both Ward Representatives (Rep A and Rep B) cover Ward Athletics for their respective organizations.
    Weeks 4–6 First Athletics Committee Meeting; season calendar published to the Communications & Calendar Lead; equipment inventory confirmed.
    Weeks 6–8 Team Captains and Connection Hosts recruited and briefed; open-play nights and league play begin; facility reservations confirmed with bishops.
    Weeks 8–12 The first monthly athletics report goes to the Committee Secretary; the first "More Than a Game" devotional is held; the first quarterly review is scheduled.

    This follows the same timing as Phase 1 and the start of Phase 2 of the Gathering Plan, and the Stake YSA Councils Charter's own First 90 Days checklist — so Athletics moves on the same clock as the rest of the Stake YSA Council.

    13. Summary¶

    This charter establishes a Stake Athletics Committee for the Boise Idaho YSA Stake, run entirely by young single adults and co-led by a YSA High Councilor and a Sister Co-Chair who share full responsibility for the athletics side of the Gathering Plan. It staffs every sport and season through Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, Connection Hosts, and a Facility & Equipment Steward, and reaches every ward through the existing Ward Representative Network without adding a new calling. A regular meeting rhythm and a clear reporting line keep the Stake YSA Council and the stake presidency informed and able to guide the vision. Meanwhile, married-couple advisors mentor from beside the court, not from a position of authority. The result: connection before competition, and every open-play night, league game, and tournament remains what the Gathering Plan intends it to be, an on-ramp on the path to covenants, not a final destination.
