    Stake YSA Council — Roles¶

    Executive Co-Chair — Stake YSA Relief Society President¶

    Executive Council — Co-Chair (Sister)

    • Reports To: Stake Presidency, together with the Priesthood Executive Co-Chair.
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Sister Co-Chair shares full leadership of the YSA Council with the Priesthood Co-Chair, co-running every Executive and Full Council meeting. She champions belonging for every sister in YSA — especially less-active and new-to-the-area sisters — and ensures the Council's work stays personal, not just programmatic.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-leads the Executive Council and Full Council; co-chairs every meeting.
    • Champions the "belonging audit" and the twice-a-year belonging survey, and follows through on what it finds.
    • Makes sure sister YSAs — especially less-active and new-to-the-area sisters — are personally known and cared for, working with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister).
    • Partners with the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead.
    • Represents the Council to Relief Society.

    What success looks like

    Belonging survey completion and trends; sister participation in "Know Them by Name."

    Implementation Ideas

    • Set a standing 15-minute check-in with the Priesthood Co-Chair before every Executive Council meeting to align the agenda.
    • Launch the twice-a-year belonging survey as a short 5-question online form, then build a personal follow-up call list for anyone who reports feeling disconnected.
    • Keep a running list of sister YSAs (less-active or new-to-area) and review it monthly with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister).
    • Rotate a short visit to each ward's Relief Society meeting over the year to stay visible and gather feedback firsthand.
    • Bring specific names, not just numbers, into every Executive Council discussion about sisters who need outreach.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 1, Section 9 — "Who's Doing What."
    • General Handbook: chapter 9, "Relief Society," on the Relief Society president's role of leading efforts in God's work of salvation and serving on councils. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on the stake young single adult committee and its co-leaders. (View chapter)

    Executive Co-Chair — YSA High Councilor¶

    Executive Council — Co-Chair (Priesthood)

    • Reports To: Stake Presidency, together with the Sister Executive Co-Chair.
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Priesthood Co-Chair shares leadership of the YSA Council with the Sister Co-Chair and assumes the stake presidency's delegated priesthood oversight. He serves as the Council's primary line of accountability to the stake presidency and ensures every program remains Christ-centered and doctrine-first.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-leads the Executive Council and Full Council; carries the stake presidency's delegated priesthood oversight.
    • Is the main point of contact with the stake presidency, including monthly and quarterly reports.
    • Coordinates with bishops on building use, priesthood correlation, and sensitive referrals.
    • Reviews and recommends any Council calling changes to the stake presidency.
    • Makes sure every program stays Christ-centered and doctrine-first.

    What success looks like

    Monthly reports delivered on time; issues resolved within a week.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Put a recurring calendar hold the same week every month for the stake presidency report, built from the Secretary's dashboard.
    • Keep a simple bishop-contact log for building use and priesthood correlation issues, and close each item within a week.
    • Bring a one-page Council calling roster to stake council meetings to flag any upcoming vacancy or change that needs approval.
    • Open each Executive Council meeting with a short doctrine-centered thought tied to one of the Gathering Plan's four priorities.
    • Track "issues resolved within a week" on the same tracker the Secretary uses for the monthly dashboard.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 1, Section 9 — "Who's Doing What."
    • General Handbook: chapter 6, "Stake Leadership," on high councilors' assignment to oversee YSA and elders quorum work under the stake presidency's direction. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on the assigned high councilor's role supporting the young single adult committee. (View chapter)

    Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister)¶

    Executive Council — Co-Lead, Outreach & Shepherding

    • Reports To: Executive Co-Chairs (works most closely with the Sister Executive Co-Chair).
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister) co-owns the stake-level "Know Them by Name" contact system with the Priesthood Co-Lead and is primarily responsible for every sister YSA on the roster. She ensures every sister — active, less-active, non-attending, newly arrived, or newly turned 18, including returned missionaries — is personally known and welcomed.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-owns the "Know Them by Name" contact system with the Priesthood Co-Lead, with primary responsibility for every sister YSA on the roster.
    • Coordinates sister Gathering Specialists; teams up with ward Relief Society presidencies and Ward Rep B on less-active rescue among sisters.
    • Runs the welcome plan for newly arrived and newly turned-18 sisters, including returned missionaries (contact within 7 days, invite within 14).
    • Keeps the sister portion of the master roster current — active, less-active, non-attending, and new move-in YSAs.
    • Partners with the Sister Co-Chair on sister-specific outreach and on following through with belonging survey findings; sends sister outreach numbers to the Priesthood Co-Lead for the combined monthly report.

    What success looks like

    Percent of sisters on the roster contacted each month; sister move-ins reached within 7 days.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep the sister tab of the master roster segmented by active, less-active, non-attending, and new move-in, and review it monthly with each Ward Rep B.
    • Give every Ward Rep B a standing monthly "Know Them by Name" call list pulled straight from the sister roster.
    • Set an automatic 7-day/14-day reminder the same week a newly arrived sister, newly turned-18 sister, or returned missionary appears in ward records.
    • Hold a monthly 30-minute call with sister Gathering Specialists and ward Relief Society presidencies to trade challenges and best practices ahead of the Executive Council meeting.
    • Send sister outreach numbers to the Priesthood Co-Lead a few days before the dashboard deadline (for example, the 22nd) so the combined report reaches the Secretary on time.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 1, Section 4 — "Priority 1: See and Love as God Does."
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on leaders reaching out to single adults and including them in the work of the Church. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 21, "Ministering," on discerning needs and providing Christlike love and follow-up. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 9, "Relief Society," on Relief Society presidencies ministering to sisters and helping them progress on the covenant path. (View chapter)

    Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Priesthood)¶

    Executive Council — Co-Lead, Outreach & Shepherding

    • Reports To: Executive Co-Chairs (works most closely with the Priesthood Executive Co-Chair).
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Priesthood) co-owns the stake-level "Know Them by Name" contact system with the Sister Co-Lead and is primarily responsible for every brother YSA on the roster. He ensures every brother — active, less-active, non-attending, newly arrived, or newly turned 18, including returned missionaries — is personally known and welcomed. He reconciles the combined roster each month and delivers the combined outreach report that feeds the Council dashboard.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-owns the "Know Them by Name" contact system with the Sister Co-Lead, with primary responsibility for every brother YSA on the roster.
    • Coordinates brother Gathering Specialists; teams up with ward elders quorum presidencies and Ward Rep A on less-active rescue among brothers, including ministering coordination.
    • Runs the welcome plan for newly arrived and newly turned-18 brothers, including returned missionaries (contact within 7 days, invite within 14).
    • Keeps the brother portion of the master roster current, and reconciles the combined roster with the Sister Co-Lead each month.
    • Sends the combined outreach numbers to the Secretary each month for the dashboard; routes any referral that needs a bishop through the Priesthood Co-Chair.

    What success looks like

    Percent of brothers on the roster contacted each month; brother move-ins reached within 7 days; combined outreach report delivered on time.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep the brother tab of the master roster segmented by active, less-active, non-attending, and new move-in, and review it monthly with each Ward Rep A.
    • Give every Ward Rep A a standing monthly "Know Them by Name" call list, and use quarterly ministering interviews as a key outreach data source.
    • Set an automatic 7-day/14-day reminder the same week a newly arrived brother, newly turned-18 brother, or returned missionary appears in ward records.
    • Meet monthly with the Sister Co-Lead to reconcile the combined roster before the numbers go to the Secretary, and flag any YSA who appears on neither list.
    • Send the combined outreach report to the Secretary by the same date every month (for example, the 25th), and pass any bishop-level referral to the Priesthood Co-Chair the same day it surfaces.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 1, Section 4 — "Priority 1: See and Love as God Does."
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on leaders reaching out to single adults and including them in the work of the Church. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 21, "Ministering," on discerning needs and providing Christlike love and follow-up. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 8, "Elders Quorum," on elders quorum presidencies ministering to quorum members and coordinating ministering. (View chapter)

    Activities Co-Chair (Sister)¶

    Executive Council — Co-Chair, Activities Committee

    • Reports To: Executive Co-Chairs.
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Activities Co-Chair (Sister) sets the tone for belonging at every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting, ensuring that greeters, name tags, and cross-ward mixing make every event feel like a place where newcomers and less-active YSAs feel personally invited and welcomed.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.
    • Sets the standard for making sure everyone feels included and welcome — greeters, name tags, mixing people from different wards.
    • Sets and reviews how Connection Hosts are recruited and trained, together with Category Leads and Ward Activity Representatives.
    • Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
    • Runs the after-event debriefs with each Category Lead and Activity Lead.

    What success looks like

    How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Build a simple greeter/name-tag/cross-ward-mixing checklist that every Category Lead runs through before doors open.
    • Give Connection Hosts a one-page training card covering how to spot a newcomer and make the first introduction.
    • Ask the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads for a short list of less-active or new-move-in names before each event and personally text an invite to each one.
    • Run the after-event debrief within 48 hours and ask each Category Lead one question: "Who came for the first time, and did we follow up?"
    • Rotate which Category Lead hosts the monthly huddle so ownership of inclusion doesn't default to one committee.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 2, Section 5 — "Who's Running This."
    • General Handbook: chapter 20, "Activities," on stake activities requiring stake presidency approval and coordination through stake council. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on activities for young single adults taking place at ward, stake, or multistake level. (View chapter)

    Activities Co-Chair (Priesthood)¶

    Executive Council — Co-Chair, Activities Committee

    • Reports To: Executive Co-Chairs.
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Activities Co-Chair (Priesthood) keeps the six-week planning board on schedule, confirms Activity Lead assignments early, and manages the budget so that every stake activity is delivered on time, to a high standard, and within its financial limits.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.
    • Keeps the six-week planning board up to date, working with the Communications & Calendar Lead.
    • Confirms Activity Lead assignments that Category Leads propose, at least six weeks before each event.
    • Manages the Activities Committee budget and sorts out any scheduling conflicts between categories.
    • Sends the monthly attendance and newcomer report to the YSA Council.

    What success looks like

    Activities happening on time and at a good standard every month, and staying within budget.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Keep the six-week planning board as a single shared document with the Communications & Calendar Lead, updated every huddle.
    • Require Category Leads to propose their Activity Lead by six weeks out, then confirm in writing within 48 hours.
    • Track the Activities Committee budget in one running spreadsheet, reviewed at each Full Activities Committee meeting.
    • Flag any building-schedule or category-timing conflict the moment it appears — these need stake presidency approval before they're locked in.
    • Send the monthly attendance and newcomer report to the YSA Council on the same date every month, matching the Secretary's dashboard deadline.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 2, Section 5 — "Who's Running This."
    • General Handbook: chapter 20, "Activities," on stake activities requiring stake presidency approval and coordination through stake council. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on activities for young single adults taking place at ward, stake, or multistake level. (View chapter)

    Athletics Co-Chair (Sister)¶

    Executive Council — Co-Chair, Athletics Committee

    • Reports To: Executive Co-Chairs.
    • Positions Needed: One (1) — Executive Council seat.

    Purpose

    The Athletics Co-Chair (Sister) shares ownership of the athletics calendar and program quality with the Priesthood Co-Chair, ensuring that every league night, open play, and tournament is designed so newcomers and less-active YSAs feel personally invited and never sit on the sidelines unnoticed.

    Key Responsibilities

    • Co-chairs the committee with the Priesthood Co-Chair, sharing full ownership of the calendar and program quality.
    • Makes sure every league, open-play night, and tournament is designed so everyone feels included, welcome, and belongs.
    • Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
    • Coordinates recruiting and training Connection Hosts with the Ward Athletics Representatives.
    • Tracks weekly attendance, absentee follow-up, and less-active/non-member turnout alongside the Priesthood Co-Lead.

    What success looks like

    How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

    Implementation Ideas

    • Co-review the season calendar with the Priesthood Co-Chair monthly to keep ownership of quality shared, not siloed by sport.
    • Ask Ward Athletics Representatives for a short list of less-active or new-move-in names before each season starts.
    • Give Connection Hosts a one-page checklist for welcoming a new face at league night or open play.
    • Track weekly attendance and absentee follow-up on one shared sheet with the Priesthood Co-Chair, reviewed every Sunday evening.
    • Personally text-invite at least three less-active or new-move-in YSAs before each week's open play.

    Reference

    • Gathering Plan / Charter: Gathering Plan, Part 3, Section 4 — "Who's Running It."
    • General Handbook: chapter 20, "Activities," on ensuring adequate adult supervision and stake presidency approval for stake-wide events. (View chapter)
    • General Handbook: chapter 14, "Single Members," on stake-level activities for young single adults. (View chapter)

    Athletics Co-Chair (Priesthood)¶

    Ward Representative Role Clarification¶

    Handbook Alignment for Rep A (Elders Quorum Counselor) and Rep B (Relief Society Counselor) A companion to the Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan — Prepared October 2026

    1. Purpose of This Document¶

    The Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan calls two Ward Representatives from every ward: a Counselor in the Ward Elders Quorum (EQ) Presidency ("Rep A") and a Counselor in the Ward Relief Society (RS) Presidency ("Rep B"). Both are already sustained, set-apart callings with duties defined in the General Handbook: Serving in The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The Charter then layers on Council-specific outreach, activity-tracking, and reporting duties.

    This document lays out, side by side, (1) the Handbook baseline for each counselor calling, (2) the additional duties the Charter contemplates for that person as a Ward Rep, and (3) whether the two fit together cleanly. It closes with practical implementation ideas for each Rep and a short list of recommendations for the Council co-chairs and stake presidency.

    A note on this being a YSA stake: because the entire population of the Boise Idaho YSA Stake is young single adults, a provision that matters in a typical geographic ward — where the EQ president or RS president may assign just one counselor to focus on young single adults (General Handbook 8.3.1 and 9.3.1, referencing 14.1.2.2) — is effectively already fulfilled by every counselor in every ward. There is no "family ward" population competing for attention. This removes a potential point of friction and is discussed further in Section 4.

    2. How the Handbook Frames Counselor Callings¶

    In both organizations, the Handbook writes the list of responsibilities under the president's calling and then states, "His counselors assist him" (elders quorum) or "Her counselors assist her" (Relief Society). Counselors do not hold independent authority over the organization; they act on assignments delegated by their president, and they may act in the president's place at ward council or elsewhere only when asked to.

    General Handbook, 8.3.3–8.3.4 (elders quorum) and 9.3.2–9.3.3 (Relief Society), describe the counselor relationship as follows: a counselor "supports and sustains" the president, "accepts and prayerfully fulfills responsibilities" the president delegates, and attends ward council "when the president invites you to go in his/her absence."

    3. Rep A — Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency¶

    3.1 Handbook Responsibilities (Elders Quorum Presidency)¶

    General Handbook, chapter 8 — "the elders quorum president has the following responsibilities. His counselors assist him." Reference: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/8-elders-quorum?lang=eng

    • Serve on the ward council — as a council member helping to address ward needs, and as a representative of the elders quorum.
    • Lead the quorum's efforts to participate in God's work of salvation and exaltation.
    • Organize and oversee the service of ministering brothers; coordinate ministering assignments with the Relief Society presidency at least quarterly; hold ministering interviews at least quarterly.
    • Under the bishop's guidance, counsel with adult members of the ward. Only the bishop counsels members about worthiness, abuse, or fast-offering approval.
    • With the Relief Society presidency, help lead ward efforts to invite all to receive the blessings of the gospel; oversee the ward mission leader if one is called.
    • With the Relief Society presidency, help lead the ward's temple and family history efforts.
    • Coordinate the quorum's efforts to strengthen young adult brethren, both single and married (one counselor may be assigned to serve young single adults).
    • Meet with each quorum member individually at least once a year to discuss priesthood duties and well-being.
    • Teach quorum members their priesthood duties; oversee and help improve teaching in the quorum.
    • Plan and conduct quorum meetings; oversee elders quorum activities.
    • Help young men and prospective elders prepare to receive the Melchizedek Priesthood.
    • Oversee quorum records, reports, and finances.

    3.2 Charter-Assigned Duties (Rep A, from Section 4.14)¶

    Boise Idaho YSA Stake, Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan, Section 4.14, "Ward Representatives (two per ward)."

    • Outreach: identify elders quorum brothers to contact, extend personal invitations, and run "Know Them by Name" contacts for the ward.
    • Activities/Attendance: track ward brothers' attendance at activities, athletics, and enrichment nights; lead 48-hour follow-up for newcomers and no-shows.
    • Attend the monthly Full Council meeting and bring stake-level assignments back to the ward.
    • Raise ward-level needs and concerns up to the relevant Council Lead.

    3.3 Alignment Check¶

    Good fit: the Handbook already directs the elders quorum presidency to strengthen young adult brethren, hold ministering interviews, meet individually with members, and coordinate outreach and temple/family history work with the Relief Society presidency. The Charter's "Know Them by Name" and outreach duties operationalize that existing mandate rather than adding a new one.

    Good fit: the Handbook explicitly allows the president to "ask a counselor or another quorum member to take the lead in planning and carrying out" activities. Naming Rep A as the activities/attendance point person is a Handbook-sanctioned delegation, not a workaround.

    YSA-specific simplification: because the whole quorum is young single adults, the Handbook line "one counselor may be assigned to serve with young single adults" has no one else to be assigned to serve instead — Rep A's Charter focus and the Handbook's YSA-strengthening duty are the same population and the same work.

    Watch item — line of authority: the Charter routes Council reporting to "Council Leads for the work" and to a bishopric liaison "for anything pastoral." The Handbook still expects Rep A to act on assignments the elders quorum president delegates. The EQ president should stay in the loop on Council assignments given to Rep A so the calling relationship is not bypassed.

    Watch item — sensitive matters: the Handbook reserves worthiness, abuse, and fast-offering decisions to the bishop alone. This matches the Charter's own Section 2 rule that sensitive personal situations "go straight to the bishops and stake presidency rather than being handled within the Council" — the two documents agree, and Rep A should be reminded of this boundary during training.

    3.4 Implementation Ideas for Rep A¶

    • Build a single "Know Them by Name" roster for the elders quorum (active, less-active, non-attending, new move-in) and reconcile it against current ministering assignments each quarter — this turns the Handbook-required quarterly ministering coordination with the Relief Society presidency into the same data review the Charter asks for.
    • Ask the elders quorum president to formally delegate the activities/attendance lead role at a presidency meeting, consistent with Handbook 8.2.1.3/8.3.5 — this gives Rep A standing to track attendance and run 48-hour follow-up as part of the calling, not as a side assignment.
    • Use ministering interview notes (already required at least quarterly) as the primary source for identifying brothers who need outreach, rather than building a second, separate contact system.
    • Add a standing five-minute item to elders quorum presidency meetings: review Full Council assignments and the shared stake calendar, and flag ward-level needs to bring to the next Lead conversation.
    • Route any personal, worthiness, or safety concern that surfaces during outreach straight to the elders quorum president and bishopric — do not attempt to resolve it inside the Council.
    • Because there is no "family ward" population splitting attention, use the full counselor assignment for YSA strengthening rather than dividing time between single and married members as a mixed ward counselor would.

    4. Rep B — Counselor, Ward Relief Society Presidency¶

    4.1 Handbook Responsibilities (Relief Society Presidency)¶

    General Handbook, chapter 9 — "the Relief Society president has the following responsibilities. Her counselors assist her." Reference: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/9-relief-society?lang=eng

    • Serve on the ward council — as a council member helping to address ward needs, and as a representative of the Relief Society.
    • Lead the Relief Society's efforts to participate in God's work of salvation and exaltation.
    • Organize and oversee the service of ministering sisters; coordinate ministering assignments with the elders quorum presidency at least quarterly; hold ministering interviews at least quarterly.
    • Under the bishop's guidance, counsel with adult members of the ward. Only the bishop counsels members about worthiness, abuse, or fast-offering approval.
    • With the elders quorum presidency, help lead ward efforts to invite all to receive the blessings of the gospel.
    • With the elders quorum presidency, help lead the ward's temple and family history efforts.
    • Coordinate the Relief Society's efforts to strengthen young adult sisters, both single and married (one counselor may be assigned to serve young single adults).
    • Meet with each Relief Society member individually at least once a year to discuss well-being.
    • Teach sisters their covenant responsibilities and blessings; oversee and help improve teaching in Relief Society.
    • Plan and conduct Relief Society meetings; oversee Relief Society activities.
    • Help young women prepare to participate in Relief Society.
    • Oversee Relief Society records, reports, and finances.

    4.2 Charter-Assigned Duties (Rep B, from Section 4.14)¶

    Boise Idaho YSA Stake, Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan, Section 4.14, "Ward Representatives (two per ward)."

    • Outreach: identify Relief Society sisters to contact, extend personal invitations, and run "Know Them by Name" contacts for the ward.
    • Activities/Attendance: track ward sisters' attendance at activities, athletics, and enrichment nights; lead 48-hour follow-up for newcomers and no-shows.
    • Attend the monthly Full Council meeting and bring stake-level assignments back to the ward.
    • Raise ward-level needs and concerns up to the relevant Council Lead.

    4.3 Alignment Check¶

    Good fit: the same logic that applies to Rep A applies here — ministering interviews, individual annual visits, and the mandate to strengthen young adult sisters already give Rep B a Handbook basis for "Know Them by Name" outreach and follow-up.

    Good fit: the Handbook lets the Relief Society president "ask a counselor or another sister to take the lead in planning and carrying out" activities, so naming Rep B the activities/attendance lead is squarely within the calling.

    YSA-specific simplification: as with Rep A, the Handbook's "one counselor may be assigned to serve with young single adults" provision has nothing else to divide against in a YSA stake — the whole Relief Society is young single adult sisters, so Rep B's Charter role and the Handbook's YSA-strengthening duty are one and the same.

    Additional YSA-stake nuance: the Handbook notes that if the stake has a young single adult committee, a member of the stake Relief Society presidency serves on it. The Charter's Sister Co-Chair seat (the Stake YSA Relief Society President) already fills that role at the executive level; Rep B is simply the ward-level extension of the same structure, which is a consistent design rather than a duplicate layer.

    Watch item — line of authority and sensitive matters: the same two cautions from Rep A apply: keep the Relief Society president informed of Council assignments given to Rep B, and route worthiness, abuse, or safety concerns straight to the bishop rather than handling them inside the Council, consistent with both the Handbook and the Charter's Section 2.

    4.4 Implementation Ideas for Rep B¶

    • Maintain the ward's "Know Them by Name" roster for sisters jointly with Rep A's elders quorum roster where households or social groups overlap, so outreach is not duplicated for sisters and brothers who attend the same activities.
    • Have the Relief Society president formally delegate the activities/attendance lead assignment in a presidency meeting, so tracking and 48-hour follow-up are recognized presidency work.
    • Use required quarterly ministering interviews as the outreach data source, and use the required quarterly ministering-coordination meeting with the elders quorum presidency as the natural sync point with Rep A — this can double as a joint Ward Rep planning check-in rather than a separate meeting.
    • Pay particular attention to newly turned-18 young women transitioning into Relief Society and newly arrived sisters in the stake boundaries — both groups need the same "contact within 7 days, invite within 14" welcome the Charter calls for, and this overlaps with the Handbook's existing duty to help young women prepare to participate in Relief Society.
    • Coordinate with the Activities Co-Chair (Sister) and Athletics Co-Chair (Sister) on Connection Host recruiting so ward-level attendance tracking feeds directly into the stake-level 48-hour follow-up standard.
    • Route any personal, worthiness, or safety concern straight to the Relief Society president and bishopric.

    6. Recommendations for the Council Co-Chairs and Stake Presidency¶

    • When a ward calls its EQ and RS counselors, have the bishopric note that these two callings will also carry the Ward Rep assignment, so the calling conversation and the sustaining reflect the full scope of the responsibility from the start.
    • Ask each elders quorum and Relief Society president to formally delegate the activities/attendance-tracking piece to their respective counselor in a presidency meeting — this keeps the Charter's duties inside normal Handbook delegation rather than as a parallel assignment.
    • Align the required quarterly EQ–RS ministering coordination meeting with the Ward Reps' outreach planning, so wards are not running two separate coordination tracks for the same population.
    • Add a short footnote to the Charter (Section 4.14 or 3.2) noting that in a YSA stake, the Handbook's "one counselor assigned to serve young single adults" provision is inherently fulfilled by every EQ and RS counselor — this heads off future confusion for leaders less familiar with YSA-stake structure.
    • Reaffirm in Ward Rep training (Section 9 of the Charter) that sensitive personal matters go to the bishopric immediately, matching both the Handbook and the Charter's own Section 2 exception.
