# Stake Activities Committee

*Charter Summary, Organization Chart, Roles, and Charter*

## Charter and Meeting Plan Summary

*Charter and Meeting Plan — One Page Summary*

### Purpose

This charter puts the Comprehensive Gathering Plan into practice for the Activities Committee. It assigns clear ownership for Social, Service, Adventure, and Signature Events; sets a steady planning rhythm; and defines how the committee reports to the YSA Council — all within the boundaries set by the Stake YSA Council Charter (§2, §7).

Notable update: Ward Activity Coordinators have been restructured as Ward Activity Representatives — two per ward (Rep A, an Elders Quorum presidency counselor, and Rep B, a Relief Society presidency counselor), up from one. Committee Leads and Committee Co-Leads are now titled Co-Chairs.

### Committee Structure

| Level | Seats | Standing / Rotating |
|---|---|---|
| Committee Leadership | 2 | Standing |
| Category Leads | 5 (up to 10 in pairs) | Standing |
| Married Couple Advisors | Up to 4 couples | Standing, advisory |
| Ward Activity Representatives (Rep A & Rep B) | 12 (two per ward) | Standing |
| Activity Leads & Connection Hosts | Varies by event | Rotates each activity |

Two Co-Chairs (Priesthood — High Councilor, and Sister) jointly own the committee and report to the YSA Executive Co-Chairs. Five Category Leads — Social, Service, Adventure & Recreation (18–25 and 26–35), and Signature Events — each own one activity type start to finish. Ward Activity Representatives (Rep A: Elders Quorum Presidency Counselor; Rep B: Relief Society Presidency Counselor) carry that work into each ward. Married couple advisors mentor from the side; young single adults plan and lead every event.

### Meeting Rhythm

- Category Lead Huddle — weekly for the first 90 days, then every two weeks; assigns Activity Leads and updates the six-week planning board.
- Full Activities Committee Meeting — monthly; coordinates the calendar and trains Connection Hosts.
- Post-Event Evaluation — within 48 hours of every activity; captures what worked and who needs follow-up.
- YSA Council Report — monthly attendance and newcomer numbers.
- Quarterly Signature Event Summit and Annual Planning Retreat (Sept/Oct) — build next year's calendar.

### Accountability & Success Measures

- Activity theme, format, and calendar slot: Category Leads decide, Co-Chairs informed. New signature events or spending beyond budget: Category Leads recommend, Co-Chairs approve, YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs sign off. Sensitive personal situations go straight to Bishops/Stake Presidency.
- Success is tracked through on-time, on-checklist, in-budget delivery; the 48-hour follow-up and newcomer-return rate; Connection Hosts trained per event; and a twice-yearly belonging survey reviewed in the Quarterly Priority Review.

## Role Descriptions

### Activities Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood)
*Committee Leadership — paired calling*

- **Reports To:** YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs (jointly with the Sister Co-Chair)
- **Positions Needed:** One, called and set apart alongside the Sister Co-Chair

**Purpose**

The Priesthood Co-Chair, together with the Sister Co-Chair, owns the entire Activities Committee. This calling keeps the six-week planning board current, confirms who is leading each event, manages the committee budget, and reports attendance and newcomer trends to the YSA Council — so Social, Service, Adventure, and Signature Events all happen on schedule, at a good standard, and within budget every month.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.
- Keeps the six-week planning board up to date, working with the Communications & Calendar Lead.
- Confirms Activity Lead assignments that Category Leads propose, at least six weeks before each event.
- Manages the Activities Committee budget and sorts out any scheduling conflicts between categories.
- Sends the monthly attendance and newcomer report to the YSA Council.

> **What success looks like:** Activities happening on time and at a good standard every month, and staying within budget.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Build a shared six-week rolling planning board (spreadsheet or shared calendar), color-coded by category, and review it at every huddle so gaps surface early.
- Set a recurring "Activity Lead confirmed?" checkpoint six weeks before each event so no event enters its final month without a named lead.
- Track budget line items against the approved annual budget in a simple shared sheet, updated as soon as an expense request comes in.
- Open and close every huddle and Full Committee meeting with prayer and a short spiritual thought tied to one of the four stake priorities.
- Make the monthly attendance/newcomer report a standing agenda item so trends are visible before they become problems.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.1 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 30, "Callings in the Church"; General Handbook 20, "Activities".

### Activities Committee Co-Chair (Sister)
*Committee Leadership — paired calling*

- **Reports To:** YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs (jointly with the Priesthood Co-Chair)
- **Positions Needed:** One, called and set apart alongside the Priesthood Co-Chair

**Purpose**

The Sister Co-Chair sets the committee's standard for belonging — making sure every activity actually welcomes people, that Connection Hosts are trained well, and that less-active and new-move-in young single adults get a personal invitation. She also runs the after-event debriefs that turn each activity into a learning moment for the next one.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.
- Sets the standard for making sure everyone feels included and welcome — greeters, name tags, mixing people from different wards.
- Sets and reviews how Connection Hosts are recruited and trained, together with Category Leads and Ward Activity Representatives.
- Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
- Runs the after-event debriefs with each Category Lead and Activity Lead.

> **What success looks like:** How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Publish a one-page "Welcome Standard" (greeters at the door, name tags, a structured mixer in the first 15 minutes) that every Category Lead hands to their Activity Lead.
- Build a short, repeatable 15-minute Connection Host training — how to greet, learn names, and hand off newcomer/absentee names — delivered within two weeks of assignment.
- Hold a quick 10-minute debrief after every event: what worked, what didn't, who's new, who's less active, what follow-up is needed.
- Keep a running "needs a personal invite" list with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads so less-active and new-move-in YSAs are never missed.
- Track the 48-hour follow-up rate and the newcomer return rate as your two headline numbers each month.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.2 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 20, "Activities" (Welcoming, fellowshipping, and belonging); General Handbook 21, "Ministering".

### Social Lead
*Category Lead — Planning Team*

- **Reports To:** Activities Committee Co-Chairs
- **Positions Needed:** One (may be called as a sister-and-priesthood pair)

**Purpose**

The Social Lead owns the Social category from the Gathering Plan start to finish — the monthly stake social plus smaller ward-level get-togethers that give people an easy, low-pressure way to build friendships and have fun together.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Plans and staffs the monthly stake social, and helps coordinate smaller ward-level get-togethers.
- Lines up an Activity Lead for every social event at least six weeks ahead.
- Makes sure every social has a way to welcome first-timers — greeters, name tags, and some structured mingling.
- Reports attendance and newcomer numbers to the Secretary after each event.

> **What success looks like:** How attendance and newcomer turnout trend each month.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Keep a rolling three-month social calendar with themes so the six-week lead time never slips.
- Recruit and confirm the Activity Lead the same week a social is added to the calendar, not the week before.
- Build a reusable "first-timer kit" — name tags, a greeter station, and one mixer game — for every social.
- Send attendance/newcomer counts to the Secretary within 48 hours using a shared quick-count template.
- Rotate host wards periodically so socials naturally mix people from across the stake.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.3 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 20, "Activities" (Purposes of Church activities).

### Service Lead
*Category Lead — Planning Team*

- **Reports To:** Activities Committee Co-Chairs
- **Positions Needed:** One (may be called as a sister-and-priesthood pair)

**Purpose**

The Service Lead owns the Service category — a quarterly stake-wide service project plus ongoing ward-level service, including JustServe activities. Community service is one of the most effective ways young single adults grow spiritually and feel connected to something bigger than themselves.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Plans the quarterly stake-wide service project and coordinates ongoing ward-level service, including JustServe activities.
- Opens every service project with a prayer and a brief thought on serving as the Savior did.
- Lines up an Activity Lead for every service project.
- Reports participation and service hours to the Secretary.

> **What success looks like:** Quarterly participation numbers and the number of ward-level service opportunities offered.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Post a standing JustServe.org search each month so ward-level options are always visible to Ward Activity Representatives.
- Build the quarterly service project checklist at least eight weeks out: cause, location, sign-up, tools/supplies, and a closing testimony moment.
- Prepare a short scripture or thought on Christlike service to open every project.
- Log participation and total service hours after every project in a shared tracker for the quarterly report.
- Line up a local service partner or JustServe contact ahead of time for facility or equipment access.

**Reference**

### Adventure & Recreation Lead (18–25)
*Category Lead — Planning Team*

- **Reports To:** Activities Committee Co-Chairs
- **Positions Needed:** One (may be called as a sister-and-priesthood pair)

**Purpose**

This Adventure & Recreation Lead owns the outdoor and active-fellowship calendar for younger young single adults — hikes, lake days, camping, ski trips, and cycling — giving people an active, shared-experience way to build friendship.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Builds the monthly and seasonal adventure calendar; hikes, lake days, camping, ski trips, cycling.
- Makes sure rides and carpools are sorted out so nobody misses an outing for lack of a ride.
- Keeps groups a manageable size so people can actually connect, and lines up an Activity Lead for every outing.
- Handles safety planning for each activity; weather, terrain, waivers when needed.

> **What success looks like:** Monthly and seasonal attendance, and whether carpools and inclusion plans are actually in place.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Publish a season-ahead outdoor calendar each quarter so people can plan around work and school.
- Set up a simple carpool sign-up sheet for every outing and confirm every rider has a ride 48 hours ahead.
- Cap group size per outing (roughly 12–15) and run two smaller outings instead of one crowd when interest is high.
- Complete a quick safety and weather check — and any waiver paperwork — the week of the activity.
- Confirm an Activity Lead and Connection Host for every outing at least two weeks ahead.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.5 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 20, "Activities" (Safety and administration).

### Adventure & Recreation Lead (26–35)
*Category Lead — Planning Team*

- **Reports To:** Activities Committee Co-Chairs
- **Positions Needed:** One (may be called as a sister-and-priesthood pair)

**Purpose**

This Adventure & Recreation Lead runs the parallel outdoor and active-fellowship calendar for the 26–35 age band — the same range of hikes, lake days, camping, ski trips, and cycling — sized and scheduled for a group whose work and life stage often looks different from the 18–25 group.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Builds the monthly and seasonal adventure calendar; hikes, lake days, camping, ski trips, cycling.
- Makes sure rides and carpools are sorted out so nobody misses an outing for lack of a ride.
- Keeps groups a manageable size so people can actually connect, and lines up an Activity Lead for every outing.
- Handles safety planning for each activity; weather, terrain, waivers when needed.

> **What success looks like:** Monthly and seasonal attendance, and whether carpools and inclusion plans are actually in place.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Coordinate the monthly calendar with the 18–25 Adventure Lead so outings don't compete for the same weekend or venue.
- Offer weekday-evening or Sunday-afternoon options alongside weekend trips, since many in this age range have less flexible full-time work schedules.
- Keep group sizes manageable and pair newcomers with a familiar face on their first outing.
- Handle safety planning — weather, terrain, waivers — the week of every activity.
- Line up rides/carpools and an Activity Lead at least two weeks ahead of each outing.

**Reference**

### Signature Events Lead
*Category Lead — Planning Team*

- **Reports To:** Activities Committee Co-Chairs
- **Positions Needed:** One (may be called as a sister-and-priesthood pair)

**Purpose**

The Signature Events Lead owns the year's marquee stake-wide gatherings — the events that anchor the calendar and draw participation from across every ward: Fall Kickoff Social, Christmas Devotional and Concert, YSA Conference/Retreat, Easter fireside, Spring Service Day, Summer Celebration, and Year-End Testimony Night.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Owns the major stake-wide events for the year: Fall Kickoff Social, Christmas Devotional and Concert, YSA Conference/Retreat, Easter fireside, Spring Service Day, Summer Celebration, and Year-End Testimony Night.
- Starts planning the annual conference/retreat at least twelve weeks out, and other signature events at least eight weeks out.
- Coordinates support from Social, Service, and Adventure, plus involvement from married-couple advisors, for each signature event.
- Runs the Quarterly Signature Event Summit and updates the Co-Chairs on where things stand.

> **What success looks like:** Events that feel high-quality and happen on schedule, with good turnout across wards.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Build a master year-at-a-glance timeline listing all seven signature events with their lead times (12 weeks for the retreat, 8 weeks for the rest).
- At kickoff for each signature event, name a specific point person from Social, Service, and Adventure so support isn't left to chance.
- Invite married-couple advisors early and give them a specific, concrete role — greeting, hosting a table — rather than a general invitation.
- Hold the Quarterly Signature Event Summit on a fixed recurring date so planning never slips through the cracks.
- Route budget, venue, and vendor requests through the Co-Chairs at least two weeks before the event.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.7 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 20, "Activities" (Stake and multistake activities).

### Married Couple Advisors
*Advisory — mentor from the side*

- **Reports To:** Activities Committee Co-Chairs, day to day; Stake Presidency for the calling
- **Positions Needed:** As assigned by the Stake Presidency, coordinated across events by the Priesthood Co-Chair

**Purpose**

Married couple advisors counsel, encourage, and share what they've learned — they don't plan or run activities. Young single adults lead and plan every event; advisors attend as guests and friends, offer advice when asked or when it clearly feels right, and let the young adult leaders take the lead.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Offer advice and experience when asked, or when it feels right; never as an instruction to follow.
- Come to activities as guests and friends, not staff; let the young single adult leaders greet, run, and close things out.
- Find whoever's standing alone, learn their name, and have a real conversation without stepping in front of the Category Lead or Activity Lead.
- After events, talk privately with Category Leads and kindly ask what worked, what they learned, and what to try differently next time.
- Coordinate who attends when with the Priesthood Co-Chair.

> **What success looks like:** How confident and supported Category Leads and Activity Leads feel, and whether advisors show up to their assigned events.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Get an event assignment calendar from the Priesthood Co-Chair each month so coverage is spread evenly across advisor couples.
- Before each event, agree between yourselves who will "work the room" for newcomers and who will stay available if a Category Lead asks for input.
- Keep a small private notebook of encouragement and coaching notes to share one-on-one with Category Leads after events, not in front of the group.
- Read A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake alongside this charter when called, and revisit it each new planning cycle.
- Practice restraint: if you catch yourself about to run something, pause and ask the Activity Lead how you can support instead.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.8 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 14, "Single Members"; General Handbook 30, "Callings in the Church".

### Ward Activity Representatives (Rep A & Rep B)
*Ward-Level — two per ward (Rep A: Elders Quorum Counselor · Rep B: Relief Society Counselor)*

- **Reports To:** Category Leads, day to day; their Bishopric liaison, pastorally
- **Positions Needed:** Two per ward — Rep A (Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency) and Rep B (Counselor, Ward Relief Society Presidency)

**Purpose**

The Ward Activity Representatives bring the Activities Committee's work home to a specific ward, split by organization so both brothers and sisters receive personal attention. Rep A works from the Elders Quorum and Rep B from the Relief Society, each personally inviting people in their respective organizations, tracking their organizations' attendance, and following up with newcomers and no-shows within 48 hours — the personal, one-by-one outreach that keeps activities from becoming impersonal calendar postings.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Rep A (Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency) and Rep B (Counselor, Ward Relief Society Presidency) each identify people in their respective organizations (brothers or sisters) to reach out to and personally invite before every stake activity.
- Each Rep tracks how their organization is doing on attendance across all four activity types.
- Each Rep leads the 48-hour follow-up for newcomers and people who missed the event within their organization.
- Both Reps recruit Connection Hosts for ward-hosted events and flag ward-level needs to the right Category Lead.

> **What success looks like:** How well invitations and follow-up get done, and the ward's attendance trend.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Keep a shared ward roster checklist split by organization — brothers tracked by Rep A, sisters by Rep B — with a column for each activity type so gaps stand out at a glance.
- Invite personally — a call, text, or visit — rather than relying only on group chats or flyers, with each Rep working their own organization's roster.
- Set a 48-hour timer as soon as an activity ends so each Rep reaches every newcomer and no-show in their organization.
- Have Rep A and Rep B check in briefly before and after each activity so Connection Host recruiting and ward-level needs are covered without duplicating effort.
- Bring specific names, not just numbers, to ward council when discussing YSA needs — Rep A speaking to the brothers' needs, Rep B to the sisters'.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §3.4 and §4.9; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 21, "Ministering"; General Handbook 14, "Single Members".

### Activity Lead
*Event-Level — rotating assignment*

- **Reports To:** The Category Lead for that activity
- **Positions Needed:** One per event, confirmed by the Co-Chairs at least six weeks ahead

**Purpose**

The Activity Lead owns one specific event from start to finish — the person on the ground who works through the planning checklist, sets the spiritual tone, handles logistics, and makes sure the event is inclusive and well followed-up.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Works through the activity planning checklist (Section 7), from setting the purpose to the post-event review.
- Owns the spiritual framing, making sure it's inclusive, logistics, and the invitation plan for their event.
- Recruits and briefs Connection Hosts for the event, with the Category Lead.
- Reports attendance, newcomer, and follow-up info to the Category Lead within 48 hours of the event.

> **What success looks like:** The event happening on time, the checklist completed, and the 48-hour report turned in.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Start the six-step checklist the day you're confirmed: purpose → priority → inclusion plan → spiritual framing → logistics → invitations → follow-up → evaluation.
- Open (and close, when appropriate) the activity with prayer and a short spiritual thought tied to one of the four stake priorities.
- Recruit Connection Hosts at least two weeks out and brief them the week of the event on names or faces to watch for.
- Build the invitation plan around personal invites — ask each Ward Activity Representative who in their ward needs a personal ask, not just a calendar posting.
- Submit the 48-hour post-event report (attendance, newcomers, follow-up needed) to the Category Lead before moving to the next event.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.10 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 20, "Activities" (Organizing and planning activities).

### Connection Host (Activities)
*Event-Level — rotating assignment*

- **Reports To:** The Activity Lead for that event
- **Positions Needed:** Multiple per event, recruited and trained by the Category Lead and Ward Activity Representatives

**Purpose**

The Connection Host is the front-line welcome at every event — the one job is to find whoever's standing alone, learn their name, and bring them in, so no one leaves an activity without a real connection.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Greets everyone who arrives, especially anyone coming alone or for the first time.
- Learns names and makes real introductions rather than leaving it to chance.
- Passes along newcomer and absentee names to the Activity Lead for follow-up before leaving the event.

> **What success looks like:** Whether newcomers say they made a real connection at the event (from the post-event survey).

**Implementation Ideas**

- Arrive 10–15 minutes early and station yourself near the entrance so no one walks in unnoticed.
- Use a simple checklist for every guest: greet, learn a name, introduce them to at least one other person.
- Watch for anyone standing alone mid-event, not just at arrival — belonging gaps often show up later in the night.
- Keep a small notecard or phone note of new and absent names to hand off to the Activity Lead before you leave.
- Complete the 15-minute Connection Host training from your Category Lead within two weeks of being assigned, before serving solo.

**Reference**

- Activities Committee Charter and Meeting Plan, §4.11 and §2–3; the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 (§2, §4, §5); General Handbook 20, "Activities" (Welcoming, fellowshipping, and belonging); The Young Single Adult Committee (Strengthening Young Single Adults).

## Detailed Charter and Meeting Plan

*Stake Activities Committee — Charter and Meeting Plan. A companion to the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan and the Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan.*

### 1. Purpose of This Document

The Comprehensive Gathering Plan outlines what our Activities need to deliver: social, service, adventure, and major signature events that help people feel they belong, not just events people show up for.

The Stake YSA Council Charter places the Activities Committee Co-Chairs in charge of that work and makes them jointly responsible to the YSA Executive Co-Chairs.

This charter is about putting that plan into practice. It outlines how the Activities Committee is organized, who's on it, and how it operates — so all four activity types are planned on a steady schedule, every event has someone clearly in charge, and we report on our progress the same way every other team does.

Think of this as the working guide for the Activities Committee Co-Chairs, Category Leads, Ward Activity Representatives, Activity Leads, Connection Hosts, and married-couple advisors, a resource new people can use to understand their role.

### 2. How This Fits with the Gathering Plan and the YSA Council Charter

Three documents work together, each answering a different question, so nothing gets duplicated or missed:

| Document | Answers | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehensive Gathering Plan, Part 2 | What needs to happen: activity types, what good looks like, outreach and belonging, and planning guidelines. | Stake Presidency (approves) |
| Stake YSA Council Charter, §3–4 | Who's responsible: the Activities Committee Co-Chairs, who report jointly to the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs. | YSA Council |
| This Charter and Meeting Plan | How the Activities Committee is staffed, run week to week, and held accountable for results. | Activities Committee Co-Chairs |

Nothing here changes the boundaries or the points at which decisions must go up the chain, as set out in the Stake YSA Council Charter (Sections 2 and 7). The Activities Committee decides on and runs the activities, themes, content, and calendar slots within the four stake priorities. However, anything involving new spending beyond budget, calling changes, or sensitive personal situations still goes up through the Activities Co-Chairs to the YSA Co-Chairs and, if needed, to the Stake Presidency.

### 3. How the Committee Is Organized

The Activities Committee operates on three levels: two Co-Chairs who own the entire team; a group of Category Leads, each owning one activity type; and the people on the ground, including Ward Activity Representatives, Activity Leads, and Connection Hosts, who make each event happen.

#### 3.1 Committee Leadership

| Seat | Name | Reports To | Owns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activities Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood, High Councilor) | | YSA Executive Co-Chairs | Co-leading the committee, keeping the six-week planning schedule on track, and confirming Activity Lead assignments. |
| Activities Committee Co-Chair (Sister) | | YSA Executive Co-Chairs | Co-leading the committee, setting the standard for making people feel included, and training Connection Hosts. |

#### 3.2 Category Leads (the planning team)

Five category spots, each person owning one type of activity from start to finish and reporting to the Co-Chairs. These match the activity types laid out in the Gathering Plan (Part 2, §2):

| Category | What They Own | When It Happens |
|---|---|---|
| Social Lead | Dances, game nights, ice cream socials, outdoor barbecues, talent shows, cultural nights. | Monthly stake social |
| Service Lead | Community service projects, JustServe projects, temple and family history service. | Quarterly stake-wide service, plus ongoing ward service |
| Adventure & Recreation Lead (18–25) | Hikes, river trips, lake days, ski and snowboard trips, camping, cycling rides. | Monthly, seasonal |
| Adventure & Recreation Lead (26–35) | Hikes, river trips, lake days, ski and snowboard trips, camping, cycling rides. | Monthly, seasonal |
| Signature Events Lead | Fall Kickoff Social, Christmas Devotional and Concert, YSA Conference/Retreat, Easter fireside, Spring Service Day, Summer Celebration, Year-End Testimony Night. | Quarterly, plus the big annual conference/retreat |

Category Leads can be called in sister-and-priesthood pairs if the stake presidency prefers, as is done elsewhere on the YSA Council. Each Category Lead owns their category's calendar slot from start to finish and is the one person the Co-Chairs look to for that category.

#### 3.3 Married Couple Advisors

|---|---|

| | Advisors to the Committee |
| | Advisors to the Committee |
| | Advisors to the Committee |

As outlined in A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake, advisor couples are there to counsel, encourage, and share what they've learned, not to plan or run activities. Young single adults lead and plan every event. Advisors attend as guests, offer advice when asked or when it feels appropriate, and let the young adults take the lead.

#### 3.4 Ward Activity Representatives

As outlined in the Stake YSA Council Charter (§3.2 and §4.14), each ward has two Ward Activity Representatives — Rep A (Counselor in the Ward Elders Quorum Presidency) and Rep B (Counselor in the Ward Relief Society Presidency) — who bring the Activities Committee's work to that ward: inviting people, tracking attendance at activities for their respective organization (brothers or sisters), and following up with newcomers and those who missed out within 48 hours. Both Ward Activity Representatives serve on the YSA Council and attend the monthly Full Activities Committee meeting.

#### 3.5 Activity Leads and Connection Hosts

- **Activity Lead** — the person responsible for each activity or event: planning, the spiritual aspect, ensuring inclusivity, logistics, and follow-up (Gathering Plan, Part 2, §5). The Category Lead selects this person, and the Co-Chairs confirm the selection at least six weeks before the event.
- **Connection Host** — greets people at every activity. Recruited and trained by the Category Lead and the Ward Activity Representatives. Their one job: find whoever's standing alone, learn their name, and bring them in.

#### 3.6 Suggested Committee Size

| Level | Seats | Standing or Rotating |
|---|---|---|
| Committee Leadership | 2 | Standing |
| Category Leads | 5 (up to 10 if called in pairs) | Standing |
| Married Couple Advisors | Up to 4 couples | Standing, advisory |
| Ward Activity Representatives | 12 (two per ward) | Standing |
| Activity Leads & Connection Hosts | Varies by event | Rotates each activity |

Keep Committee Leadership at two people and Category Leads at five; that's what keeps one person clearly responsible for each piece, the same principle the Stake YSA Council Charter uses at the stake level (§3.3).

### 4. Roles and What Each One Actually Does

Here's what each role really involves. Use this wording when you call or brief someone onto the Activities Committee.

#### 4.1 Activities Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood)

Reports to: YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs (jointly with the Sister Co-Chair)

- Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.
- Keeps the six-week planning board up to date, working with the Communications & Calendar Lead.
- Confirms Activity Lead assignments that Category Leads propose, at least six weeks before each event.
- Manages the Activities Committee budget and sorts out any scheduling conflicts between categories.
- Sends the monthly attendance and newcomer report to the YSA Council.

> **What success looks like:** Activities happening on time and at a good standard every month, and staying within budget.

#### 4.2 Activities Committee Co-Chair (Sister)

Reports to: YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs (jointly with the Priesthood Co-Chair)

- Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.
- Sets the standard for making sure everyone feels included and welcome — greeters, name tags, mixing people from different wards.
- Sets and reviews how Connection Hosts are recruited and trained, together with Category Leads and Ward Activity Representatives.
- Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
- Runs the after-event debriefs with each Category Lead and Activity Lead.

> **What success looks like:** How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

#### 4.3 Social Lead

Reports to: Activities Committee Co-Chairs

- Plans and staffs the monthly stake social, and helps coordinate smaller ward-level get-togethers.
- Lines up an Activity Lead for every social event at least six weeks ahead.
- Makes sure every social has a way to welcome first-timers — greeters, name tags, and some structured mingling.
- Reports attendance and newcomer numbers to the Secretary after each event.

> **What success looks like:** How attendance and newcomer turnout trend each month.

#### 4.4 Service Lead

Reports to: Activities Committee Co-Chairs

- Plans the quarterly stake-wide service project and coordinates ongoing ward-level service, including JustServe activities.
- Opens every service project with a prayer and a brief thought on serving as the Savior did.
- Lines up an Activity Lead for every service project.
- Reports participation and service hours to the Secretary.

> **What success looks like:** Quarterly participation numbers and the number of ward-level service opportunities offered.

#### 4.5 Adventure & Recreation Lead (18–25)

Reports to: Activities Committee Co-Chairs

- Builds the monthly and seasonal adventure calendar; hikes, lake days, camping, ski trips, cycling.
- Makes sure rides and carpools are sorted out so nobody misses an outing for lack of a ride.
- Keeps groups a manageable size so people can actually connect, and lines up an Activity Lead for every outing.
- Handles safety planning for each activity; weather, terrain, waivers when needed.

> **What success looks like:** Monthly and seasonal attendance, and whether carpools and inclusion plans are actually in place.

#### 4.6 Adventure & Recreation Lead (26–35)

Reports to: Activities Committee Co-Chairs

- Builds the monthly and seasonal adventure calendar; hikes, lake days, camping, ski trips, cycling.
- Makes sure rides and carpools are sorted out so nobody misses an outing for lack of a ride.
- Keeps groups a manageable size so people can actually connect, and lines up an Activity Lead for every outing.
- Handles safety planning for each activity; weather, terrain, waivers when needed.

> **What success looks like:** Monthly and seasonal attendance, and whether carpools and inclusion plans are actually in place.

#### 4.7 Signature Events Lead

Reports to: Activities Committee Co-Chairs

- Owns the major stake-wide events for the year: Fall Kickoff Social, Christmas Devotional and Concert, YSA Conference/Retreat, Easter fireside, Spring Service Day, Summer Celebration, and Year-End Testimony Night.
- Starts planning the annual conference/retreat at least twelve weeks out, and other signature events at least eight weeks out.
- Coordinates support from Social, Service, and Adventure, plus involvement from married-couple advisors, for each signature event.
- Runs the Quarterly Signature Event Summit and updates the Co-Chairs on where things stand.

> **What success looks like:** Events that feel high-quality and happen on schedule, with good turnout across wards.

#### 4.8 Married Couple Advisors

Reports to: Activities Committee Co-Chairs, day to day; Stake Presidency for the calling

- Offer advice and experience when asked, or when it feels right; never as an instruction to follow.
- Come to activities as guests and friends, not staff; let the young single adult leaders greet, run, and close things out.
- Find whoever's standing alone, learn their name, and have a real conversation without stepping in front of the Category Lead or Activity Lead.
- After events, talk privately with Category Leads and kindly ask what worked, what they learned, and what to try differently next time.
- Coordinate who attends when with the Priesthood Co-Chair.

> **What success looks like:** How confident and supported Category Leads and Activity Leads feel, and whether advisors show up to their assigned events.

#### 4.9 Ward Activity Representatives (Rep A & Rep B) — two per ward

Reports to: Category Leads day to day; their Bishopric liaison pastorally

- Rep A (Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency) and Rep B (Counselor, Ward Relief Society Presidency) each identify people in their respective organizations (brothers or sisters) to reach out to and personally invite before every stake activity.
- Each Rep tracks how their organization is doing on attendance across all four activity types.
- Each Rep leads the 48-hour follow-up for newcomers and people who missed the event within their organization.
- Both Reps recruit Connection Hosts for ward-hosted events and flag ward-level needs to the right Category Lead.

> **What success looks like:** How well invitations and follow-up get done, and the ward's attendance trend.

#### 4.10 Activity Lead (per event, rotating assignment)

Reports to: The Category Lead for that activity

- Works through the activity planning checklist (Section 7), from setting the purpose to the post-event review.
- Owns the spiritual framing, making sure it's inclusive, logistics, and the invitation plan for their event.
- Recruits and briefs Connection Hosts for the event, with the Category Lead.
- Reports attendance, newcomer, and follow-up info to the Category Lead within 48 hours of the event.

> **What success looks like:** The event happening on time, the checklist completed, and the 48-hour report turned in.

#### 4.11 Connection Host (Activities) — per event, rotating assignment

Reports to: The Activity Lead for that event

- Greets everyone who arrives, especially anyone coming alone or for the first time.
- Learns names and makes real introductions rather than leaving it to chance.
- Passes along newcomer and absentee names to the Activity Lead for follow-up before leaving the event.

> **What success looks like:** Whether newcomers say they made a real connection at the event (from the post-event survey).

### 7. How We Plan an Activity

- Plan every stake activity at least six weeks in advance, eight weeks for signature events, and twelve weeks for the annual conference/retreat.
- Every activity follows a planning checklist, owned by the Activity Lead: state the purpose → identify the priority → build an inclusion plan → set the spiritual framing → sort logistics → plan invitations → plan follow-up → evaluate afterward.
- What good looks like: starts on time, is well organized, has a spiritual anchor, welcomes newcomers, and stays within budget.
- Post-event evaluation is quick and consistent: what worked, what didn't, who came, who was new, who's less active, and what follow-up is needed.

### 8. Outreach, Belonging, and Following Up

The Gathering Plan's approach to inclusion (Part 2, §4) is assigned to specific roles.

| Phase | Who Owns It | What's Expected |
|---|---|---|
| Before the Event | Ward Activity Representatives, with the Category Lead | Personal invitations to the right people; reminders through several channels (announcement → group text → personal text → day-of); carpools arranged. |
| During the Event | Connection Hosts, recruited and trained by the Category Lead | Greeters at every activity; name tags; structured mingling; mixing people from different wards. |
| After the Event | Activity Lead, with Ward Activity Representatives | Contact newcomers and absentees within 48 hours; invite them to the next thing; track attendance and report it to the Secretary. |

### 9. Budget and Resources

- The Activities Committee Co-Chairs manage the approved Activities budget line by line, following the Stake YSA Council Charter (§2, §7).
- Category Leads submit expense requests at least two weeks before an event; the Co-Chairs approve them within the approved budget.
- Activities stay affordable and accessible; help is available so nobody's left out for financial reasons.
- Supplies, spaces, and know-how are shared across wards, and stakeholders work together to reduce duplicate costs for larger events.
- Any spending beyond the approved budget or moving money between categories is recommended by the Co-Chairs and requires sign-off from the YSA Executive Co-Chairs before it occurs.

### 11. How the Committee Works

- Every huddle and Full Committee meeting opens and closes with prayer, and starts with a short spiritual thought tied to one of the four stake priorities.
- Young single adult Category Leads and Activity Leads plan, lead, and teach; married couple advisors support, attend, and share their experience only when asked or when it feels right.
- Decisions are made by consensus among the Co-Chairs and Category Leads; if something can't be resolved, it goes to the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs within a week rather than remaining unsettled.
- Conversations about specific people (follow-up needs, sensitive situations) stay within the Committee and are only shared with bishops on a need-to-know basis.
- Every activity should feel like an on-ramp, not a finish line. The Committee tries to practice that in its own meetings, just as it wants activities to feel for everyone else.

**A note for married couple advisors**

Come as a guest and a friend, not as staff. Let the Category Lead and Activity Lead greet, run, and close things out — that's part of helping them lead, not something you're just putting up with.

Find whoever's standing alone. Learn their name. Ask a real question, and actually listen to the answer.

Only offer your experience when asked, or when it feels right. The goal is for the Category Lead and Activity Lead to feel stronger because you were there — not for the event to only work because you were.

### 12. Getting New People Up to Speed

- New Committee members get an onboarding packet: this charter, the Comprehensive Gathering Plan (Part 2), the current planning board, and a handoff document from whoever had the role before them.
- New Category Leads shadow the person before them for one full planning cycle before taking over fully, whenever the timing allows for overlap.
- Connection Hosts receive training from their Category Lead within two weeks of being assigned and before they're on their own, covering how to greet, learn names, and hand off the 48-hour follow-up.
- Married couple advisors receive A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise, Idaho YSA Stake when they're called and should read it alongside this charter.
- Each Category Lead maintains a living handoff document: vendor and venue contacts, budget history, and lessons learned, updated as they go, not just at the end.

### 14. Keeping Track of How We're Doing

- Things to watch month to month: whether activities are delivered on time and follow the checklist; how many newcomers and less-active people show up; the 48-hour follow-up rate; and how many Connection Hosts are trained.
- Things to check over time: whether newcomers keep coming back; whether friendships form across wards and how connected people feel; whether people move from activities into institute or temple nights.
- Belonging survey: conducted twice a year, feeding into the stake-wide survey run by the Committee Secretary.
- Quarterly review: Activity results are reviewed against the four stake priorities alongside Athletics and Education & Enrichment during the Quarterly Priority Review (YSA Charter §6).

### 15. Summary

This charter establishes the Stake Activities Committee as the team of young single adults responsible for the Gathering Plan's Social, Service, Adventure, and Signature Events. Two Co-Chairs own the entire effort; five Category Leads each own one activity type from start to finish; married-couple advisors mentor from the side rather than the front; and every event has a named Activity Lead and trained Connection Hosts behind it. A steady meeting schedule and a clear reporting line keep the YSA Council in the loop and able to direct the effort.
