## Charter and Meeting Plan Summary

*Counseling Together to Strengthen Young Single Adults*

### The Vision This Committee Serves

A stake where every young single adult is seen, known, loved, and drawn toward Jesus Christ and sacred covenants. This committee unites every married adult serving in the Boise YSA Stake so no one serves alone — anchored to the handbook, the Guide, and the stake's Comprehensive Gathering Plan.

### Purpose & Charge

Established by the Boise YSA Stake Presidency, the committee unites all married adults serving in the stake, including ward leaders, advisors, activities and athletics couples, institute mentors, ministering companions, and stake specialists, into a single counseling body tasked with Training, Sharing, Implementing the Guide and Gathering Plan, Counseling Together, and Reinforcing the Role of YSAs.

### Membership & Roles

Membership includes every married adult sustained to a stake-serving calling. A Stake Presidency-appointed Committee Coordinator convenes meetings and ensures the annual plan is carried out; three additional roles share specific responsibility:

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Committee Coordinator | Sets the agenda, conducts monthly meetings, tracks action items, and reports quarterly to the Stake Presidency. |
| Stake Presidency Liaison | Represents Presidency direction, approves the annual meeting plan, and carries counsel back to the Presidency. |
| Committee Members | Attend regularly, share honest experience, and apply the Guide and Gathering Plan in their calling. |

### Meeting Cadence

| Meeting | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Full Committee Meeting | Monthly | Training, shared best practices, and counsel |
| Stake Presidency Check-in | Quarterly | Report progress; receive direction and approval |
| Annual Vision & Planning Retreat | Yearly (August) | Review the year; approve next year's plan |

### Alignment & Measures of Success

Every training topic is weighed against the Gathering Plan's four priorities: See and Love as God Does, Teach Divine Identity and Potential, Focus on Jesus Christ, and Invite All to Come unto Christ and Make and Keep Sacred Covenants. The committee's monthly training rotates through the Guide in step with this rhythm.

Leading indicators: meeting attendance, best practices shared, adults trained per Guide section, ministering assignments staffed.

Lagging indicators: institute/activity/athletics attendance, temple recommend and temple-night participation, reactivation and new-move-in retention.

### Who We Serve / This Is a Training Stake

Young single adults are unmarried adult members of the Church, ages 18 to 35 (General Handbook: *Serving in The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints*, chapter 14, "Single Members," https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/14-single-members). In this stake, they study, work, serve missions, date, wrestle with faith, build careers, seek meaningful connections, and prepare for temple covenants, marriage, and lifelong discipleship. Please speak of them, and to them, as adults.

| We say | We refrain from saying |
|---|---|
| Young adults, young single adults, brothers and sisters, members of the stake | Youth, kids, the kids, my kids, these kids, the youth |
| Leaders, presidencies, activity leads, ministers | Helpers, the youth committee, chaperones, supervisors |
| Counsel with, train, support, share experience | Run it for them, take over, manage them, keep them in line |

> **This is not a youth calling.** Youth programs protect, supervise, and prepare minors. This calling is different. You serve adults. Treat them as peers in the gospel who happen to have less leadership experience than you.

**This Is a Training Stake**

The Savior increased others' capacity by giving them responsibility and opportunities to grow. He did not do their work for them. He taught, trusted, and then sent them.

The members of this stake are very capable. Most are serving in leadership callings for the first time. Lack of experience is not a weakness to work around. Providing opportunities for growth, experience, and connection is the point of a young single-adult stake.

Our role is to share experience, not to replace theirs. We have seen wards function, councils counsel, activities succeed and fail, and ministering bless people one by one. As a mentor, role model, and teacher, be quick to listen, observe, and quietly offer your experience. Then let them lead.

**What training looks like here**

We seek to emulate the Savior's example as he taught His disciples. He walked with them, sat with them, and ate with them. He was patient, and he listened.

So, we:

- Sit beside a presidency, not in front of it.
- Seek to understand what they are trying to accomplish before we suggest how. First observe, listen, and inquire.
- Teach from the handbook, not from "the way we did it in our last ward," unless that way is also the Lord's way.
- After an event or meeting, debrief with kindness: what worked, what we learned, what might be tried next time.
- Celebrate honest effort. Accept their best work.
- When something needs to be corrected, do it privately, specifically, and with patience.

### How We Serve Effectively

Keep these six commitments. They are at the heart of this orientation.

1. **Share your experience.** You are here because you have walked farther down the covenant path. First observe, listen, and seek to understand; a young adult is more likely to welcome your counsel once they trust that you have truly heard them. Seek the Spirit before you speak; let Him show you whether this is a moment to offer a story or a moment to simply stay quiet. Offer stories, patterns, and counsel when asked or when the Spirit prompts, and offer them as an invitation to consider, never as a directive to follow. Do not make yourself the expert in the room. Make the handbook and the young adult leaders the experts, and add what you have learned along the way.

2. **Encourage, inspire, and motivate.** First-time leaders often underestimate themselves. Listen closely enough to hear what they are actually afraid of, and let that shape how you encourage them. Your confidence in them, offered consistently over time, builds the relationship of trust that lets them take risks. Speak hope. Notice growth. Invite them to stretch, never pressure or shame them into it. Remind them that the Lord qualifies those He calls, and pray for the discernment to see that qualification before they can see it in themselves.

3. **Allow them to lead and fulfill their callings.** Delegating is more than giving an assignment. It includes teaching and trusting another person to fulfill it. Build relationships of trust by listening first, understanding how they think and what they need before you hand over a task. If you plan the activity, run the council, or "just handle it," you have taken the calling from them and compelled where you should have invited. Coach. Invite their ideas, invite their questions, invite them to try their own way. Then let them do the work, including the parts that will be imperfect. Seek the Spirit to know when silence is the greater act of trust over intervention.

4. **Correct when needed, with kindness and patience.** Doctrine, safety, and handbook principles are not optional. But how you raise a concern still matters as much as that you raise it. Listen first to understand what happened and why before you speak. When something needs to change, speak privately and soon. Be specific. Assume good intent. Invite them to see the change and choose it themselves; do not simply command it. Seek the Spirit, so your words carry correction without wounding. Leave them more confident, not smaller. Never correct a young adult leader in front of those they lead.

5. **Be with them outside of Sunday meetings.** Firesides, stake activities, service projects, athletics, and FHE are where names become people and where trust is actually built, not through instruction, but through shared time. Make a genuine effort to attend. Stand among them, not over them. Learn faces. Laugh. Serve. Listen to what they talk about when no one is teaching a lesson. Stay long enough to have a real conversation, and let the Spirit direct you to the conversation that matters most at that time.

6. **Minister one by one.** Programs gather people. Ministering turns gathering into belonging. Learn names. Notice who is missing. Listen for what is unsaid as much as what is said. Follow the Spirit to the one. He knows needs you cannot see. Extend an invitation to connect rather than expecting anyone to come to you. Details are in section 6.

### The Stake Vision and Gathering Plan

The Boise YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan is the local roadmap for this work. Please review it. You do not need to memorize every calendar item, but you should understand the vision, the four priorities, and the three channels through which we gather.

**Vision**

A stake where every young single adult is seen, known, loved, and drawn toward Jesus Christ and sacred covenants.

Gathering means reaching young adults and fostering connection and belonging with Jesus Christ and with one another. Reaching includes those who already attend, those who have stepped away, new move-ins, and friends of other faiths. Belonging is connection that precedes conversion. It is created through relationships of trust in which divine identity is affirmed, and covenants are invited.

Guiding principle: every event, league, and class is a covenant-path on-ramp, not an end in itself.

Planning rubric: all activities will be evaluated based on their alignment with one or more of our stake priorities and how it will help a young adult move closer to Christ.

**The four priorities**

| # | Priority | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | See and Love as God Does | Outreach, shepherding, and inclusion. Know them by name. |
| 2 | Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential | Education and enrichment. Institute, workshops, mentorship. |
| 3 | Focus on Jesus Christ and His Atonement | Spiritual depth and healing. Firesides, temple nights, testimony. |
| 4 | Invite All to Come unto Christ and Make and Keep Sacred Covenants | Conversion and covenant progression. Baptism, temple, calling, marriage. |

**Three channels of gathering**

The plan runs through three coordinated channels. Young adult leaders own the work. All efforts are to be pointed toward Jesus Christ.

| Channel | Description |
|---|---|
| Stake Activities | Monthly stake-wide gatherings plus quarterly signature events: socials, service, adventure, firesides, temple nights, and the annual conference or retreat. Designed so a person arriving alone is seen, welcomed, and connected. |
| Stake Athletics | Open-play nights, stake leagues, and inter-stake events. Connection before competition. Every gathering opens with prayer and a brief spiritual thought. A low-pressure on-ramp for newcomers and less-active members. |
| Education and Enrichment | Institute as the core engine, monthly enrichment nights, workshops, discussion groups, and mentorship. Threads: Who God says I am. Why I am here. Who I am becoming. How I come unto Him. |

**Activity Standards**

- It begins with prayer and a brief "why we gather" moment connected to one of the four priorities.
- A newcomer attending alone can find an instant connection through greeters, hosts, name tags, and mixed-ward teaming.
- Young adults plan, conduct, and teach. Married couples support and attend.
- Personal invitations occur before the event. Follow-up occurs within 48 hours after.
- Someone leaves with a next step: sacrament meeting, institute, temple night, a calling, or a friendship.
- It is well planned, on time, spiritually grounded, affordable, and newcomer-friendly.

**Standards you will hear often**

- Know Them by Name: every young adult on the roster, active and less-active, is personally contacted each month.
- New move-ins are contacted within 7 days and personally invited to the next ward or stake event within 14 days.
- Newcomers and absentees are followed up within 48 hours.
- Elders Quorum presidencies, Relief Society presidencies, and ward mission leaders hold primary responsibility for contacting, welcoming, and ministering. Married couples train, coach, model, support, and befriend. We need to ensure that our efforts do not detract from their opportunity to own and fulfill their stewardship.

### Be Present, Then Minister

**Be with them**

Being with our young single adults during Sunday meetings is important, but that is not enough. Being present at events and activities outside Sunday meetings, without an agenda beyond showing up, is one of the best ways to build relationships of trust that enable real ministering. Trust is rarely built through a program; it is built by simply being there, again and again, until they stop wondering why you came.

Make a genuine effort to attend:

- Firesides and devotionals
- Stake activities and signature events
- Service projects
- Family home evening gatherings
- Athletics nights and open play, even if you only stay for the opening prayer and a few conversations
- Institute classes or enrichment nights from time to time

> **How to be present without taking over.** Arrive as a guest and a friend, not as staff. Let young adult leaders greet, conduct, and close; this is itself an invitation to lead, not a hand-off you're merely tolerating. Find the person standing alone. Learn a name. Ask a real question, and then actually listen to the answer rather than moving on to the next greeting. Stay off to the side of the program. If something is going poorly, help the leader succeed rather than stepping up to the microphone; seek the Spirit to discern whether the moment calls for a quiet word to the leader afterward or no intervention at all. The goal is for them to feel stronger because you came, not for the event to run because you did.

**Individual ministering**

Ministering means serving others as the Savior did. He loved, taught, prayed for, comforted, and blessed those around Him. He listened before He spoke, and He invited people to follow rather than compelling them. In our stake, individual ministering is how Priority 1 becomes personal — and it depends entirely on relationships of trust built one person at a time.

Formal ministering assignments are made by the elders quorum and Relief Society with the bishop's approval. Every single brother is assigned ministering brothers. Every single sister is assigned ministering sisters and ministering brothers. Assignments are made to individuals, not to apartments or groups. A married couple may be assigned to minister together.

Your calling does not replace formal ministering assignments that young single adults have been given. Your efforts are intended to supplement and magnify them. You minister in three ways:

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| As a disciple | Watch over specific brothers and sisters the Spirit places on your heart. Learn names. Remember details. Pray for them by name. Reach out when they are missing. Let the Spirit, not a roster, tell you who needs you today. |
| As a mentor | Offer adult friendship and life experience — work, education, dating and marriage, temple worship, emotional resilience, and discipleship. Mentorship in our stake is intentional, structured, and centered on identity, purpose, potential, and Christ. A mentor listens long before offering perspective, and offers it as counsel to weigh, never as instruction to obey. |
| As a trainer | Help quorum and Relief Society leaders see ministering as the central effort of gathering: monthly contact, the 7-day / 14-day welcome, 48-hour follow-up, and prayerful rescue of those who have stepped away. Train them to build trust, listen, and seek the Spirit themselves, rather than simply handing them a checklist to execute. |

**What individual ministering looks like**

- Learn the names, stories, hopes, and current next steps of the people in your circle of influence.
- Listen first. Seek to understand without judging or rushing to offer counsel; trust is built in the listening, long before any advice is given.
- Seek the Spirit before you reach out, and seek Him again in the middle of the conversation. He often changes the plan.
- Ask about adult life: work, school, roommates, family far away, temple worship, institute, and what they hope for.
- Invite, do not manage. "Would you come with us to temple night?" is ministering. Checking whether they "should be dating more" is not. An invitation leaves them free to say no; a management question does not.
- Open your home when it feels natural: a Sunday dinner, an FHE group, a holiday, or a quiet conversation. These informal moments are often where trust deepens fastest.
- Notice the quiet ones. The Savior sought the one.
- When needs are serious — worthiness, mental health, safety, finances — love them and walk them to the bishopric, elders quorum, or Relief Society. Avoid becoming a private counselor for what priesthood keys should hold.
- Keep confidences. Report needs through proper channels, not through hallway conversation. Trust, once broken by a breach of confidence, is very difficult to rebuild.

**Adult-to-adult boundaries**

- We are friends and mentors, not parents, chaperones, or social directors.
- A man and a woman should not travel alone together for Church assignments unless they are married to each other, or are both single.
- Use wisdom in one-on-one settings. When in doubt, meet as a couple or in a visible, appropriate place.
- Do not hover at dances or activities as if you were supervising youth.
- Refrain from commenting on bodies, dating choices, or asking questions like "when are you going to get married?"
- Correct privately. Praise publicly. Even boundary corrections are more effective when offered as an invitation to adjust than as a rebuke; assume good intent, and leave the relationship of trust intact.

Here are some ideas of things you can do to get started.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|

**Questions worth asking**

- How can I incorporate our stake priorities into my own personal ministry?
- Which of the four priorities could I focus on first?
- Who has not been seen, known, or invited this month? Prayerfully seek inspiration to "find the one".
- Who are the young adult leaders I am here to strengthen?
- What are some things that I can allow young adult leaders to do for themselves?

### A Closing Invitation

You have been called to help young adults become the leaders the Lord already knows they can be, and to love them toward Jesus Christ while they learn. We have not been called to make our stake look like a well-run youth program.

Please share what you know, then step back enough to let them receive their own revelation. Encourage more than you instruct. Attend more than you announce. Minister more than you manage. When you must correct, do so as the Savior would: clearly, kindly, and with a hand still extended.

Every gathering is an on-ramp. Every name is a soul. Every first-time leader is in training, and so are we.

We are grateful you are here. We love you. The young adults of this stake will be different because you have chosen to see them, know them, love them, teach them, and then let them lead.

Welcome to the work!

> **Keep this close**
> - They are young adults and brothers and sisters, never "youth" or "kids".
> - This is a training stake. Listen, observe, share experience. Let them lead.
> - Encourage, inspire, motivate. Correct privately, with kindness.
> - Be at firesides, activities, service, athletics, and FHE.

> - Point every effort to Jesus Christ.

## Detailed Charter and Meeting Plan

*Counseling Together to Strengthen Young Single Adults*

### Why This Committee Exists

The Boise Idaho YSA Stake Presidency calls married couples as ward leaders, advisors, activity and athletics couples, institute and enrichment mentors, ministering companions, and stake specialists. Each serves faithfully within a separate calling, but often without a shared table for training, counsel, and encouragement.

The Married Adults Committee brings together all married adults serving in the Boise YSA Stake into a single body. It exists so that no one serves alone, so that what one ward or couple learns benefits the whole stake, and so that our combined effort stays anchored to the handbook, the Guide, and the stake's Comprehensive Gathering Plan.

### Purpose and Charge

The Married Adults Committee is established by the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Presidency and includes all married adults called to serve in the Boise Idaho YSA Stake. Its charge is to:

- **Train.** Provide ongoing, practical training for married adults serving with young single adults.
- **Share.** Share ideas, successes, best practices, and challenges across wards, callings, and channels.
- **Implement the Guide.** Put into practice the principles outlined in *A Guide for Married Adults Serving in the Boise Idaho YSA Stake*.
- **Implement the Gathering Plan.** Carry out the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan across activities, athletics, and education and enrichment.
- **Counsel together.** Counsel together regularly to identify and meet the needs of young single adults.
- **Reinforce the role of YSAs.** Reinforce the role of young single adults in the stake and how married adults can best teach, minister, mentor, support, and invite them to come unto Christ, keep covenants, prepare for the temple, and develop practical life skills.

> **The vision this committee serves.** A stake where every young single adult is seen, known, loved, and drawn toward Jesus Christ and sacred covenants. Every training topic, shared idea, and agenda item exists to move that vision forward.

This committee mirrors the pattern generally described for young single adult committees: married and single adults counseling together, under priesthood direction, to help YSAs come unto Christ (*Strengthening Young Single Adults*, "The Young Single Adult Committee," https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/strengthening-young-single-adults-manual/01-ysa-committee).

### Committee Membership

Membership includes every married adult sustained to a calling that serves the Boise Idaho YSA Stake and is a member of this committee for as long as that calling is held.

### Committee Coordinator

The Stake Presidency appoints a Committee Coordinator from among the married adults serving in the stake. The Coordinator convenes and conducts committee meetings, coordinates with the Stake Presidency liaison, and ensures the annual meeting plan in Section 7 is carried out.

> **This is a peer counsel, not a program office.** The committee does not run activities, athletics, or enrichment for young single adults; young adult leaders own that work. The committee exists so the married adults supporting them can counsel together, train, and grow more capable as mentors.

### Roles and Responsibilities

Four roles carry specific responsibility for the committee's work. Every member shares responsibility for showing up, contributing, and applying what is learned.

**Committee Coordinator**

Sets the agenda with the Stake Presidency liaison, conducts monthly meetings, tracks action items, and reports progress on the Gathering Plan and the Guide's six commitments to the Stake Presidency each quarter.

**Stake Presidency Liaison**

Represents the Stake Presidency's direction and priorities, approves the annual meeting plan, and carries counsel and concerns from the committee back to the Presidency.

**Ward Advisor Representatives**

Bring ward-level needs, successes, and challenges to the committee; carry training and best practices back to their ward's married adult advisors and priesthood and Relief Society leaders.

**Committee Members**

Attend regularly, participate in training, share honest experience, and apply the Guide's principles and the Gathering Plan in their own calling between meetings.

### Operating Principles

The committee counsels in the same way its members are asked to serve, by observing, listening, and sharing experience rather than directing. These principles, drawn from the Guide's six commitments, govern how the committee operates:

| We do | We avoid |
|---|---|
| Sit as peers and counsel together | Speak as if one couple or ward has the only right answer |
| Share experience as an invitation to consider | Turn a shared idea into a directive for every ward |
| Let young adult leaders own their work | Plan, run, or take over YSA activities, leagues, or classes |
| Bring challenges with specific, kind counsel | Air frustration without seeking a solution together |
| Return to the handbook and the Guide when unsure | Default to "the way we did it before" |

Councils of this kind exist so members can counsel together, seek revelation for the people they serve, and coordinate the Lord's work in their area of responsibility (General Handbook, chapter 4, "Leadership and Councils in the Church of Jesus Christ," https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/4-leadership-in-the-church-of-jesus-christ).

**Standard Monthly Agenda**

Rooted in the handbook's pattern for council and presidency meetings: prepare in advance, counsel together, and close with clear action.

1. Opening prayer and a brief spiritual thought tied to one of the four stake priorities
2. Review of action items and follow-through from the previous meeting
3. Successes: what worked this month, and why
4. Challenges: what is hard right now, and what counsel is needed
5. Training topic: one section of the Guide or one Gathering Plan channel, rotated monthly (see Section 7)
6. Gathering Plan alignment check: are our efforts still pointed at Jesus Christ and the four priorities?
7. Assignments and next steps, with names and dates attached
8. Closing prayer

The handbook describes presidency and council meetings as occasions to strengthen individuals and families, prepare in advance, and close with clear direction — the pattern this committee follows each month (General Handbook, chapter 29, "Meetings in the Church," https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/29-meetings-in-the-church).

### Communication and Reporting

- A shared digital calendar carries meeting dates, training topics, and Gathering Plan milestones for every ward.
- A group text or messaging channel keeps married adults connected between meetings for quick questions and reminders.
- The Chair sends a brief written summary after each full committee meeting: decisions made, best practices shared, and assignments given.
- The Chair reports quarterly to the Stake Presidency on training completed, challenges raised, and progress on the Gathering Plan.
- Ward advisor representatives carry relevant training and best practices back into their ward council.

### Measures of Success

**Leading indicators (tracked by the committee)**

- Committee meeting attendance across wards and callings
- Number of best practices and successes documented and shared
- Number of married adults trained on each section of the Guide
- Ministering and mentoring assignments actively staffed and current

**Lagging indicators (observed with the Gathering Plan)**

- Institute enrollment and stake activity, athletics, and enrichment attendance trends
- Temple recommend holders and stake temple night participation
- Reactivations, new-move-in retention, and callings filled with confidence
