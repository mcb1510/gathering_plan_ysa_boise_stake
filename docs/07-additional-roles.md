# Additional Stake-Level Roles

## Stake Gathering Specialist

*Combined role — Ward Shepherding & Geographic Stake Liaison*

- **Reports To:** Stake Presidency (stake calling); works paired with Ward Priesthood and Relief Society leadership, attends ward council, and typically coordinates with the YSA Stake Clerk (records/referral tracking). Ward shepherding is coordinated with the Stake YSA Council's Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads — the sister specialist with the Sister Co-Lead and the brother specialist with the Priesthood Co-Lead. Geographic Stake Liaison efforts are coordinated through the counselor and high councilor assigned to oversee young single adults.
- **Positions Needed:** One married adult couple per ward

### Purpose

The Boise YSA Stake Gathering Specialist is the execution layer for shepherding, invitations, and referral coordination for young single adults (ages 18–35). On the ward side, this calling drives the "Know Them by Name" initiative, less-active rescue, and new move-in welcome efforts. On the stake side, it bridges the gap between the YSA Stake and the surrounding geographic ("home") stakes that feed young single adults into the YSA Stake, ensuring referrals don't get lost and new YSAs feel welcomed quickly. Together, these efforts keep communication and personal outreach flowing consistently in every direction — within the ward and across stake boundaries.

### Category 1: Ward Shepherding

**Key Responsibilities**

- Assist with ("Know Them by Name") initiative — every YSA on the ward roster, active and less-active, is contacted monthly.
- Partner with Ward Priesthood and Relief Society leadership to prayerfully identify specific less-active individuals to reach.
- Contact new move-ins within 7 days and personally invite them to the next ward or stake event within 14 days.
- Maintain and reconcile the ward roster against actual attendance.
- Coordinate with the Ward Representatives (Rep A and Rep B) on follow-up for absentees.
- Meet monthly with your Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead — the sister specialist with the Sister Co-Lead, the brother specialist with the Priesthood Co-Lead — to reconcile the ward's portion of the master roster and agree on who needs a personal contact.
- Report outreach contacts made each month to the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads for the Council's combined outreach report, and to the Stake Presidency and High Council for the monthly stewardship review.
- Regularly meet with other Stake Gathering Specialists to coordinate efforts, share challenges, successes, and best practices.
- Welcome and assimilate new YSAs into the ward — graduating high school seniors, returning missionaries, and new move-ins alike — coordinating with Young Men/Young Women, seminary, and ward mission leaders to identify them early and extend a personal invitation to the next YSA activity.
- Track upcoming transitions within the YSA population — including individuals turning 26 (moving between YSA age groupings) and turning 35 (exiting the YSA program) — so each transition is anticipated well ahead of time rather than discovered after the fact.
- Shepherd YSAs approaching their 35th birthday out of the program with a warm handoff — introducing them to leadership in their next geographic ("home") ward or singles ward before their transition date so no one simply "ages out" unnoticed.
- Include upcoming new-YSA arrivals (graduating seniors, returning missionaries, move-ins) and age-based transitions (26th and 35th birthdays) in the monthly stewardship report to the Stake Presidency and High Council.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Keep a simple monthly contact tracker for each YSA on the roster.
- Set a 7-day/14-day timer as soon as a new move-in is identified.
- Bring specific names, not just statistics, to prayerful ward council discussions.
- Maintain a "Transitions Calendar" tracking upcoming graduation dates, expected missionary return dates, and 26th/35th birthdays so outreach is proactive instead of reactive.
- Partner with seminary and Young Men/Young Women leaders each spring to get a list of graduating seniors and personally invite them to a YSA activity before they even turn 18.
- Create a "Welcome Home" checklist for returning missionaries: personal visit or call within their first week home, an invitation to the next activity, and an introduction to ward YSA leadership.
- Set a reminder 60–90 days before a YSA's 35th birthday to begin transition conversations, including identifying their next ward and personally introducing them to that ward's leadership.
- Add a "New/Transitioning YSA" flag to the monthly contact tracker so new move-ins, graduating seniors, returning missionaries, and age-based transitions receive extra follow-up touchpoints during their first 90 days.

### Category 2: Geographic Stake Liaison

**Key Responsibilities**

- Serve as the single point of contact between the Boise YSA Stake presidency and the leadership (stake presidencies, YSA-assigned high councilors, and ward-level YSA representatives) of those assigned geographic stakes (approximately 3) that feed young single adults into the YSA Stake.
- Maintain an up-to-date contact directory of the YSA-assigned leader (counselor/high councilor) and ward YSA advisor/representative in each assigned geographic stake.
- Facilitate the referral process — ensure each geographic stake has a simple, known way to notify the YSA Stake when a young single adult turns 18, moves into the area, or becomes eligible to attend YSA units.
- Track referrals through to contact, confirming that every referred individual is reached out to by a YSA ward leader within about one week, and following up if they are not.
- Push information out to geographic stake leaders — activity calendars, conference dates, institute schedules, and gathering-place information — so it reaches graduating youth and their families.
- Schedule and help conduct periodic coordination meetings (recommend quarterly) with geographic stake YSA-assigned leaders and ward advisors, similar to the existing YSA committee cadence.
- Report status and metrics to the YSA Stake presidency on referral volume, response times, and any stakes that are under-referring or unresponsive.
- Attend Stake YSA Council and stake council meetings as needed to relay geographic-stake feedback and concerns.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Set up a shared referral form (via LCR/Church tools or a simple online form) each geographic stake's YSA representative can submit when a new young single adult is identified.
- Build a basic tracking sheet (spreadsheet or shared document) logging referral date, source stake, assigned ward contact, and follow-up status — reviewed weekly.
- Establish a named point person (ward YSA advisor or rep) in every geographic stake so communication doesn't rely on ad-hoc contacts.
- Send a brief quarterly email/newsletter to all geographic stake leaders summarizing upcoming YSA activities, institute offerings, and any process changes.
- Hold an annual training/kickoff meeting with geographic stake presidencies or their designated YSA leaders to clarify the referral process and answer questions.

## Appendix: Activity Planning Checklist

*For Activity Leads and Ward Activity Coordinators — complete one per activity.*

Use this checklist for every stake or ward YSA activity, from a small social to a signature event. Start at least 6 weeks before the activity date (signature events need more lead time). Based on the planning standards in Part 2 of the Boise YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan.

### Activity At-a-Glance

- **Activity name:**
- **Date / time:**
- **Category:**
- **Hosting ward(s):**
- **Activity Lead:**
- **Checklist started:**

**Category options:** Social · Service · Adventure/Recreational · Signature Stake-Wide Event · Spiritual/Worship · Athletics · Enrichment

**Which of the Four Priorities does this activity primarily serve?** (check one or more)

- [ ] See and Love as God Does (outreach, shepherding, inclusion)
- [ ] Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential (educational, enrichment)
- [ ] Focus on Jesus Christ and the Atonement (spiritual depth, healing)
- [ ] Invite All to Come unto Christ and Make/Keep Covenants (conversion, covenant progression)

> **Planning rubric:** this activity should not be approved until a leader can state which priority it serves and how it moves a YSA one step closer to Christ.

### Step 1 — Purpose & Priority

- [ ] Stated in one sentence: which priority does this activity serve, and how does it move a YSA one step toward Christ?
- [ ] Activity type matches a defined category (see options above).
- [ ] Confirmed this is not scheduled purely for entertainment without a gospel purpose.

### Step 2 — Inclusion Plan

- [ ] Imagined a YSA arriving alone, new to the area, less-active, or attending for the first time.
- [ ] Connection Host(s) called, briefed, and assigned to greet and integrate newcomers.
- [ ] Name tags ordered or prepared.
- [ ] Structured mingling or mixer activity planned — never left to chance.
- [ ] Cross-ward teaming plan set, where applicable, to build friendships beyond ward lines.
- [ ] Cost-conscious design confirmed; subsidy available so no YSA is excluded for financial reasons.

### Step 3 — Spiritual Framing

- [ ] Opening prayer assigned.
- [ ] "Why we gather" moment planned — a leader or YSA connects this activity to the Savior and to belonging.
- [ ] Spiritual thought rotation assigned to a YSA (rotate who gives it, don't default to the same leader every time).
- [ ] Closing gratitude moment planned.
- [ ] Next covenant-step invitation identified (institute, temple night, ministering, calling, sacrament, etc.).
- [ ] Scheduling respects the Sabbath and the sacred nature of Church buildings.

### Step 4 — Logistics

- [ ] Facility booked and confirmed.
- [ ] Budget requested and approved.
- [ ] Equipment and supplies list finalized.
- [ ] Transportation / carpool plan in place so no YSA is excluded by lack of a ride.
- [ ] Safety needs addressed where applicable (first aid, release forms, etc.).
- [ ] RACI written and shared before the event (see table below).

**RACI — Who Plans, Funds, Invites, Hosts, Follows Up**

| Role | Name / Assignment |
|---|---|
| Plans (Activity Lead) | |
| Funds (Budget owner) | |
| Invites (Ward Activity Coordinator(s)) | |
| Greets / Hosts (Connection Host(s)) | |
| Follows Up (Ward Activity Coordinator(s)) | |

### Step 5 — Invitation Plan (Pre-Event Outreach)

- [ ] Personal invitation chain started — Ward Activity Coordinators identify target individuals and invite personally, not just by announcement.
- [ ] "Bring a friend" encouraged and members equipped to invite someone who wouldn't otherwise attend.
- [ ] Multi-channel reminders scheduled: announcement → group text → personal text → day-of reminder.
- [ ] Less-active and new-move-in target list reviewed; each person personally invited.
- [ ] Non-member friends welcome message included, where appropriate.

### Step 6 — At-Event Belonging (Day-Of)

- [ ] Connection Hosts briefed and stationed near the entrance.
- [ ] Name tags available at check-in.
- [ ] Structured mingling activity ready to run.
- [ ] Cross-ward teams or small groups set.
- [ ] Attendance tracked, including newcomers, less-active, and non-member guests.

### Step 7 — Follow-Up Plan (Within 48 Hours)

- [ ] Newcomer and absentee list compiled the day of the activity.
- [ ] Personal contact made with every newcomer within 48 hours.
- [ ] Personal contact made with every absentee within 48 hours.
- [ ] Next-step invitation extended in every follow-up contact.
- [ ] Patterns of repeated absence flagged to a Stake Gathering Specialist for shepherding outreach.

### Step 8 — Evaluation (Within 1 Week)

- [ ] What worked well?
- [ ] What didn't work?
- [ ] Who attended — new, less-active, and non-member counts logged.
- [ ] What follow-up is still needed?
- [ ] Lessons carried forward to the next activity.

**Notes:**

### Quality Bar

An activity meets the Boise Idaho YSA Stake standard when it:

- [ ] Starts on time.
- [ ] Is well-organized.
- [ ] Is spiritually anchored.
- [ ] Is newcomer-friendly.
- [ ] Stays within budget.

### Planning Timeline Reminder

- [ ] 6-week planning lead time minimum for regular stake and ward activities.
- [ ] Longer lead time required for signature stake-wide events (Fall Kickoff Social, Christmas Devotional, YSA Conference/Retreat, Summer Celebration, Year-End Testimony Night, etc.).
- [ ] Planning sequence: purpose stated → priority identified → inclusion plan → spiritual framing → logistics → invitation plan → follow-up plan → evaluation.

## Appendix: Our Stake Priorities (Quick-Reference Poster)

Four priorities to guide us this season — all centered on Jesus Christ.

1. **See and Love as God Does** — Look at every person the way Heavenly Father does — with genuine love and without judgment.
2. **Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential** — Help each young adult know who they are, why they're here, and what they can become.
3. **Focus on Jesus Christ and the Atonement** — Keep the Savior at the center of everything we do.
4. **Invite All to Come unto Christ and Make/Keep Covenants** — Extend genuine invitations to draw closer to Christ through covenants.

To help these stick, they're shortened to four words:

| See | Love | Teach | Invite |
|---|---|---|---|
| See each other as God does | Love as He loves | Identity, purpose, and potential | All to come unto Christ |

*Everything we do flows from — and points back to — Jesus Christ and His Atonement.*
