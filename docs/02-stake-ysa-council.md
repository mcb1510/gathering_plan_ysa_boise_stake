## Charter and Meeting Plan — One Page Summary

*Prepared for the Boise Idaho YSA Stake YSA Council — October 2026*

### Purpose

This charter operationalizes Section 9 of the Gathering Plan — "Organization, Roles, and Accountability." It defines how the Stake YSA Council is organized, staffed, and held accountable for executing the plan day-to-day, and serves as the executive co-chairs' standing reference and onboarding guide.

| Total Council Members | Executive Council Seats | Ward Reps (6 Wards) | Priority Program Areas |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 13 | 12 | 4 |

### Who Does What

- **Stake Presidency** — sets vision and priorities; approves the Gathering Plan, budget, and major changes; calls/releases Council leaders; receives regular reports and offers guidance.
- **Stake YSA Council** — owns execution across Activities, Athletics, Education & Enrichment, and Outreach/Shepherding; builds the calendar, manages the approved budget, and reports progress on a regular schedule.

### Council Structure

| Group | Composition |
|---|---|
| Executive Council | 13 seats, all YSAs: Executive Co-Chairs (RS President; High Councilor); Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads (2); Activities, Athletics, and Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (2 each); Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead; Communications & Calendar Lead; Council Secretary/Metrics Recorder. |
| Ward Representatives | 2 per ward: Rep A (Ward Elders Quorum Presidency Counselor) covers outreach for EQ brothers; Rep B (Ward RS Presidency Counselor) covers activity attendance/follow-up for RS sisters — an extension of their existing calling. Both sit on the Full Council. |
| Terms | Co-chairs and ward reps serve as long as they hold their base calling; Leads/Secretary serve 12–18 months, staggered, with a 4–6 week handoff overlap. |

### Meeting Rhythm & Reporting

| Meeting | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Council | Weekly (first 90 days), then biweekly | Review numbers, plan next 2–4 weeks, work shepherding assignments |
| Full Council | Monthly | Coordinate calendar, cross-ward invitations, training |
| Stake Presidency Report | Monthly | Written + verbal update on highlights, risks, decisions needed |
| Quarterly Priority Review | Quarterly | Deeper look at progress on the four priorities |
| Belonging Survey Review | Twice yearly | Review survey results and act on findings |
| Annual Planning Retreat | Yearly (Sept/Oct) | Look back and build next year's calendar and goals |

Reporting flow: Ward Reps → Leads → Secretary (dashboard & monthly report) → Co-Chairs → Stake Presidency.

### Decision Guide

- **Council decides:** program content, calendar/event design, and duty rotation within the Council.
- **Council recommends, Stake Presidency approves:** new signature events or spending beyond budget; extending or releasing callings.
- **Flagged immediately:** building-use/priesthood conflicts and sensitive personal or pastoral situations go straight to bishops and the stake presidency.

### First 90 Days

- **Weeks 1–2:** confirm charter, clean up roster, identify leads.
- **Weeks 2–4:** call/set apart leads and secretary; bishoprics identify the assigned EQ & RS counselors as ward reps.
- **Weeks 4–6:** first Executive Council meeting; belonging survey launched.
- **Weeks 6–8:** first Full Council meeting; 90-day calendar published.
- **Weeks 8–12:** first monthly report and first activities delivered under the new structure.

> **Bottom line:** A 25-member, YSA-run Council — 13 executive seats plus ward reps — with a steady meeting rhythm and single reporting line keeps the stake presidency informed and able to guide through vision and approval, while the Council holds real ownership, clear accountability, and a direct connection to the plan's four priorities.

## Role Descriptions

### Executive Co-Chair — Stake YSA Relief Society President
*Executive Council — Co-Chair (Sister)*

- **Reports To:** Stake Presidency, together with the Priesthood Executive Co-Chair.
- **Positions Needed:** One (1) — Executive Council seat.

**Purpose**

The Sister Co-Chair shares full leadership of the YSA Council with the Priesthood Co-Chair, co-running every Executive and Full Council meeting. She champions belonging for every sister in YSA — especially less-active and new-to-the-area sisters — and ensures the Council's work stays personal, not just programmatic.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Co-leads the Executive Council and Full Council; co-chairs every meeting.
- Champions the "belonging audit" and the twice-a-year belonging survey, and follows through on what it finds.
- Makes sure sister YSAs — especially less-active and new-to-the-area sisters — are personally known and cared for, working with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister).
- Partners with the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead.
- Represents the Council to Relief Society.

> **What success looks like:** Belonging survey completion and trends; sister participation in "Know Them by Name."

**Implementation Ideas**

- Set a standing 15-minute check-in with the Priesthood Co-Chair before every Executive Council meeting to align the agenda.
- Launch the twice-a-year belonging survey as a short 5-question online form, then build a personal follow-up call list for anyone who reports feeling disconnected.
- Keep a running list of sister YSAs (less-active or new-to-area) and review it monthly with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister).
- Rotate a short visit to each ward's Relief Society meeting over the year to stay visible and gather feedback firsthand.
- Bring specific names, not just numbers, into every Executive Council discussion about sisters who need outreach.

- Bring a one-page Council calling roster to stake council meetings to flag any upcoming vacancy or change that needs approval.

### Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister)
*Executive Council — Co-Lead, Outreach & Shepherding*

- **Reports To:** Executive Co-Chairs (works most closely with the Sister Executive Co-Chair).
- **Positions Needed:** One (1) — Executive Council seat.

**Purpose**

The Outreach & Shepherding Co-Lead (Sister) co-owns the stake-level "Know Them by Name" contact system with the Priesthood Co-Lead and is primarily responsible for every sister YSA on the roster. She ensures every sister — active, less-active, non-attending, newly arrived, or newly turned 18, including returned missionaries — is personally known and welcomed.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Co-owns the "Know Them by Name" contact system with the Priesthood Co-Lead, with primary responsibility for every sister YSA on the roster.
- Coordinates sister Gathering Specialists; teams up with ward Relief Society presidencies and Ward Rep B on less-active rescue among sisters.
- Runs the welcome plan for newly arrived and newly turned-18 sisters, including returned missionaries (contact within 7 days, invite within 14).
- Keeps the sister portion of the master roster current — active, less-active, non-attending, and new move-in YSAs.
- Partners with the Sister Co-Chair on sister-specific outreach and on following through with belonging survey findings; sends sister outreach numbers to the Priesthood Co-Lead for the combined monthly report.

> **What success looks like:** Percent of sisters on the roster contacted each month; sister move-ins reached within 7 days.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Keep the sister tab of the master roster segmented by active, less-active, non-attending, and new move-in, and review it monthly with each Ward Rep B.
- Give every Ward Rep B a standing monthly "Know Them by Name" call list pulled straight from the sister roster.
- Set an automatic 7-day/14-day reminder the same week a newly arrived sister, newly turned-18 sister, or returned missionary appears in ward records.
- Hold a monthly 30-minute call with sister Gathering Specialists and ward Relief Society presidencies to trade challenges and best practices ahead of the Executive Council meeting.
- Send sister outreach numbers to the Priesthood Co-Lead a few days before the dashboard deadline (for example, the 22nd) so the combined report reaches the Secretary on time.

### Activities Co-Chair (Sister)
*Executive Council — Co-Chair, Activities Committee*

- **Reports To:** Executive Co-Chairs.
- **Positions Needed:** One (1) — Executive Council seat.

**Purpose**

The Activities Co-Chair (Sister) sets the tone for belonging at every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting, ensuring that greeters, name tags, and cross-ward mixing make every event feel like a place where newcomers and less-active YSAs feel personally invited and welcomed.

**Key Responsibilities**

- Co-chairs every Category Lead huddle and Full Activities Committee meeting.

- Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
- Runs the after-event debriefs with each Category Lead and Activity Lead.

> **What success looks like:** How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

**Implementation Ideas**

- Build a simple greeter/name-tag/cross-ward-mixing checklist that every Category Lead runs through before doors open.
- Give Connection Hosts a one-page training card covering how to spot a newcomer and make the first introduction.
- Ask the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads for a short list of less-active or new-move-in names before each event and personally text an invite to each one.
- Run the after-event debrief within 48 hours and ask each Category Lead one question: "Who came for the first time, and did we follow up?"
- Rotate which Category Lead hosts the monthly huddle so ownership of inclusion doesn't default to one committee.

## Ward Representative Role Clarification

*Handbook Alignment for Rep A (Elders Quorum Counselor) and Rep B (Relief Society Counselor)*
*A companion to the Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan — Prepared October 2026*

### 1. Purpose of This Document

The Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan calls two Ward Representatives from every ward: a Counselor in the Ward Elders Quorum (EQ) Presidency ("Rep A") and a Counselor in the Ward Relief Society (RS) Presidency ("Rep B"). Both are already sustained, set-apart callings with duties defined in the General Handbook: Serving in The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The Charter then layers on Council-specific outreach, activity-tracking, and reporting duties.

This document lays out, side by side, (1) the Handbook baseline for each counselor calling, (2) the additional duties the Charter contemplates for that person as a Ward Rep, and (3) whether the two fit together cleanly. It closes with practical implementation ideas for each Rep and a short list of recommendations for the Council co-chairs and stake presidency.

A note on this being a YSA stake: because the entire population of the Boise Idaho YSA Stake is young single adults, a provision that matters in a typical geographic ward — where the EQ president or RS president may assign just one counselor to focus on young single adults (General Handbook 8.3.1 and 9.3.1, referencing 14.1.2.2) — is effectively already fulfilled by every counselor in every ward. There is no "family ward" population competing for attention. This removes a potential point of friction and is discussed further in Section 4.

### 2. How the Handbook Frames Counselor Callings

In both organizations, the Handbook writes the list of responsibilities under the president's calling and then states, "His counselors assist him" (elders quorum) or "Her counselors assist her" (Relief Society). Counselors do not hold independent authority over the organization; they act on assignments delegated by their president, and they may act in the president's place at ward council or elsewhere only when asked to.

General Handbook, 8.3.3–8.3.4 (elders quorum) and 9.3.2–9.3.3 (Relief Society), describe the counselor relationship as follows: a counselor "supports and sustains" the president, "accepts and prayerfully fulfills responsibilities" the president delegates, and attends ward council "when the president invites you to go in his/her absence."

### 3. Rep A — Counselor, Ward Elders Quorum Presidency

#### 3.1 Handbook Responsibilities (Elders Quorum Presidency)

_General Handbook, chapter 8 — "the elders quorum president has the following responsibilities. His counselors assist him." Reference: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/8-elders-quorum?lang=eng_

- Serve on the ward council — as a council member helping to address ward needs, and as a representative of the elders quorum.
- Lead the quorum's efforts to participate in God's work of salvation and exaltation.
- Organize and oversee the service of ministering brothers; coordinate ministering assignments with the Relief Society presidency at least quarterly; hold ministering interviews at least quarterly.
- Under the bishop's guidance, counsel with adult members of the ward. Only the bishop counsels members about worthiness, abuse, or fast-offering approval.
- With the Relief Society presidency, help lead ward efforts to invite all to receive the blessings of the gospel; oversee the ward mission leader if one is called.
- With the Relief Society presidency, help lead the ward's temple and family history efforts.
- Coordinate the quorum's efforts to strengthen young adult brethren, both single and married (one counselor may be assigned to serve young single adults).
- Meet with each quorum member individually at least once a year to discuss priesthood duties and well-being.
- Teach quorum members their priesthood duties; oversee and help improve teaching in the quorum.
- Plan and conduct quorum meetings; oversee elders quorum activities.
- Help young men and prospective elders prepare to receive the Melchizedek Priesthood.
- Oversee quorum records, reports, and finances.

#### 3.2 Charter-Assigned Duties (Rep A, from Section 4.14)

_Boise Idaho YSA Stake, Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan, Section 4.14, "Ward Representatives (two per ward)."_

- Outreach: identify elders quorum brothers to contact, extend personal invitations, and run "Know Them by Name" contacts for the ward.
- Activities/Attendance: track ward brothers' attendance at activities, athletics, and enrichment nights; lead 48-hour follow-up for newcomers and no-shows.
- Attend the monthly Full Council meeting and bring stake-level assignments back to the ward.

#### 3.3 Alignment Check

Good fit: the Handbook already directs the elders quorum presidency to strengthen young adult brethren, hold ministering interviews, meet individually with members, and coordinate outreach and temple/family history work with the Relief Society presidency. The Charter's "Know Them by Name" and outreach duties operationalize that existing mandate rather than adding a new one.

Good fit: the Handbook explicitly allows the president to "ask a counselor or another quorum member to take the lead in planning and carrying out" activities. Naming Rep A as the activities/attendance point person is a Handbook-sanctioned delegation, not a workaround.

YSA-specific simplification: because the whole quorum is young single adults, the Handbook line "one counselor may be assigned to serve with young single adults" has no one else to be assigned to serve instead — Rep A's Charter focus and the Handbook's YSA-strengthening duty are the same population and the same work.

Watch item — line of authority: the Charter routes Council reporting to "Council Leads for the work" and to a bishopric liaison "for anything pastoral." The Handbook still expects Rep A to act on assignments the elders quorum president delegates. The EQ president should stay in the loop on Council assignments given to Rep A so the calling relationship is not bypassed.

Watch item — sensitive matters: the Handbook reserves worthiness, abuse, and fast-offering decisions to the bishop alone. This matches the Charter's own Section 2 rule that sensitive personal situations "go straight to the bishops and stake presidency rather than being handled within the Council" — the two documents agree, and Rep A should be reminded of this boundary during training.

#### 3.4 Implementation Ideas for Rep A

- Build a single "Know Them by Name" roster for the elders quorum (active, less-active, non-attending, new move-in) and reconcile it against current ministering assignments each quarter — this turns the Handbook-required quarterly ministering coordination with the Relief Society presidency into the same data review the Charter asks for.
- Ask the elders quorum president to formally delegate the activities/attendance lead role at a presidency meeting, consistent with Handbook 8.2.1.3/8.3.5 — this gives Rep A standing to track attendance and run 48-hour follow-up as part of the calling, not as a side assignment.
- Use ministering interview notes (already required at least quarterly) as the primary source for identifying brothers who need outreach, rather than building a second, separate contact system.
- Add a standing five-minute item to elders quorum presidency meetings: review Full Council assignments and the shared stake calendar, and flag ward-level needs to bring to the next Lead conversation.
- Route any personal, worthiness, or safety concern that surfaces during outreach straight to the elders quorum president and bishopric — do not attempt to resolve it inside the Council.
- Because there is no "family ward" population splitting attention, use the full counselor assignment for YSA strengthening rather than dividing time between single and married members as a mixed ward counselor would.

- Works with the Outreach & Shepherding Co-Leads to make sure less-active and new-move-in YSAs get personally invited.
- Coordinates recruiting and training Connection Hosts with the Ward Athletics Representatives.
- Tracks weekly attendance, absentee follow-up, and less-active/non-member turnout alongside the Priesthood Co-Lead.

What success looks like: How many 48-hour follow-ups actually happen, and how many newcomers come back.

#### 4.8 Athletics Co-Chair (Priesthood)

**Reports to:** Executive Co-Chairs

- Co-chairs the committee with the Sister Co-Chair, and oversees the Sport Commissioners, Team Captains, and Connection Hosts.
- Owns facility scheduling, equipment inventory, and safety standards for all athletics.
- Builds and runs the season calendar (open play, leagues, tournaments, inter-stake and outdoor events) and gets it to the Communications & Calendar Lead at least 60 days ahead.
- Makes sure every league and open-play night includes the "More Than a Game" spiritual message and the written sportsmanship standard.
- Recruits, briefs, and checks in with Sport Commissioners each season, and represents the Committee at the Stake YSA Council meeting.

What it looks like when it's working: attendance trending up week over week; absentees contacted within 48 hours; seasons starting on time.

#### 4.9 Education & Enrichment Co-Chair (Sister)

**Reports to:** Executive Co-Chairs

- Co-leads the Education & Enrichment Committee; co-chairs every Committee Core meeting with the Priesthood Executive Co-Chair.
- Runs the Monthly Stake Enrichment Night and rotates through the four curriculum threads over the year, together with the Priesthood Co-Chair.
- Champions sister-specific facilitator and mentor recruiting, and makes sure small groups are set up so no one sits alone.
- Represents the Committee at the monthly YSA Council meeting alongside the Priesthood Co-Chair.
- Makes sure every enrichment night ends with a specific invitation to take a covenant step.

Success metric: Monthly enrichment attendance and trend; facilitators trained per cycle.

#### 4.10 Education & Enrichment Co-Chair (Priesthood)

**Reports to:** Executive Co-Chairs

- Co-leads the Education & Enrichment Committee; carries the Committee's reporting relationship with the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs.
- Coordinates the Workshop/Seminar series and keeps it aligned with the Institute, drawing on stake members' expertise.
- Reviews Committee coordinator changes and recommends them to the YSA Council Co-Chairs.
- Makes sure Christ-centered framing and doctrine-first teaching standards are upheld across every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group.
- Delivers the monthly enrichment stewardship report to the YSA Council Co-Chairs, prepared by the Secretary.

Success metric: On-time monthly reports; teaching-standards compliance across all sessions.

#### 4.11 Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead — Institute Council President

**Reports to:** Executive Co-Chairs

- Partners with Institute faculty to boost enrollment and remove barriers (rides, scheduling).
- Owns the covenant pathway map (attend → connect → institute → temple → calling → mission/marriage) for people being tracked.
- Runs the temple recommend campaign and coordinates stake temple nights and group temple trips.
- Lines up institute course topics with the stake's four curriculum threads where it makes sense.
- Reports institute enrollment and temple recommend numbers to the Secretary.

What success looks like: Institute enrollment rate, temple recommend holders, and temple night attendance.

#### 4.12 Communications & Calendar Lead

**Reports to:** Executive Co-Chairs

- Owns the one shared stake calendar used by every ward.
- Runs the invitation flow for every event: personal invite → text reminder → group announcement.
- Creates outreach materials (announcements, group texts, graphics) for the other leads.
- Gathers stories — testimonies and experiences from leaders and YSAs — to keep momentum going.
- Keeps the Council's communication channels (group chat, shared calendar, contact lists) up to date.

What success looks like: Calendar published 60+ days out; invite chain completed for each event.

#### 4.13 Council Secretary / Metrics Recorder

**Reports to:** Executive Co-Chairs

- Takes minutes and tracks action items for every Executive and Full Council meeting.
- Puts together the monthly dashboard using data from each lead.
- Drafts the monthly report and quarterly review packet for the stake presidency (Section 6).
- Tracks the Council roster, term dates, and upcoming turnover.
- Runs the twice-a-year belonging survey and compiles the results.

What success looks like: Reports delivered on time; dashboard is accurate and complete.

#### 4.14 Ward Representatives (two per ward)

**Reports to:** Council Leads for the work; their bishopric liaison for anything pastoral

- Counselor in Ward EQ Presidency (Rep A), Counselor in Ward RS Presidency (Rep B).[^1]
  - Outreach: identifies ward members in their respective organizations to contact, extend personal invitations, and runs "Know Them by Name" contacts for that ward.
  - Activities/Attendance: tracks ward attendance at activities, athletics, and enrichment nights for their respective organizations; leads 48-hour follow-up for newcomers and no-shows in that ward/organization.
- Both attend the monthly Full Council meeting and bring stake-level assignments back to their ward.
- Both raise ward-level needs and concerns up to the relevant Lead.

What success looks like: Ward-level contact and follow-up rate; ward attendance trend.

### 5. How People Are Chosen and How Long They Serve

| Seat | How They Get There | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Co-Chairs | Already called as YSA High Councilor and Stake YSA Relief Society President; no separate calling needed. | As long as they hold that calling |
| Leads, Co-Leads & Secretary | Nominated by the Co-Chairs with input from the stake presidency; called or formally assigned ("Stake YSA Gathering Specialist – [role]"); interviewed and set apart. | 12–18 months, staggered |
| Ward Representatives | Already called as Elders Quorum Presidency Counselor and Counselor in Ward Relief Society Presidency; no separate calling needed. | As long as they hold that calling |

Stagger terms so no more than half of the Council turns over at once. Give outgoing and incoming leads a 4–6 week overlap for the handoff, since the YSA population changes often. Where a role is shared by two co-leads or co-chairs, stagger their terms so at least one experienced partner stays in place.

[^1]: Because this is a YSA stake, its entire population is young single adults. The General Handbook provision that lets an elders quorum or Relief Society president assign one counselor to focus specifically on young single adults (General Handbook 8.3.1 and 9.3.1, referencing 14.1.2.2) is written for a geographic ward that also serves married and older members. In this stake, there is no such other population to divide attention from — every EQ and RS counselor already serves an entirely young-single-adult organization. Rep A and Rep B's Ward Rep duties are therefore an operational extension of a responsibility the Handbook already assigns them, not a new or competing calling. No additional YSA-focused counselor assignment is needed on top of this Charter's Ward Rep structure.

### 6. Meetings and Reporting

| Meeting | How Often | Who Comes | What It's For | What Comes Out of It |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive Council Meeting | Weekly for the first 90 days, then every other week | Co-Chairs, Leads, Secretary | A spiritual thought and prayer for specific people; review the numbers; plan the next 2–4 weeks; work through cross-program issues; seek guidance for specific YSAs by name; line up shepherding assignments | Minutes and action items; updated rescue and shepherding list |
| Full Council Meeting | Monthly | Executive Council + all Ward Reps | Coordinate the calendar; plan cross-ward invitations; train; hear updates from the wards. | Updated master calendar; ward assignments. |
| Stake Presidency Report | Monthly | Co-Chairs (+ a Lead if needed) and the Stake Presidency | A short written report plus a verbal update on highlights, risks, and any decisions needed | Approved next steps; resource decisions |
| Quarterly Priority Review | Quarterly | Executive Council + Stake Presidency | A deeper look at progress on the four priorities and all the numbers | Calendar and program adjustments |
| Belonging Survey Review | Twice a year | Full Council + Stake Presidency | Review the belonging survey results and act on what it shows | Program adjustments |
| Annual Planning Retreat | Yearly (Sept/Oct) | Full Council + Stake Presidency | Look back at the year and build next year's calendar | Year-two calendar and goals |

#### 6.1 How Information Flows

Information flows in one consistent line so nothing gets lost between the wards and the stake presidency:

1. Ward reps track and report attendance, contacts, and follow-up at the ward level.
2. Leads pull that ward data together with their own program data and bring it to the Executive Council meeting.
3. The Secretary combines all the leads' reports into one dashboard and puts together the monthly report.
4. The Co-Chairs review it, add context and any requests, and send it to the Stake Presidency.

#### 6.2 Monthly Report Template

The Secretary drafts this every month for the Co-Chairs to pass along to the stake presidency. Keep it to one page, following the Gathering Plan's measurement framework (Section 10):

- **Attendance & Participation** — activities, athletics, and enrichment turnout compared to last month and to the roster.
- **Outreach & Rescue** — percent of roster personally contacted; less-active visits; new move-ins welcomed on schedule.
- **Covenant Progression** — institute enrollment, temple recommend holders, reactivations, baptisms, and callings accepted.
- **What's Coming** — the next 60 days on the calendar and the support that's needed.
- **Risks, Needs & Decisions** — anything that needs stake presidency input or approval.

### 7. Decision Guide: What Needs Stake Presidency Approval

| Decision | Council's Role | Stake Presidency's Role |
|---|---|---|
| Program content, calendar, and event design | Decides | Kept informed |
| Assignments and duty rotation within the Council | Decides | Kept informed |
| A new signature event or spending beyond budget | Recommends | Approves |
| Extending or releasing callings/assignments | Recommends | Approves and sets apart |
| Building-use or priesthood scheduling conflicts | Coordinates | Resolves conflicts |
| Sensitive personal or pastoral situations | Flags right away | Handles with the bishops |

### 8. How the Council Works Together

- Every meeting opens and closes with prayer, and starts with a short spiritual thought tied to one of the four priorities.
- Conversations about specific people (rescue efforts, sensitive needs) stay within the Council and are shared with bishops only when they need to know.
- Decisions are made by consensus among the Co-Chairs and the relevant Leads. If something can't be resolved, it goes to the stake presidency within a week rather than sitting unsettled.
- Leads are expected at Executive Council meetings. If a Lead can't make it, a substitute (a deputy or Ward Rep) comes in their place.
- Every Council member holds to the same "on-ramp, not destination" standard the Gathering Plan sets for activities — the Council tries to model belonging and purpose in its own meetings, the same way it wants to create that for YSAs.

### 9. Onboarding, Training, and Handoffs

- New Council members get a starter packet: this charter, the Gathering Plan, the current dashboard, and a handoff document from whoever had the role before them.
- New Leads shadow their predecessor for one full meeting cycle before taking over fully, whenever the timing allows for overlap.
- Each Lead keeps a living handoff document (contacts, vendor/venue info, open items, lessons learned) updated all along — not just at turnover.
- Ward Reps are trained by their Lead counterpart within two weeks of starting, covering the invitation chain, follow-up expectations, and how to report data.

### 10. First 90 Days: Getting Started

| Timeframe | Milestones |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Co-chairs confirm the charter with the stake presidency, clean up the YSA roster, and identify lead candidates. |
| Weeks 2–4 | Leads and Secretary called/assigned and set apart; ward bishoprics asked to identify two ward reps each (the EQ & RS counselors who have been assigned). |
| Weeks 4–6 | First Executive Council meeting; reporting templates and dashboard set up; belonging survey launched. |
| Weeks 6–8 | First Full Council meeting; ward reps trained; "Know Them by Name" assignments handed out; 90-day calendar published. |
| Weeks 8–12 | First monthly report delivered to the stake presidency; first quarterly review scheduled; first stake activity, league season, and enrichment night run under the new setup. |

This timeline lines up with Phase 1 and the start of Phase 2 of the Gathering Plan's implementation timeline (Section 13), so the Council and the plan move forward together.

### 11. Summary

This charter establishes a Council for the Boise YSA Stake, fully run by Young Single Adults and co-led by the YSA High Councilor and the Stake YSA Relief Society President. It has thirteen executive seats, each responsible for one clear piece of the Gathering Plan, plus a network of ward reps who carry the plan into every ward. A steady meeting rhythm and a single reporting line keep the stake presidency informed and able to guide through vision and approval — without managing the day-to-day — while the Council has real ownership, clear accountability, and a direct connection back to the plan's four priorities.
