# Stake Education & Enrichment Committee — Detailed Charter and Meeting Plan

*Boise Idaho YSA Stake — Stake Education and Enrichment Committee — Charter and Meeting Plan*

*Companion document to the Boise Idaho YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan and the Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan.*

### 1. Purpose of This Document

The Boise YSA Stake Comprehensive Gathering Plan outlines the vision for reaching and gathering Young Single Adults. The Stake YSA Council Charter and Meeting Plan establishes the twelve-seat Executive Committee that delivers on that vision, including the two Education & Enrichment Co-Lead seats described in Sections 4.8 and 4.9 of that charter. This document brings those two seats and Part 4 of the Gathering Plan ("Education and Enrichment Curriculum") into action by creating a standing committee with its own structure, staffing, meeting rhythm, and accountability for day-to-day work.

The Gathering Plan outlines what the four curriculum threads and delivery formats must accomplish, and the YSA Council Charter identifies who owns Education & Enrichment at the Executive Committee level. This document explains how that ownership plays out week to week: how many coordinators and facilitators we need, how the work is divided among them, how married couple advisors help without leading, and how the Committee keeps the Stake YSA Executive Council in the loop through the Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs.

This document is meant to be adopted by the Stake YSA Executive Co-Chairs and used directly by the Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs as their working charter and onboarding guide for every coordinator, facilitator, mentor, and married-couple advisor on the Committee.

### 2. Relationship to the Gathering Plan and the YSA Council

The YSA Council Charter draws a clear line between the YSA Council, which owns outcomes across all four priorities, and the specialist committees under it, which own the execution of one piece of the plan. This charter keeps that same line for Education & Enrichment:

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| Stake YSA Council (Executive Co-Chairs) | Extends or recommends the Education & Enrichment Co-Chair assignments and signs off on changes to Committee makeup. Sets the yearly budget for enrichment nights, workshops, and materials, and approves any spending outside that budget. Gets the Committee's monthly report and gives direction, counsel, and correction. Resolves building-use conflicts and other issues the Committee can't settle on its own. |
| Education & Enrichment Committee | Fully owns delivering Priority 2 ("Teach Divine Identity, Purpose, and Potential") and the enrichment-related parts of Priorities 3 and 4, through the Monthly Stake Enrichment Night, workshops, discussion groups, and mentorship pairings. Builds and runs the curriculum calendar across the four threads, recruits and trains facilitators and mentors, and tracks attendance, facilitator counts, and covenant-progression indicators. Works with the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead so institute topics and stake enrichment topics reinforce each other. Gathers members' interests, talents, and needs and keeps a stake-wide resource list of teachers, presenters, and subject-matter experts, so every offering responds to real member needs. Reports progress, risks, and needs to the Stake YSA Council on a set schedule (Section 6). |

**What the Committee can decide and run on its own, without asking first:**

- Content and scheduling for enrichment nights, workshops, discussion groups, and mentorship pairings within the four curriculum threads.
- Recruiting, training, and assigning facilitators, presenters, and mentors.
- Forming, rotating, or changing the format of small groups to help people feel like they belong.
- Gathering member needs from ward and stake leaders, keeping the stake resource list, and planning needs-driven workshops and firesides within the approved budget and curriculum calendar.

**What needs to go to the Stake YSA Executive Co-Chairs before moving forward:**

- Any new coordinator calling, release, or change to who's on the Committee.
- A new paid presenter, honorarium, or any spending beyond the approved enrichment budget.
- The scope and wording of any stake-wide member interest survey before it is distributed.
- Building scheduling conflicts with priesthood or other stake and ward activities.
- Sensitive situations that come up through mentorship or small groups — mental health crises, worthiness concerns, safety issues — these go straight to the bishops or stake presidency, not handled inside the Committee.

### 3. Committee Structure and Composition

The Committee works in two tiers, matching the two-tier design used across the YSA Council: a Committee Core that owns outcomes and reports to the Executive Co-Chairs, and a Facilitator & Mentor Network that carries the curriculum into every small group, workshop, and mentoring relationship.

Committee Core total: 10 members.

#### 3.2 Tier 2 — Facilitator and Mentor Network

In line with the Gathering Plan's delivery formats, the Committee recruits small-group facilitators for the Monthly Enrichment Night, presenters for the Workshop & Seminar Series, hosts for Discussion Groups, and mentors for the Mentorship Pairing program every curriculum cycle. Network members aren't standing Committee members; they're trained and assigned by the relevant Coordinator, show up for the sessions they're assigned to, and report attendance and participation to that Coordinator after every session. When recruiting, Coordinators start with the stake resource list kept by the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator.

#### 3.3 Married Couple Advisors

Per A Guide for Married Couples Serving in the Boise YSA Stake, married couples called to this committee serve as advisors, not leaders. They sit with the Co-Chairs during Committee planning, are available as mentors in the Mentorship Pairing pool, and may be invited by the Workshop & Seminar Series Coordinator to speak on marriage prep, parenting, or career and financial topics based on their own experience. With their permission, their skills and teaching interests are added to the stake resource list. Young single adults plan, run, and teach every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group; advisor couples support and attend.

#### 3.4 Suggested Total Committee Size

| Layer | Composition | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Committee (Tier 1) | Co-Chairs and eight coordinators, all Young Single Adults | 10 members |
| Standing Advisor Couples | Married couples serving per the Guide for Married Couples; non-voting | 2–3 couples |
| Facilitator & Mentor Network (Tier 2) | Small-group facilitators, workshop presenters, discussion-group hosts, and mentors, recruited each curriculum cycle | 15–25, seasonal |

Keep the Committee fixed at ten; that keeps accountability clear and undivided for each delivery format in Part 4 of the Gathering Plan and for the needs-and-resources work that feeds them, while letting the Facilitator & Mentor Network grow and shrink with the season and curriculum cycle.

### 4. Roles, Responsibilities, and Assignments

Each Committee seat below is written as a standing assignment description, matching the format used in Section 4 of the YSA Council Charter. Use these descriptions as-is when calling or briefing Committee members.

#### 4.1 Education & Enrichment Committee Co-Chair (Sister)

Reports to: YSA Council Co-Chairs (jointly with Priesthood Co-Chair)

- Co-leads the Education & Enrichment Committee; co-chairs every Committee Core meeting with the Priesthood Executive Co-Chair.
- Runs the Monthly Stake Enrichment Night and rotates through the four curriculum threads over the year, together with the Priesthood Co-Chair.
- Champions sister-specific facilitator and mentor recruiting, and makes sure small groups are set up so no one sits alone.
- Represents the Committee at the monthly YSA Council meeting alongside the Priesthood Co-Chair.
- Makes sure every enrichment night ends with a specific invitation to take a covenant step.
- Uses the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's needs summary when setting each enrichment night's emphasis and designing small groups.

Success metric: Monthly enrichment attendance and trend; facilitators trained per cycle.

#### 4.2 Education & Enrichment Committee Co-Chair (Priesthood)

Reports to: YSA Council Co-Chairs (jointly with Sister Co-Chair)

- Co-leads the Education & Enrichment Committee; carries the Committee's reporting relationship with the YSA Council Executive Co-Chairs.
- Coordinates the Workshop/Seminar series and keeps it aligned with the Institute, drawing on stake members' expertise.
- Reviews Committee coordinator changes and recommends them to the YSA Council Co-Chairs.
- Makes sure Christ-centered framing and doctrine-first teaching standards are upheld across every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group.
- Delivers the monthly enrichment stewardship report to the YSA Council Co-Chairs, prepared by the Secretary.
- Serves as the assigned high councilor for the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator; reviews member-survey content and needs-based topic recommendations for Christ-centered framing and carries any that need approval to the Executive Co-Chairs.

Success metric: On-time monthly reports; teaching-standards compliance across all sessions.

#### 4.3 Monthly Enrichment Night Producer

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs

- Books the teaching slot and speaker for each month's enrichment night, matching the current curriculum thread.
- Runs the standard format: brief worship (hymn, prayer), 35–40 minutes of teaching, small-group discussion, and a personal commitment invitation.
- Coordinates room setup and logistics with the building scheduler and the Communications & Calendar Lead.
- Makes sure the night is on the master calendar at least 60 days ahead of time.
- Runs a quick post-event debrief with the Small-Group Facilitator Coordinator and reports attendance to the Secretary.
- Draws on the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's topic recommendations and resource list when booking speakers, and shares commitment-invitation patterns that point to new needs.

Success metric: On-time, quality-bar enrichment night delivered monthly; commitment-invitation completion noted.

#### 4.4 Small-Group Facilitator Coordinator

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs

- Recruits and trains 6–10-person cross-ward small-group facilitators for every enrichment night.
- Briefs facilitators on the teaching standards in Section 6 of Part 4 of the Gathering Plan: doctrine first, Christ-centered, interactive, safe space, confidentiality, and referral pathways.
- Assigns facilitators to groups each cycle, and makes sure no facilitator stays with the same group long enough to form a clique.
- Runs a short debrief after each enrichment night and passes facilitator counts and notes to the Secretary.
- Recruits potential facilitators from the stake resource list, and passes recurring debrief themes (without names) to the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator for the needs summary.

Success metric: Facilitators trained per cycle; small-group belonging feedback.

#### 4.5 Workshop & Seminar Series Coordinator

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs

- Builds the quarterly workshop calendar: career development, financial literacy, mental and emotional wellness, scripture study and prayer methods, marriage prep and healthy dating, and parenting basics.
- Recruits presenters from among stake members with relevant expertise, starting with the stake resource list, including married-couple advisors where appropriate.
- Coordinates logistics and makes sure every workshop is framed around divine identity, purpose, or potential rather than feeling like a stand-alone class.
- Reports attendance and presenter-recruiting status to the Secretary each month.
- Builds the quarterly calendar from the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's needs summary, meets with that Coordinator in the monthly Needs & Resources Huddle (Section 6), and takes on pilot topics that prove useful as recurring workshops.

Success metric: Workshops delivered per quarter; attendance and presenter pipeline health.

#### 4.6 Discussion Group Coordinator

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs

- Forms small, recurring 4–8-person discussion groups studying Come, Follow Me, recent general conference talks, or approved Church resources.
- Recruits and briefs group hosts on confidentiality and the fundamentals of leading a discussion.
- Tracks group health, attendance, retention, and whether groups are building real depth and connection, not just showing up.
- Reports the number of active groups and the participation trend to the Secretary.
- Uses member-interest survey results from the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator to choose study topics and to invite new participants and hosts.

Success metric: Number of active discussion groups; average attendance and retention.

#### 4.7 Mentorship Pairing Coordinator

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs

- Recruits mature stake members, including couples and individuals, such as married couple advisors, to serve as mentors for life, career, and spiritual guidance.
- Pairs YSAs with mentors, paying particular attention to single YSAs who don't have family nearby.
- Sets up a monthly mentor-meeting rhythm built around the four curriculum threads.
- Tracks how the pairings are going and reports active-pairing counts and meeting completion to the Secretary.
- Draws on the professional skills and life experience recorded on the stake resource list to build the mentor pool, and receives career, education, and life-transition needs flagged by the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator.

Success metric: Number of active mentor pairings; monthly meeting completion rate.

#### 4.8 Institute & Covenant Progression Liaison

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (works with the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead)

- Partners with institute faculty and the Gathering Place & Covenant Progression Lead to boost enrollment and clear away barriers like rides and scheduling.
- Lines up institute course topics with the stake's four curriculum threads wherever possible.
- Sends institute enrollment numbers relevant to Education & Enrichment programming to the Secretary each month.
- Shares institute course offerings with the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator and uses needs patterns to recommend courses that complement, rather than duplicate, stake workshops and firesides.

Success metric: Institute enrollment rate among tracked YSAs; topic alignment with the annual curriculum arc.

#### 4.9 Learning & Enrichment Coordinator

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (a stake calling serving under the direction of the stake presidency and the assigned high councilor, the Priesthood Co-Chair)

This seat is the Committee's needs-and-resources hub. It is more than event planning. It is a way of ministering: gathering members' interests, talents, and needs and connecting members with the people and resources that can bless them. Every offering it supports should help members feel seen, strengthen faith, and support covenant keeping.

**Needs Gathering & Resource Coordination**

- Identifies member interests, talents, professional skills, and unmet needs through a short member interest survey, interviews, and leader input, with a focus on practical needs, spiritual interests, and life-transition challenges.
- Gathers current needs each planning cycle from bishoprics, Relief Society and Elders Quorum presidencies, ward YSA leaders, and ministering interviews, asking each leader, "What are the most common needs or gaps you see among our YSA members?" and combines the answers with survey results.
- Keeps the stake-wide resource list of willing teachers, presenters, and subject-matter experts (topic, audience, suggested presenter, cost, location, and spiritual purpose), reviews it each planning cycle, and shares it with every Coordinator.
- Matches needs with available resources so offerings are useful, simple, and reasonably cost-effective.

**Workshop & Fireside Planning**

- Recommends 3 to 5 topics each planning cycle that fit stake priorities and the current curriculum thread, each paired with a Christ-centered framing so practical help points members toward deeper faith and covenant living.
- Plans and coordinates stake firesides and needs-driven practical classes, starting with one pilot (for example, a "Faith and Future" workshop with a short devotional, a practical class, and a testimony-bearing close), and helps with promotion and follow-up.
- Hands pilot topics that prove useful to the Workshop & Seminar Series Coordinator for the quarterly calendar, so the two seats never run competing events.
- Gathers feedback after each event and reports needs patterns, attendance, feedback, and recommended future topics to the Secretary and the Co-Chairs.

Success metric: Members surveyed or interviewed; top recurring needs identified; resources added to the list; workshops and firesides held and their attendance; share of Committee topics traced to identified needs.

#### 4.10 Committee Secretary / Metrics Recorder

Reports to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs

- Takes minutes and tracks action items for every Committee Core meeting.
- Pulls together the monthly attendance, facilitator, workshop, discussion-group, mentorship, and needs-gathering dashboard from data each Coordinator provides.
- Drafts the monthly enrichment stewardship report for the Co-Chairs to deliver to the Executive Co-Chairs (Section 6.2).
- Keeps track of the Committee roster, term dates, and any upcoming turnover or succession needs.

Success metric: Reports delivered on schedule; dashboard accuracy and completeness.

#### 4.11 Married Couple Advisors

Advisory relationship to: Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs (not a supervisory reporting line)

- Sit with the Committee Core in planning meetings as counsel, not as chairs; share experience when asked or when the Spirit prompts, never as a directive to follow.
- Serve in the Mentorship Pairing pool as mentors, and may be invited to speak in the Workshop & Seminar Series on marriage, parenting, or career and financial topics; share their skills for the stake resource list and suggest topics when the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator asks.
- Attend enrichment nights, workshops, and discussion groups as guests and friends, there to be present, not to run the program; young adult facilitators greet, lead, and teach.
- Coach and encourage Coordinators privately; if something needs correcting, do it specifically, kindly, and away from the group, per the Guide for Married Couples.

Success metric: Mentor-pairing satisfaction; Coordinators report feeling supported, not managed.

#### 4.12 Facilitator and Mentor Network (Tier 2)

Reports to: the relevant Coordinator (Small-Group Facilitator, Workshop & Seminar, Discussion Group, or Mentorship Pairing)

- Complete training on teaching standards and confidentiality within two weeks of being assigned.
- Attend every session they're assigned to, and immediately report attendance and participation notes to the Coordinator afterward.
- Bring any individual needs, concerns, or opportunities to the Coordinator rather than handling sensitive situations on one's own.

Success metric: Session coverage rate; timeliness of post-session reporting.

#### 6.1 Reporting Chain

Information and accountability flow in one consistent line, so nothing gets lost between a facilitator or mentor and the stake presidency:

1. Facilitators, presenters, discussion-group hosts, and mentors report attendance and participation notes to their Coordinator after every session.
2. Coordinators pull together their program data ahead of the Committee Core meeting, and the Learning & Enrichment Coordinator adds the needs-and-topics summary.
3. The Secretary combines all the Coordinator reports into one dashboard and drafts the monthly enrichment stewardship report.
4. Co-Chairs review it, add narrative and requests, deliver the report to the Stake YSA Council Co-Chairs, and represent Education & Enrichment at the YSA Council meeting.

#### 6.2 Monthly Enrichment Stewardship Report Template

The Secretary drafts this report every month for the Co-Chairs to deliver to the YSA Co-Chairs. Keep it to one page, following the Gathering Plan's measurement framework (Part 4, Section 8):

- Attendance & Participation — enrichment night, workshop, discussion group, and mentor-pairing attendance versus last month and versus the roster.
- Facilitator & Mentor Health — how many were trained and are active this cycle, and what's needed for the next cycle.
- Covenant Progression — the change in institute enrollment, how well commitment invitations were followed through, and self-reported understanding of divine identity, purpose, and potential.
- Member Needs & Resources — members surveyed or interviewed, top recurring needs, resources added to the stake resource list, workshops and firesides held from identified needs, and recommended future topics.
- Look-Ahead — what's on the enrichment calendar for the next 60 days and what support is needed.
- Risks, Needs & Decisions Requested — anything that needs the YSA Council Co-Chairs' approval or a decision.

### 8. Committee Operating Norms

- Every meeting opens and closes with prayer, and starts with a short spiritual reflection tied to one of the four curriculum threads.
- Young single adults plan, run, and teach every enrichment night, workshop, and discussion group; Married Couple Advisors support and attend, consistent with the Guide for Married Couples.
- Coordinators bring facilitator and mentor names, not just numbers, to every meeting; the Committee follows the Spirit to specific people, in line with the Gathering Plan's "on-ramp, not destination" standard.
- Anything about specific individuals: mentoring concerns and sensitive needs are kept confidential outside the Committee and shared with bishops only on a need-to-know basis.
- Member survey responses and the needs list are kept confidential and reported as patterns, not names; any individual need that surfaces goes to the member's bishop rather than onto the resource list.
- Decisions are made by consensus among the present Co-Chairs and Coordinators; if something can't be resolved, it goes to the YSA Council Co-Chairs within a week rather than remaining unsettled.
- Coordinators are expected to attend Committee Core meetings. If a Coordinator can't attend, a trained replacement should attend in their place.
- Married Couple Advisors maintain the adult-to-adult boundaries outlined in the Guide for Married Couples: they don't hover over activities as if supervising youth, they correct privately and praise publicly, and they exercise good judgment in one-on-one mentoring settings.

### 9. Onboarding, Training, and Succession

- New Committee members get an onboarding packet: this charter, Part 4 of the Gathering Plan, the YSA Council Charter, the current dashboard, and (for advisor couples) the Guide for Married Couples.
- New Coordinators shadow their predecessor for one full meeting cycle before taking over fully, whenever the term overlap allows for it.
- Each Coordinator keeps a living handoff document (presenter and facilitator contacts, curriculum notes, open action items, lessons learned) updated all the time, not just at turnover.
- The Learning & Enrichment Coordinator's handoff also includes the current stake resource list, survey tools and results, and the topic pipeline.
- Married Couple Advisors complete the First 30 Days orientation described in the Guide for Married Couples and meet with the high councilor who supports the assignment.
- Facilitators, presenters, and mentors receive training on teaching standards and confidentiality within two weeks of being assigned.

### 11. Summary

This charter establishes a ten-member Education & Enrichment Committee Core for the Boise YSA Stake, fully owned and run by Young Single Adults and co-led by the Education & Enrichment Co-Chairs named in the YSA Council Charter. It outlines eight coordinator seats, each with clear, undivided accountability for one delivery format or support function in Part 4 of the Gathering Plan, including a Learning & Enrichment Coordinator who grounds every offering in members' real interests, talents, and needs; a Facilitator & Mentor Network that carries the four curriculum threads into every small group, workshop, and mentoring relationship; and a defined place for married couple advisors who train, coach, and support without leading. A steady meeting rhythm and a single reporting chain keep the Stake YSA Council informed and able to steer the vision and grant approvals. At the same time, the Committee has real ownership, clear accountability, and a direct line back to the plan's four priorities.
